Seven days is enough for the four Andalusian cities that matter most (**Seville, Córdoba, Granada and Málaga**) plus a trip to **Ronda**, with no car and no silly early starts. The trick is the order: west to east, sleeping in each city rather than doing out-and-back day trips, with the two critical tickets bought before you leave home.

## The route at a glance

| Day | City | Plan | Overnight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Seville | Alcázar, Cathedral, Santa Cruz, Plaza de España | Seville |
| 2 | Seville | Triana, the river and flamenco | Seville |
| 3 | Córdoba | Morning train, Jewish Quarter, patios, Roman bridge | Córdoba |
| 4 | Córdoba → Granada | Mezquita first thing, train to Granada, Albaicín | Granada |
| 5 | Granada | Alhambra and a Sacromonte cave show | Granada |
| 6 | Granada → Málaga | Avant to Málaga, Alcazaba and the Malagueta | Málaga |
| 7 | Ronda | Day trip, back to Málaga | Málaga |

## Getting between cities

| Leg | Train | Indicative fare | Car |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seville → Córdoba | AVE or Avant, about 45 min, 25+ departures a day | from about €12 booked ahead; €30-40 last minute | 1 h 30 |
| Córdoba → Granada | Direct, 1 h 30, few departures a day | from about €23 | about 2 h |
| Granada → Málaga | Direct Avant, 1 h 18 | from about €18 | 1 h 30 |
| Málaga → Ronda | No direct train; bus 1 h 45 to 2 h | from about €10-12 | 1 h 45 |

Book trains on Renfe's website as soon as your dates are fixed: cheap fares disappear as trains fill, and Córdoba-Granada has few services, so plan day 4 around the timetable. Between Granada and Málaga there is also an **Alsa coach** (1 h 45, about €13, 20+ a day) if the train is full. The stations in Córdoba, Granada and Málaga are 15-25 minutes' walk from the centre.

## What to book, and how far ahead

| What | Official price | Book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alhambra (timed Nasrid Palaces) | €22.27 | 3-4 weeks; up to 3 months in high season |

## Days 1 and 2: Seville

**Day 1, morning.** The **Real Alcázar** at 9:30, the first slot (open 9:30-17:00 October to March and until 19:00 April to September): 2 h 30 including the gardens. Opposite, the **Cathedral and Giralda** (1 h 30), open Monday to Saturday 10:45-19:00 and Sundays from 14:30. If you'd like both explained without queuing, with a guide just for your group:

**Lunch** in El Arenal, five minutes away, not on the terraces facing the Cathedral. **Afternoon:** the Santa Cruz quarter and then, 20 minutes on foot through the Murillo gardens, **Plaza de España** as the sun drops. **Evening:** an intimate flamenco show at Casa de la Memoria (one hour, from €24).

[actividad: casa-de-la-memoria-flamenco-show-intimo-sevilla]

**Day 2.** Cross the Isabel II bridge into **Triana**: the market, Calle Betis and tapas on Calle Pureza. In the afternoon, Casa de Pilatos (€12) or the Fine Arts Museum (free for EU citizens), and sunset by the river. The hour-by-hour version is in [Seville in 2 days](/en/guides/sevilla/seville-in-2-days).

**Where to stay in Seville:** **El Arenal** (by the Cathedral, quieter and slightly cheaper), **Santa Cruz** (picture-postcard, more touristy) or **Triana** (more local, 15 minutes from the centre). Staying by Santa Justa station saves a walk to the train but you lose the atmosphere.

## Day 3: Córdoba

**Morning.** Train at around 9:00, in Córdoba by 9:45; 20-25 minutes' walk to the old town. Start with the **Alcázar de los Reyes Cristianos** (€7; gardens only for now while the rest is restored) and wander the **Jewish Quarter**: Calleja de las Flores, the Synagogue and Plaza del Potro.

**Lunch:** salmorejo and flamenquín in a tavern in the Judería or San Basilio, away from the streets right around the Mezquita.

**Afternoon:** the patios of the **Palacio de Viana** (€8.50 patios only) and, at sunset, the **Roman bridge** with the floodlit Mezquita behind. If you'd rather see the patios with someone who knows the owners, the [private patios tour of the Axerquía](/en/cities/cordoba/activities/cordoba-patios-private-tour-axerquia) includes three courtyards.

**Where to stay in Córdoba:** the **Judería** or **San Basilio**, five minutes from the Mezquita. Sleeping here is what makes day 4's trick possible.

## Day 4: the Mezquita and the train to Granada

**8:30 — Mosque-Cathedral.** Monday to Saturday from 8:30 to 9:30 entry is **free** for individual visitors, with no groups: the best hour of the day, at no cost. For the full visit with commentary, go at 10:00 with a €15 ticket or the [skip-the-line guided tour](/en/cities/cordoba/activities/mosque-cathedral-skip-the-line-guided-tour) (from €32); privately, the [Mezquita and Jewish Quarter tour](/en/cities/cordoba/activities/cordoba-mosque-jewish-quarter-private-tour) (from €260 per group).

**Midday:** train to Granada (1 h 30). **Afternoon:** drop your bags, walk the **Carrera del Darro** and head up into the **Albaicín** on the C32 minibus from Plaza Nueva (€1.60) or on foot via Cuesta del Chapiz (25 minutes). Sunset at the **Mirador de San Nicolás**, then free tapas with every drink on Calle Navas.

## Day 5: the Alhambra

**8:30 — Alhambra.** The whole day revolves around your Nasrid Palaces time: there are only **30 minutes' grace**, and it's a 30-45 minute walk from the entrance to the palaces. Alcazaba, Nasrid Palaces and Generalife take about 4 hours. If tickets are gone, the [guided tour with entry included](/en/cities/granada/activities/alhambra-generalife-guided-tour) (from €49) has its own allocation; privately, the [skip-the-line tour just for you](/en/cities/granada/activities/alhambra-nasrid-palaces-private-tour-skip-the-line) (from €278 per group).

**Afternoon:** the Royal Chapel (€7), with the tombs of Ferdinand and Isabella, and the Cathedral. **Evening:** a zambra in a Sacromonte cave, €22-30 with a drink. The detail is in [Granada in 1 day](/en/guides/granada/granada-in-1-day).

**Where to stay in Granada:** the **centre between Plaza Nueva and Gran Vía** (five minutes from everything), the **Realejo** (more local) or the **lower Albaicín** (Alhambra views, hills and higher prices). Avoid the station area and Camino de Ronda.

## Day 6: Granada → Málaga

A slow morning in Granada (the Realejo or the Cartuja monastery, €7) and the **direct Avant to Málaga** after lunch: 1 h 18. **Afternoon:** the **Alcazaba and Gibralfaro** (€10 combined), the Roman Theatre from outside and the Cathedral (€10 with audio guide). **Evening:** sardine espetos on the Malagueta or in Pedregalejo.

**Where to stay in Málaga:** the **historic centre** or **Soho**, 15 minutes' walk from María Zambrano station. The Malagueta if you want the beach on your doorstep.

## Day 7: Ronda

Bus from Málaga bus station (1 h 45 to 2 h, about eight a day; fewer direct services at weekends) or a tour. The Puente Nuevo over the gorge, the bullring, the old town and the walk down to the viewpoints; back to Málaga in the evening. All the options are in [Ronda from Málaga](/en/guides/malaga/ronda-from-malaga):

- **Group tour:** [Ronda and Setenil de las Bodegas](/en/cities/malaga/activities/ronda-setenil-day-trip-from-malaga), from €34.
- **Private with pick-up:** [private day trip to Ronda](/en/cities/malaga/activities/ronda-private-day-trip-from-malaga), from €580 per group; for 4 people that's about €180 each.

Málaga airport is 12 minutes from the centre on the C-1 commuter train (€1.80), so you can fly out on the evening of day 7 or the morning of day 8.

**With a car:** pick one up in Granada on day 6, drive to Ronda (about 2 hours), stay the night (it's another town once the day-trippers leave) and on day 7 drive down to Málaga through the hills (1 h 45) to drop it at the airport.

## Indicative budget per person

| Item | Per person, 7 days |
| --- | --- |
| Trains and buses | €75-110 |
| Tickets (Alcázar, Cathedral, Mezquita, Viana, Alhambra, Royal Chapel, Alcazaba) | €85-100 |
| Flamenco and cave show | €45-60 |
| Food (tapas and the odd restaurant) | €250-350 |
| Mid-range accommodation, sharing a double | €50-90 a night (€350-630) |
| **Total** | **about €800-1,250** |

Granada is the cheap city for food, as tapas come free with drinks. Seville and Málaga are the priciest nights.

## With children, or in summer

- **With kids:** swap Córdoba's patios for the Alcázar gardens, and in Granada take the minibus up to the Alhambra. Under-12s go free at the Alhambra but **need a named ticket**. More ideas in [Seville with kids](/en/guides/sevilla/seville-with-kids) and [Granada with kids](/en/guides/granada/granada-with-kids).
- **June to September:** outdoors until 13:00, indoors or a siesta from 14:00 to 18:00, back out at 19:00. In Seville and Córdoba this is non-negotiable.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying Alhambra tickets once you're in Andalusia:** in high season there are none.
2. **Doing Córdoba as a day trip from Granada:** 4 hours on the road in a day; from Seville it's 45 minutes.
3. **Hiring a car for the whole route:** you'll pay €25-30 a day to park it in cities and use it for two days.
4. **Planning trains without checking frequencies** on Córdoba-Granada.
5. **Trying to squeeze Cádiz, Ronda and the white villages into the same week.** If they appeal more, drop Málaga and sleep in Ronda.

## In a nutshell

Four cities linked by trains of under 90 minutes, two tickets booked well ahead and a night in each place that deserves one: that's how a week in Andalusia is enjoyed rather than endured. With a spare day, add Cádiz and Jerez from Seville; see [day trips from Seville](/en/guides/sevilla/day-trips-from-seville).
