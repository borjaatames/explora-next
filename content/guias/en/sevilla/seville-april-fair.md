## The 2027 calendar

| Day | Date | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | 12 April | **Pescaíto** night: fried fish dinners in the casetas and, at midnight, the **lighting-up** of the gateway and thousands of paper lanterns |
| Tuesday to Saturday | 13 – 17 April | Fair by day (horses and carriages) and by night (dancing and sevillanas until the small hours) |
| Sunday | 18 April | Last day; at midnight, **fireworks** over the river and closing |

The Fair starts two weeks after Easter Sunday, which falls on 28 March in 2027. The city confirms the dates when it unveils the poster; we'll update them then.

## How to get into a caseta without knowing anyone

The fairground, the Real de la Feria in the Los Remedios district, has more than a thousand casetas, and almost all belong to families, clubs, companies or associations: without an invitation you can't go in. The ones **open to everyone**:

- **District casetas:** free entry, sevillanas and rumba music, open until around 3:00. Your best option if you come on your own.
- **The municipal caseta (Caseta de Sevilla):** with a terrace and an indoor area, live music from 16:00 to 3:00 and tables bookable through the tourist office.
- **With locals:** if you know someone from Seville, ask them to take you; it's very common to be invited into friends' casetas.

In the casetas you pay for what you have, and some work with drink vouchers: bring cash.

## Fair by day and fair by night

- **By day (14:00 to 19:00):** the most photogenic time. Horses and carriages parade and the casetas serve food. A horse carriage costs about €95 an hour (2025 rate).
- **By night (from 20:00):** once the horses leave and the sand is cleaned, the dancing and sevillanas start and go on until the small hours.

If you can only go one day, pick a **Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon**: plenty of atmosphere and slightly smaller crowds than at the weekend.

## What to drink, eat and wear

- **Drinks:** fino and manzanilla sherry, and above all **rebujito**, manzanilla with lemon-lime soda and lots of ice, ordered by the jug.
- **Food:** fried fish, ham, Spanish omelette and small sandwiches.
- **Clothes:** you don't have to dress as a flamenco dancer. Women often wear flamenco dresses and men a suit and tie. If you don't have one, dress smart and wear **comfortable shoes**: the ground is sand and you'll walk a lot.

## Getting there

- **Metro line 1:** Plaza de Cuba, Parque de los Príncipes and Blas Infante stations, running non-stop during the Fair.
- **Tussam shuttle buses:** from Prado de San Sebastián, €1.60 a ride (2025 fare).
- **Car:** the area around the fairground is closed to traffic and parking is impossible. Taxis and ride-hailing have special fares.

## Beyond the casetas

- **Calle del Infierno:** the funfair next to the fairground, with a Ferris wheel, roller coasters and stalls. A safe bet with kids.
- **Bullfights at the Maestranza:** the Fair is the bullring's big season. The cheapest seats, in the sun, were around €31 in 2025. If you'd rather not watch a bullfight, the guided tour of the bullring and its museum is the alternative.
- **Guided tour of the Fair:** the city runs one-hour tours (€16 in 2025) from the kiosk by the gateway.

## Common mistakes

- **Going on Saturday night with no plan:** it's the busiest day and the public casetas fill up. Arrive before 22:00.
- **Counting on finding a hotel in March:** the Fair is booked months ahead.
- **Wearing heels or new shoes:** the sand ruins them in an afternoon.
- **Taking photos inside a private caseta without asking:** it's a family party, not a stage set.
