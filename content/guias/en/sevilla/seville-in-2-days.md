## Day 1: Alcázar, Cathedral and Plaza de España

**9:30 — Real Alcázar.** The first slot, before the groups. Open 9:30 to 17:00 from October to March and until 19:00 from April to September. The Patio de las Doncellas, the Hall of the Ambassadors, the Baths of Doña María de Padilla and **all of the gardens**, which half of visitors skip: 2 h 30. The Cuarto Real Alto (€5.50 extra) is optional; with only two days, you can do without it. More detail in [how to visit the Alcázar](/en/guides/sevilla/alcazar-of-seville-how-to-visit).

[actividad: real-alcazar-sevilla-entrada]

**12:30 — Cathedral and Giralda**, across the square (2 minutes). Open Monday to Saturday 10:45 to 19:00 and Sundays 14:30 to 19:00. Columbus's tomb, the main altarpiece and the climb up the Giralda by ramps, no stairs: 1 h 30. The ticket also covers the Church of El Salvador, which you can leave for day 2. If you'd rather not worry about times or queues, there are private tours with an official guide that cover both monuments in 3 hours with tickets included.

**14:30 — Lunch in El Arenal**, 5 minutes away: Calle Adriano, Arfe and García de Vinuesa. Avoid the terraces on Plaza Virgen de los Reyes.

**16:30 — Santa Cruz quarter.** Callejón del Agua, Plaza de Doña Elvira and Plaza de Santa Cruz. An hour of aimless wandering.

**18:00 — Plaza de España and María Luisa Park**, 20 minutes on foot through the Murillo gardens. Free; a rowing boat on the canal costs about €6. Go in the low evening light, not at midday.

**21:00 — Flamenco.** For a first show, **Casa de la Memoria**: one hour, a small room and top-class artists, from €24. If you're unsure which venue to pick, see [which tablao to choose in Seville](/en/guides/sevilla/flamenco-seville-which-tablao-to-choose). Afterwards, tapas around the Alfalfa.

## Day 2: Triana and Seville without the queues

**10:00 — Triana.** From the Torre del Oro, 10 minutes along the river to the **Isabel II bridge**. On the other side:

- The **Triana Market**, built over the Castillo de San Jorge, seat of the Inquisition, with a small free interpretation centre.
- **Calle Betis**, facing the old town.
- The ceramics shops on Calle Alfarería and the **Santa Ana** church.

**13:30 — Tapas in Triana.** Calle Pureza, Pagés del Corro and San Jacinto. If you want a sure thing, there are private tapas tours of the taverns the locals go to.

**16:30 — A palace or a museum.** Head back across the river (15-20 minutes). **Casa de Pilatos** (€12, 1 hour), with patios reminiscent of the Alcázar and a fraction of the crowds, or the **Fine Arts Museum** (free for EU citizens), with Murillo and Zurbarán.

**19:00 — Sunset.** Two options, depending on taste: the **Setas** viewpoint (from €16), with 360° views and the Giralda in the background, 10 minutes from the Casa de Pilatos; or a **one-hour cruise** on the Guadalquivir from the Torre del Oro (from €16.50).

**21:30 — Dinner on the Alameda de Hércules**, 10 minutes from the Setas: the neighbourhood with the most local atmosphere at night.

## What to book for a Seville weekend itinerary, and what it costs

| What | Official price | How far ahead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real Alcázar | €15.50 (reduced €8) | 1-2 weeks in spring and autumn; the 9:30 slot goes fast |
| Cathedral and Giralda | €13 online, €14 at the ticket office | 2-3 days |
| Flamenco | from €24 | 2-3 days at weekends |
| Setas (viewpoint) | from €16 | same day |

**Indicative budget per person, excluding accommodation: about €135-200** for the two days. The Alcázar and Cathedral add up to €28.50; Casa de Pilatos or the Setas, €12-16; flamenco, €24-45; and tapas lunches and dinners, €70-110. With a private tour (the Alcázar and Cathedral one, from €300 per group) the budget goes up, but for 3 or 4 people the difference per head narrows.

## Getting there, getting around and where to stay

- **Getting around:** the whole itinerary is on foot; you need neither metro nor bus.
- **Getting there:** from Santa Justa station, 20 minutes on foot or a short taxi ride; from the airport, the EA bus to Plaza de Armas.
- **Where to stay:** **El Arenal** or **Santa Cruz**, to walk everywhere; El Arenal is quieter and a bit cheaper. **Triana**, more local, is 15 minutes from the centre.

## Variations and common mistakes

- **If it rains:** day 1 works the same (you only lose the gardens). On day 2, swap Triana for the Casa de Pilatos, the **Archivo de Indias** (free) and the covered Triana Market.
- **With kids:** the boats at Plaza de España, María Luisa Park and the Setas viewpoint are a hit; so is the Alcázar, with its garden mazes. More in [Seville with kids](/en/guides/sevilla/seville-with-kids).
- **In summer:** Alcázar at 9:30, indoor sights at midday and stay off the streets between 14:00 and 19:00.

Mistakes worth avoiding:

1. **Leaving the Alcázar for day 2 without a ticket:** if it sells out, your trip falls apart.
2. **Plaza de España at midday:** no shade and flat light.
3. **Eating opposite the Cathedral.**
4. **Having dinner at 20:00:** in Seville people dine from 21:00.
5. **Flamenco with dinner in Santa Cruz:** better a serious tablao and dinner afterwards.

If you can add a third day, [Seville in 3 days](/en/guides/sevilla/seville-in-3-days) adds Itálica; for the whole region, [Andalusia in 7 days](/en/guides/sevilla/andalusia-7-day-itinerary).
