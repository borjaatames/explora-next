## How to choose in 30 seconds

- **Tight budget:** Teatro Flamenco Triana (€25), Casa de la Memoria (€24) or Teatro Flamenco Sevilla (€25), the three cheapest and all well rated.
- **Maximum intimacy:** Casa de la Memoria, with just two rows around the stage.
- **You want dinner:** Tablao El Arenal, the only one with a traditional dinner alongside the show.
- **You're interested in flamenco history:** the Flamenco Dance Museum, with choreography by Cristina Hoyos.
- **You want it in its home neighbourhood:** Teatro Flamenco Triana, across the Guadalquivir.

## Best flamenco show in Seville: what each venue is really like

- **Teatro Flamenco Sevilla:** the city's best seller. A proper theatre with amplified sound and lighting: comfortable, with good sightlines thanks to the tiered seats, but less intimate. It has several shows a day, so it's the easiest to fit into a busy plan. There's a single ticket type with unnumbered seats, and no drinks during the show.
- **Casa de la Memoria:** on Calle Cuna in the centre, it seats you 1-3 metres from the artists in a historic patio, with shows at 19:30 and 21:00. The cast changes every night. No drinks are served and the chairs are basic.
- **Flamenco Dance Museum:** in the Santa Cruz quarter, with six artists on stage and shows at 17:00, 19:00 and 20:45. Museum entry costs €7 extra and you can visit the same day before the show (open 11:00 to 18:00).
- **Tablao El Arenal:** next to the Maestranza bullring, with 50 years of history, it's the only venue where you sit at tables. The €45 base price includes a drink; tapas and set menus cost more.
- **Teatro Flamenco Triana:** one of the cheapest and the one that takes you across the river, to the historically Gitano neighbourhood. Plaza de Cuba metro is five minutes away, and Calle Betis and the Altozano bars are right there for afterwards.

## Where to see flamenco in Seville: show times and getting there

- **Show times:** most have two or three shows, the first around 18:00-19:00 and the last from 21:00-22:00. At Casa de la Memoria and the Dance Museum, the 19:30-20:00 shows fill first.
- **Where they are:** Casa de la Memoria and the Flamenco Dance Museum, five minutes from Plaza del Salvador; Teatro Flamenco Sevilla, also in the centre; El Arenal, by the Maestranza; Teatro Flamenco Triana, across the Isabel II bridge. All walkable from the old town, and none includes hotel pick-up.
- **What you'll see:** a troupe of 4 to 7 artists for **one hour**, with no storyline: different palos one after another, finishing with bulerías. The singing is in Spanish, but you don't need to understand it to follow.

## Tips before you book

- **Arrive 20-30 minutes early:** at most venues seats are first come, first served, and at Casa de la Memoria the difference between the first and second row is big.
- **Book early in high season:** in July-August and at weekends, Casa de la Memoria and El Arenal often sell out. All five have **free cancellation up to 24 hours ahead**.
- **With or without dinner:** only El Arenal offers it. It's decent; if you want to eat well, have tapas beforehand in El Arenal or Triana and buy just the ticket with a drink.
- **Photos and video:** never with flash. The Dance Museum and Teatro Flamenco Triana don't allow them during the performance; the others usually allow a few photos without flash. Ask when you go in.
- **Children:** allowed everywhere; from about 7-8 they manage the hour. At Casa de la Memoria, better from 10.
- **Heat:** the patios (Casa de la Memoria, Dance Museum) don't have strong air conditioning. In July and August, pick the last show or a theatre.
