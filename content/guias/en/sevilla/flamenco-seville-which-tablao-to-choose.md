Seville doesn't have a single "flamenco show" — it has five distinct venues, each with its own format, price and atmosphere. Unlike other clusters on this site (like tours to a single monument), these aren't duplicates: they're five genuinely different experiences. The question isn't "which is cheapest" but "which fits what you're looking for".

## Seville's 5 flamenco shows

| Show | Price from | Format | Atmosphere | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| [Casa de la Memoria](/en/cities/sevilla/activities/seville-casa-de-la-memoria-flamenco-show-intimate) | €24 | Andalusian courtyard, only 2 rows | Maximum intimacy, performers a metre away | Anyone who values closeness over seat comfort |

## How to choose in 30 seconds

- **Tight budget:** Teatro Flamenco Triana (€25), Casa de la Memoria (€24) or Teatro Flamenco Sevilla (€25) — the three cheapest, and all well rated.
- **Maximum intimacy:** Casa de la Memoria, with only two rows of seats around the stage.
- **Want dinner with the show:** Tablao El Arenal, the only one with a traditional dinner option alongside the performance.
- **Interested in flamenco's cultural background:** Museo del Baile Flamenco, with choreography by Cristina Hoyos and an 18th-century courtyard.
- **Want to experience it in its neighbourhood of origin:** Teatro Flamenco Triana, across the Guadalquivir in the neighbourhood where the genre was born.

## What each venue is really like

- **Teatro Flamenco Sevilla** is the most booked show in the city (18,292 reviews at 4.7). It's a proper theatre with amplified sound and lighting, so it's comfortable but less intimate. Several shows a day make it the easiest to fit into a tight schedule. There's a single ticket type with unnumbered seats, and no drinks are allowed during the show.
- **Casa de la Memoria**, on Calle Cuna in the centre, puts you 1–3 metres from the artists in a historic patio, with shows at 19:30 and 21:00. The line-up changes every night. No drinks are served, and the chairs are basic. It's the top-rated of the big shows (4.8 from 13,526 reviews).
- **Museo del Baile Flamenco** in Barrio Santa Cruz has a six-artist cast and shows at 17:00, 19:00 and 20:45. Museum entry adds €7 and can be done the same day, before the show (the museum is open 11:00–18:00). Photos and video are not allowed during the performance.
- **Tablao El Arenal**, next to the Maestranza bullring, is the only venue where you sit at tables. The €45 base price includes one drink; tapas and full-menu options cost more. It has only 22 reviews on GetYourGuide because the listing is new, not because the venue is.
- **Teatro Flamenco Triana** is the cheapest option and the one that gets you across the river. Plaza de Cuba metro is a 5-minute walk away, and Calle Betis and the Altozano tapas bars are right there for afterwards.

## What to know before booking

- **What you'll see:** a group of four to seven artists for **one hour**, with no storyline: a series of flamenco styles (palos) that ends with bulerías. The singing is in Spanish, but you don't need the language to follow it.
- **Showtimes:** most venues run two or three shows a night, the first around 18:00–19:00 and the last from 21:00–22:00. The early-evening shows at Casa de la Memoria (19:30) and the Museo del Baile (19:00) sell out first.
- **Arrive 20–30 minutes early.** In most venues, including Teatro Flamenco Sevilla, seats go in order of arrival. At Casa de la Memoria the gap between the first and second row is noticeable. Shows start on time, and after 10 minutes you may not be let in.
- **Dinner or no dinner:** only El Arenal serves food. If eating well matters more than convenience, have tapas in El Arenal or Triana beforehand and book a show-only ticket.
- **Photos:** never with flash. The Museo del Baile bans them completely; the theatres usually allow a few without flash. Ask at the door.
- **Children** are allowed everywhere; from about 7 or 8 they can sit through the hour. At Casa de la Memoria, with its small room and total silence, aim for 10 and over.
- **Heat:** the patio venues (Casa de la Memoria, Museo del Baile) get warm in July and August. In midsummer, choose the last show or one of the theatres.

## Booking and getting there

All five shows run about an hour and offer free cancellation up to 24 hours ahead. In high season (July–August and weekends), the most popular ones — especially Casa de la Memoria and Tablao El Arenal — tend to sell out ahead of time, so it's worth booking as soon as your dates are fixed. None include hotel pickup, and you don't need it: all five are walkable from the historic centre, Triana included (15–20 minutes from the Cathedral across the river).
