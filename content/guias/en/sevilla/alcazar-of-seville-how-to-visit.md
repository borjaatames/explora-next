## What to see inside the Royal Alcázar of Seville

The Alcázar is a palace in layers: Almohad, Mudéjar, Gothic and Renaissance, one on top of another. These are the parts you shouldn't miss:

- **Patio del Yeso:** 12th century, one of the few surviving Almohad civil buildings in Spain. Many people walk straight past it.
- **Façade of the Palace of Pedro I:** Arabic and Gothic inscriptions side by side, the finest example of Mudéjar art.
- **Courtyard of the Maidens (Patio de las Doncellas):** the most photographed spot, with its sunken garden.
- **Hall of Ambassadors:** under a golden dome, where Charles V married Isabella of Portugal in 1526.
- **Courtyard of the Dolls (Patio de las Muñecas):** look for the little faces hidden in the capitals.
- **Baths of Doña María de Padilla:** Gothic cisterns reflected in the water.
- **Gardens:** more than half the site, with the Grotesque Gallery and the Mercury Pond. They were the Water Gardens of Dorne in *Game of Thrones*.

The full visit takes **about 2.5-3 hours**. Allow at least 45 minutes for the gardens.

## Alcázar Seville tickets price

- **General admission:** €15.50 (2026).
- **Reduced:** €8, for over-65s, students aged 14 to 30 and Carné Joven holders.
- **Free:** under-13s, people with disabilities and people born in or living in Seville.
- **Upper Royal Quarters (Cuarto Real Alto):** €5.50 extra. These are the rooms still used by the Royal Family; visits are in small groups with 30-minute slots and **sell out before general admission**.
- **Audio guide:** €6 at the ticket office, or the official app.

Tickets are sold in 30-minute slots with limited capacity, and they can't be changed or refunded.

**Free for everyone on Mondays**, 18:00 to 19:00 from April to September and 16:00 to 17:00 from October to March. Online only, with limited places that go within minutes: don't count on it unless you've already booked.

## Alcázar Seville opening hours

- **April to September:** 9:30 to 19:00.
- **October to March:** 9:30 to 17:00.
- **Last entry:** one hour before closing.
- **Closed:** 1 and 6 January, Good Friday and 25 December.

From March to October the ticket-office queue **tops 2 hours**, so buy a few days ahead. If the official website is already sold out, our guide on [what to do if Alcázar of Seville tickets are sold out](/en/guides/sevilla/alcazar-of-seville-tickets-sold-out) goes through every option left.

## Alcázar Seville guided tour or on your own?

On your own works if you have your ticket and an audio guide. With a guide you get two things: **you skip the queue** and you understand what you're seeing, because without explanation the palace's different eras blur together.

## Getting there

The entrance is the **Lion Gate (Puerta del León)**, on Plaza del Triunfo, opposite the Cathedral. Metro **Puerta de Jerez (L1)** or the **Archivo de Indias** tram stop, both 3 minutes' walk away. There's no parking on site; the nearest car parks are on Avenida de Roma and Paseo de Colón.

## Tips for the gardens and your visit

1. **Don't turn up without a ticket in high season:** the ticket-office queue tops 2 hours and daily capacity sells out.
2. **Bring your ID or passport:** the ticket is in the buyer's name and it's checked at the door. A ticket on your phone is fine, no need to print.
3. **Avoid midday in summer:** the gardens have no shade and in July it goes above 40 °C. Go in at 9:30 or after 17:00.
4. **Leave big backpacks at the hotel:** there's no left-luggage and bags over 30 litres aren't allowed in.
5. **Don't think of it as an "Arab" palace:** it's mostly Mudéjar, Christian art with an Islamic look. Pedro I had it built in 1364, a century after the Christian conquest of Seville.
6. **Don't skip the gardens:** they're half the visit and what people remember most.
