## Seville Christmas lights and the Plaza de San Francisco light show

Seville lights around **300 streets with religious motifs**: the Christ Child, the Virgin, the ox and the mule, some on a large scale. The streets with the most atmosphere are **Tetuán, Sierpes, Salvador, Reyes Católicos and Avenida de la Constitución**.

The highlight is the **projection onto the Plateresque façade of City Hall**, on Plaza de San Francisco. In 2025 it was called "Acordes de Sevilla", lasted about 14 minutes and featured *Carmen* and the band Triana. It repeats every evening from **18:30 to 23:00**; on Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and the eve of Epiphany it ran until midnight. To see it without walking, a private electric tuk tuk covers the centre and Plaza de España in an hour.

**A December evening well spent:**

1. **17:00, Feria del Belén:** with the Cathedral and the Giralda lighting up behind.
2. **17:45, Avenida de la Constitución towards Plaza Nueva**, with the lights already on.
3. **18:30, Plaza de San Francisco:** the first light show, the quietest.
4. **19:00, Sierpes and Tetuán**, and the Fundación Cajasol nativity scene.
5. **20:00, Las Setas or La Magdalena**, for the Christmas market.
6. **21:00, flamenco or tapas in Triana.**

## Seville Christmas market, nativity scenes and convent sweets

- **Feria del Belén:** over three decades of figurines, moss and cork between the Cathedral and the Archivo de Indias. In 2025 it opened 10:30-14:00 and 17:00-21:00 on weekdays, and 11:00-15:00 and 16:30-21:30 at weekends and on holidays.
- **Nativity scene route:** about twenty nativity scenes around the centre, such as the City Hall one (in the Arquillo), the Fundación Cajasol one on Plaza de San Francisco itself, or the Círculo Mercantil one on Sierpes. Many close on 24, 25 and 31 December and 1 January.
- **Convent sweets:** every December the Real Alcázar hosts a fair selling sweets made by enclosed nuns, around the Immaculate Conception holiday (in 2023, 5 to 8 December, with 20 convents). The rest of the year you buy them at the convent hatches: the **yemas of San Leandro** are the best known.

## Zambombas, flamenco and plans with children

A **zambomba** is a party of flamenco carols with tambourines, anís bottles and the friction drum that gives it its name. In 2025 the Alcázar held free zambombas in the Salón de los Tapices on several days before Christmas, and in December there are more in Triana's clubs and bars. If you prefer a show at a set time, Casa de la Memoria, in the centre, is intimate and costs from €24. And if you like the city at night, a private legends tour at sunset (an hour and a half, €125 per group of up to four) fits in just before the light show.

**With children:**

- **Setalandia**, under Las Setas de la Encarnación, and the **ice rink at Prado de San Sebastián** (29 November to 6 January in 2025).
- **Navigalia** tells a Christmas story projected onto the Guadalquivir, at the Muelle de la Sal, for €2.
- **Naturaleza Encendida**, the light show in the Alcázar gardens, runs until March.

- **On 6 January** the Alcázar, the Cathedral and the Museum of Fine Arts are closed.
- **Real Alcázar:** October to March, 9:30 to 17:00. With December tickets in high demand, a private visit with an official guide secures your day and time.
- **Cathedral:** Monday to Saturday 10:45 to 18:00 and Sundays 14:30 to 19:00; €13 online and €14 at the ticket office. On the 24th and 31st it has special hours because of services: check the calendar on catedraldesevilla.es before you go.
- **Christmas markets:** last year, the one in the Jardines de Cristina closed on the 24th, 25th, 31st and 1st, and the craft fair under the Setas closed on the 24th and 31st and opened 11:00 to 16:00 on the 25th and the 1st.
- **Shops and bars:** on the 24th and 31st they close mid-afternoon, and few open on the 25th and the 1st. Book your dinner.

## New Year's Eve and the Three Kings parade in Seville

- **New Year's chimes:** on **Plaza Nueva**, in front of City Hall.
- **Royal Herald:** on 4 January he parades through the city announcing the Kings.
- **Ateneo Three Kings parade (5 January):** in 2026 it left the old Tobacco Factory at **16:15**, went along Menéndez Pelayo, Feria, Campana, San Pablo and Reyes Católicos, crossed to Triana and returned around 22:00, with 33 floats. To see it well without the crowds, pick a stretch outside the centre, such as Ronda de Capuchinos.

## Things to do in Seville at Christmas: tips

- **Don't go to the light show at 20:00 on a Saturday:** San Francisco fills up all the way to Sierpes. The 18:30 show or a weekday one looks just as good.
- **Don't plan the Alcázar for the 25th or the 6th:** it's closed. And on the 24th and 31st it may close early.
- **Book dinner for the 24th and 31st:** many bars close or only offer a set menu.
- **Don't drive on 5 January:** the parade closes half the centre and Triana for hours.
- **If you have a free day,** in winter Córdoba can be visited without the heat that makes it hard from April to October; the full-day trip with the Mezquita costs from €109.
