The Royal Alcázar sells tickets in **30-minute slots with limited capacity**, and from March to October, at Easter and on Spanish bank holidays, the busiest dates go early. "Sold out" on the official website doesn't mean you miss the Alcázar, but the usual tricks don't work here: there's no waiting list and no last-minute release. These are the routes left, in the order worth trying them.

## 1. The official website and the same-day box office

The official ticket site is `alcazardesevilla.infoticketing.com`, linked from `alcazarsevilla.org`. General admission is **€15.50** (reduced €8), and the **Cuarto Real Alto** (Upper Royal Rooms) is €5.50 extra. If your date shows as sold out:

- **Check every slot that day:** each half hour has its own allocation, so don't stop at the early ones.
- **Look at the option with the Cuarto Real Alto too**, which is listed separately.
- **Go to the box office early:** it's in the Patio de Banderas, opens at **9:00**, sells same-day tickets only and takes cards only. The official site says that even when online sales are sold out, it keeps **a very limited number of tickets for each slot**. On busy days they recommend arriving **at least an hour before opening**.
- **Everyone brings their own ID:** you can't buy for others at the box office, so the whole group has to queue.

What doesn't work: waiting for cancellations. Official tickets **can't be changed or refunded**, and the site states there is no waiting list and no early release.

## 2. Free Mondays

On Mondays there's a free hour: **18:00 to 19:00** from April to September and **16:00 to 17:00** from October to March. It's booked online only, on the same site, and places go very quickly. Worth a look if you're in town on a Monday, but not a plan.

## 3. A resold timed ticket or a guided tour

When the official site has nothing left, licensed resellers usually still have places because they buy their allocations ahead. You pay more than on the official site:

- **Timed ticket, no guide (from €20):** the cheapest way in if you just want to visit.
- **Guided tour of the Alcázar only (from €36):** 105 minutes with a guide, and the one we link also includes the Upper Royal Rooms.
- **Cathedral, Giralda and Alcázar (from €53):** if you haven't sorted the Cathedral yet, you do both in one morning.

Before paying, check that the listing says **"Royal Alcázar entry included"**. Some Santa Cruz walks only pass by outside.

## 4. What you can see without a ticket

If you get nothing, you can still see a fair amount from outside:

- The **Patio de Banderas**, a public square inside the walled precinct, with a view of the Giralda.
- The **Lion Gate** and the Almohad wall, from **Plaza del Triunfo**.
- The **Callejón del Agua** and the **Murillo Gardens** in the Santa Cruz quarter, which run along the Alcázar wall.

For Mudéjar architecture somewhere else, the **Casa de Pilatos** has courtyards and tilework from the same family, about 15 minutes away on foot.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Buying from sites that look like the official one.** They overcharge and don't always deliver a valid ticket. The official site is `alcazardesevilla.infoticketing.com`.
2. **Booking a tour without checking it includes entry.** You pay for a guide and stay at the gate.
3. **Arriving late for your slot.** Entry is by 30-minute slots and you need to turn up at your time.
4. **Forgetting your passport or ID.** Tickets are in the buyer's name and staff check the physical document.

## In one sentence

If the official site is sold out, queue at the Patio de Banderas box office an hour before opening or book a timed ticket or a guided tour that includes entry; everything else is in [how to visit the Royal Alcázar of Seville](/en/guides/sevilla/alcazar-of-seville-how-to-visit).
