## First, check the official website properly

The official sales website is **alcazardesevilla.infoticketing.com**, linked from alcazarsevilla.org. General admission is **€15.50** (reduced €8) and the **Upper Royal Quarters (Cuarto Real Alto)** cost €5.50 extra. If your date shows as sold out:

- **Check every slot that day:** each half hour has its own quota, so don't just look at the early ones.
- **Check the option with the Upper Royal Quarters too**, which is listed separately on the website.

What doesn't work: waiting for cancellations. Official tickets **can't be changed or refunded**, and the website states there's no waiting list and no early releases.

## Alcázar Seville same day tickets at the ticket office

The ticket office is in the **Patio de Banderas**, opens at **9:00**, only sells tickets for that day and **only takes cards**. According to the website itself, even when online sales are sold out, it keeps **a very limited number of tickets for each slot**.

- On busy days they recommend arriving **at least an hour early**.
- **Everyone with their own ID:** you can't buy for other people at the ticket office. If you're a group, you all have to queue.

## Alcázar Seville last minute tickets: resale or a tour with tickets

When the official website has nothing left, authorised resellers usually still have places because they buy their quotas in advance. You pay more than on the official site:

- **Timed ticket without a guide (from €20):** the cheapest option if you just want to get in.
- **Guided tour of the Alcázar only (from €36):** 105 minutes with a guide; ours also includes the Upper Royal Quarters.
- **Cathedral, Giralda and Alcázar (from €53):** if you haven't got the Cathedral yet, you sort both monuments in one morning.

Before paying, check the listing says **"Real Alcázar entry included"**. Some Santa Cruz walks only go past the outside.

## Alcázar Seville free Monday

On Mondays there's one free hour: **18:00 to 19:00** from April to September and **16:00 to 17:00** from October to March. It's booked online only, on the same website, and the places go very fast. If you happen to be there on a Monday, try it, but don't make it your plan.

## Alcázar Seville sold out: what you can see from outside

If you can't get anything, you can still see quite a lot without going in:

- The **Patio de Banderas**, a public square inside the walled precinct, with views of the Giralda.
- The **Lion Gate** and the Almohad wall, from **Plaza del Triunfo**.
- **Callejón del Agua** and the **Murillo Gardens**, in the Santa Cruz quarter, which run along the Alcázar wall.

And if you're after Mudéjar art elsewhere, the **Casa de Pilatos** has courtyards and tiles from the same family, about 15 minutes' walk away.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Buying from websites that imitate the official one.** They overcharge and don't always give you a valid ticket.
2. **Booking a tour without checking it includes entry.** You pay for a guide and end up at the door.
3. **Arriving late for your slot.** Entry is in 30-minute slots and you have to turn up at your time.
4. **Forgetting your ID or passport.** The ticket is in the buyer's name and they check the physical document at the entrance.
