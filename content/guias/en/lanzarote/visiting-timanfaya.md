## What you'll see at the Fire Mountains

The eruptions lasted from September 1730 to April 1736, and there was a shorter one in 1824. This is the only way to visit the inside of the park:

- **Volcano Route:** a coach tour of about **35-40 minutes** among craters, lava flows and lava fields, with a recorded commentary telling the story of the eruption. Coaches leave roughly every 20 minutes and are accessible. You don't get off during the ride.
- **Geothermal demonstrations:** at Islote de Hilario, staff pour water into a pipe sunk into the ground and it shoots out as steam, and dry brushwood catches fire on its own in a pit. They are included in the ticket.
- **El Diablo restaurant:** designed by César Manrique, it grills meat over a well heated by the volcano and has a glass-walled dining room overlooking the lava. **No reservations**: if you want to eat, go as soon as you park.
- **Experiencia Insólita:** a small-group guided visit (€59.90) on Mondays, Wednesdays and Saturdays at 8:45, about two hours long, with a climb up Montaña Rajada, the Tinecheide Refuge and a tasting. Book online.

**Camel rides** leave from the camel station by the LZ-67, on the way from Yaiza to the park, and are not included in the ticket: you pay separately on the spot.

## Fire Mountains tickets: price and opening hours

- **Price:** **€30** per adult; children aged 0 to 6 go free. There is a special rate for Canary Islands residents.
- **Where:** on the website of the Cabildo's Centres of Art, Culture and Tourism (cactlanzarote.com). **There is no ticket office.**
- **Compulsory time slot:** you choose a day and time between **9:00 and 15:45**. Arrive **15 minutes early**; outside your slot the ticket is not valid, and there are **no changes or refunds**.
- **If a slot doesn't appear, it's full:** try another time or day. In summer, Easter and long weekends, buy several days ahead.
- **Download your ticket before you set off:** mobile coverage in the park is unreliable.

Before this system you just turned up and queued: in July 2025 the local press reported **hundreds of cars at a standstill** between Tinajo and Yaiza waiting to get in. Time slots avoid those waits, as long as you stick to yours.

## Getting to Timanfaya and parking

The entrance is on the **LZ-67**, the road between Yaiza and Tinajo that crosses the Los Volcanes Natural Park. It's about 25 minutes from **Playa Blanca**, a little more from Puerto del Carmen or Arrecife.

At the **El Taro** junction you head up to **Islote de Hilario**. From there **you can't get out of the car** until the car park, which is on a slope: open the doors carefully.

**There is no public bus** to Timanfaya. Without a car, the alternative is a trip with the ticket included, either half-day or combined with Jameos del Agua and Cueva de los Verdes.

## Free in Timanfaya National Park: visitor centre and walks

The **Mancha Blanca Visitor and Interpretation Centre**, on the Tinajo-Yaiza road (km 11.5), opens daily from **9:00 to 16:00** and is free: rooms on volcanism, an eruption simulation and a walkway over the 1730-1736 lava.

The National Park also runs **free guided walks**, the only ones that enter areas closed to the public:

- **Tremesana Route:** about 3.5 km and 2 hours, easy.
- **Coastal Route:** about 10 km and 5 hours along the coast, demanding, with few dates.

Places are very limited and are booked through the **National Parks booking centre** (reservasparquesnacionales.es); the park leaflet sets age limits and requires closed shoes. If you can't get a place, a [private guided hike in the Los Volcanes Natural Park](/en/cities/lanzarote/activities/timanfaya-volcano-hike) crosses the lava surrounding the park on foot.

## When to go and tips

- **The first slot of the morning** is the coolest and has the best light for photos.
- **Spring, autumn and winter** are comfortable. In **July and August**, at midday, Islote de Hilario is an oven with no shade.
- **Combine it** with La Geria and El Golfo in the afternoon: they're less than half an hour away. More ideas in [what to see in Lanzarote](/en/guides/lanzarote/what-to-see-in-lanzarote).
- **Closed shoes:** the ground at Islote de Hilario is uneven and slippery in places.
- **Pushchairs:** hard to manoeuvre; a baby carrier is better.
- **Pets:** not allowed, except assistance dogs.
- **Water, hat and sunscreen:** the only facilities in the park are the restaurant and café at Islote de Hilario.
- **Stay within the permitted areas:** it's a protected space and the lava breaks easily.
