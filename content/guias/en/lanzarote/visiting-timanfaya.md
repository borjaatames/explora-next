**Timanfaya is the reason Lanzarote looks like nowhere else.** Between 1 September 1730 and April 1736 a chain of volcanoes kept erupting, burying villages and farmland in the south-west of the island; there was another, shorter eruption in 1824. Today about 51 km² are a **National Park**, and the only way to tour its interior is the **Volcano Route** at the **Fire Mountains** (Montañas del Fuego), run by the island council. This guide explains how it works as of October 2026.

## Tickets: online only, with a time slot

- **Price:** **€30** per adult; children aged 0 to 6 go free. There's a special rate for Canary Islands residents.
- **Where:** on the island council's Art, Culture and Tourism Centres website (cactlanzarote.com). **There's no ticket office.**
- **Compulsory time slot:** you choose a day and time between **9:00 and 15:45**. Be there **15 minutes early**; if you arrive outside your slot the ticket is invalid, and **there are no changes or refunds**.
- **If a slot doesn't show, it's full:** try another time or day. In summer, at Easter and on long weekends, buy several days ahead.
- **Download your ticket before you set off:** mobile coverage in the park is unreliable.

Before the current system you could just turn up and queue: in July 2025 the local press reported **hundreds of cars at a standstill** on the road between Tinajo and Yaiza waiting to get in. Time slots avoid those waits, as long as you stick to yours.

## Getting there and parking

The entrance is on the **LZ-67**, the road between Yaiza and Tinajo that crosses the Parque Natural de los Volcanes. From Playa Blanca it takes about 25 minutes, a little longer from Puerto del Carmen or Arrecife. At the **El Taro** junction you head up to **Islote de Hilario**; from there **you can't leave your car** until the car park, which is on a slope, so open your doors carefully.

**There's no public bus** to Timanfaya. Without a car, the alternative is a tour with entry included: the [half-day tour](/en/cities/lanzarote/activities/timanfaya-national-park-half-day-tour) or the one that [adds Jameos del Agua and Cueva de los Verdes](/en/cities/lanzarote/activities/timanfaya-jameos-cueva-de-los-verdes-tour).

## What you see inside

- **Volcano Route:** a coach circuit of about 35-40 minutes through craters, lava flows and lava fields, with recorded commentary on the eruption. Coaches leave roughly every 20 minutes and are adapted. You don't get off during the route.
- **Geothermal demonstrations:** at Islote de Hilario, staff pour water into a pipe sunk in the ground and it shoots out as steam, and dry brushwood bursts into flames in a pit. They're included in the ticket.
- **El Diablo restaurant:** designed by César Manrique, it grills meat over a pit that uses the volcano's heat and has a glass-walled dining room overlooking the lava field. **It doesn't take reservations**: if you want to eat, go as soon as you park.
- **Experiencia Insólita:** a small-group guided visit (€59.90) on Mondays, Wednesdays and Saturdays at 8:45, lasting about two hours, adding a climb up Montaña Rajada, the Tinecheide refuge and a tasting. Book online.

**Camel rides** leave from the camel station beside the LZ-67, on the way from Yaiza to the park, and aren't part of the Fire Mountains ticket: you pay separately on the spot.

## Free: visitor centre and ranger-led walks

The **Mancha Blanca Visitor and Interpretation Centre**, on the Tinajo-Yaiza road (km 11.5), opens daily from 9:00 to 16:00 and is free: rooms on volcanism, an eruption simulation and a walkway over the 1730-1736 lava.

The National Park also runs **free guided walks**, the only ones that enter areas closed to the public:

- **Tremesana Route:** about 3.5 km and 2 hours, easy.
- **Coastal Route (Ruta del Litoral):** about 10 km and 5 hours along the coast, demanding, with few dates.

Places are very limited and are booked through the **National Parks reservation centre** (reservasparquesnacionales.es); the park's leaflet sets age limits and requires closed footwear. If you can't get a place, the [private guided hike in the Parque Natural de los Volcanes](/en/cities/lanzarote/activities/timanfaya-volcano-hike) crosses the lava around the park on foot.

## When to go

- **The first slot of the morning** is the coolest and has the best light for photos.
- **Spring, autumn and winter** are comfortable. In **July and August**, at midday, the Islote de Hilario area is an oven with no shade.
- **Combine it** with La Geria and El Golfo in the afternoon: they're less than half an hour away.

## Practical tips

- **Closed shoes:** the ground at Islote de Hilario is uneven and slippery in places.
- **Pushchairs:** hard to manoeuvre; a baby carrier is better.
- **Pets:** not allowed, except assistance dogs.
- **Water, cap and sun cream:** the only facilities in the park are the restaurant and café at Islote de Hilario.
- **Stay within the permitted areas:** it's a protected site and the lava crumbles easily.
