**Lanzarote looks like no other island.** An eruption that lasted six years, from 1730 to 1736, buried almost a quarter of it in lava, and a local artist, **César Manrique**, persuaded the island to build low, white and around those volcanoes. The result is an island that has been a Biosphere Reserve since 1993, where the best things aren't on the beach but in the lava: **Timanfaya**, **Jameos del Agua**, **Cueva de los Verdes** and the vineyards of **La Geria**.

## How many days and when to go

**Four or five days** cover the essentials without rushing. With three, choose between La Graciosa and a beach day. With a week, add some hikes and an afternoon at Famara or Papagayo.

The weather is good all year. According to AEMET, average highs at the airport **range from 20.7 °C in January to 29.1 °C in August**, there's about **111 mm of rain a year** and nearly 3,000 hours of sunshine. What you'll notice most is the **wind**: the north-easterly trade wind blows almost daily, strongest in summer, and it feels cool in the north and at sea. **Spring and autumn** are the best seasons for walking; in July and August, Timanfaya at midday is an oven with no shade.

## Timanfaya and the volcanic south

**Timanfaya National Park** is the one visit you can't skip. The **Fire Mountains** ticket costs **€30** (free up to age 6), is sold **online only for a set date and time**, and includes the **Volcano Route** by coach, the only way to tour the inside of the park, plus the geothermal demonstrations at **Islote de Hilario**. You can drive your own car up to the Islote de Hilario car park, but you can't drive freely around the park. All the practical details are in [how to visit Timanfaya](/en/guides/lanzarote/visiting-timanfaya).

South of the park, along the LZ-30, lies **La Geria**: vines planted one by one in ash pits sheltered by stone walls, and wineries where you can taste volcanic Malvasía. On the west coast, **El Golfo** hides the **Charco de los Clicos**, a green lagoon beside a black beach; nearby are **Los Hervideros**, where the sea crashes through lava tunnels, and the **Janubio salt pans**, best at sunset.

## César Manrique's north

**Jameos del Agua** (from €17, 10:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:15) is a lava tube turned into a lake, garden, white pool and auditorium; tiny blind crabs live in the lake, and swimming or throwing coins is forbidden. A kilometre away, **Cueva de los Verdes** (€17, 9:30 to 16:45) is visited with a guide for about 50 minutes through the same lava tube, with stairs and low ceilings. As they're neighbours, **combine them**: book your Cueva time first and fit the Jameos around it.

Further up, the **Mirador del Río** (€9, 10:00 to 17:00) looks out from the Famara cliffs over **La Graciosa**; pick a clear day. On the way down, **Haría**, the valley of a thousand palms, with the house-museum where Manrique lived, and the **Cactus Garden** in Guatiza (€9), his last major work on the island.

## The centre: Teguise, the Foundation and Arrecife

**Teguise** was the island's capital for centuries and keeps its manor houses, churches and white streets; on **Sunday mornings** it hosts Lanzarote's busiest market, so go another day if you want peace. The **César Manrique Foundation** in Tahíche is the house the artist built over five lava bubbles (€10; €17 combined with the Haría house-museum). In **Arrecife**, the **Castillo de San José** houses the International Museum of Contemporary Art (€5.40), and the Charco de San Ginés is the capital's most pleasant corner.

## La Graciosa and the beaches

From **Órzola**, at the northern tip, the ferry takes **about 25 minutes** to **La Graciosa**, an island with no tarmac roads where you get around on foot, by bike or by 4x4 taxi. Timetables, fares and beaches are in [how to get to La Graciosa](/en/guides/lanzarote/how-to-get-to-la-graciosa).

On Lanzarote itself, the best swimming beaches are the **Papagayo coves** in the south, with golden sand and calm water; you reach them on a dirt track (non-residents pay **€3 per car**) or by boat from Playa Blanca. **Famara**, below the cliffs, is stunning for walks and surfing but has strong currents: don't swim under a yellow or red flag. In Puerto del Carmen, **Playa Grande** is the town beach with the most facilities, and in the north, **Caletón Blanco**, near Órzola, forms pools of white sand among the lava.

## Routes by area

1. **Volcanic south:** Timanfaya first thing, lunch in Yaiza or La Geria, El Golfo, Los Hervideros and the Janubio salt pans at sunset.
2. **Manrique's north:** Cueva de los Verdes at your booked time, Jameos del Agua, Mirador del Río and Haría; back via the Cactus Garden.
3. **Centre:** César Manrique Foundation in the morning, Teguise and the Santa Bárbara castle, afternoon at Famara.
4. **La Graciosa:** first ferry from Órzola, bike to the beaches and back mid-afternoon.
5. **Papagayo and Playa Blanca:** coves in the morning, by boat or kayak, and a free afternoon.

## Practical tips

- **Car:** the easiest option. Arrecife to Órzola is about 35 minutes; Playa Blanca to Timanfaya about 25. Always keep water and sun cream in the car.
- **Buses:** Arrecife Bus lines link the airport, Arrecife and the resorts, but **don't reach Timanfaya** and run to Órzola only a few times a day. Check timetables on their website before planning.
- **Island council centre tickets (2026):** Fire Mountains €30, Cueva de los Verdes €17, Jameos del Agua from €17, Mirador del Río €9, Cactus Garden €9, Castillo de San José €5.40; the Casa Museo del Campesino is free. Under-7s go free. **Multi-centre passes no longer exist**.
- **Booking:** for the Fire Mountains and Cueva de los Verdes tickets are **sold online only**, for a set date and time, with no changes or refunds. In summer and at Easter, buy several days ahead.
- **Sun and wind:** the wind is deceptive; the sun burns even when you don't feel hot. A cap, high-factor sun cream and a light jacket for the north.

## Where to stay

- **Puerto del Carmen:** the liveliest resort, with a town beach and a central location.
- **Playa Blanca:** quieter, next to Papagayo and with ferries to Fuerteventura.
- **Costa Teguise:** close to the airport and Arrecife, with steady wind for windsurfing.
- **Rural north and centre:** houses and small hotels in Haría, Teguise or La Geria, ideal with a car.

## Common mistakes

1. **Turning up at Timanfaya without a ticket:** there's no ticket office, and outside your time slot the ticket isn't valid.
2. **Swimming at Famara in rough seas:** the currents are dangerous.
3. **Going up to the Mirador del Río on a cloudy day:** you'll miss the view of La Graciosa.
4. **Visiting Teguise at midday on a Sunday without realising:** the market fills the streets and car parks.
5. **Missing the last ferry back from La Graciosa:** check the return time before heading to the beaches.

## In one sentence

Lanzarote is lava, Manrique and sea: book timed tickets for Timanfaya and Cueva de los Verdes, hire a car and save a day for La Graciosa.
