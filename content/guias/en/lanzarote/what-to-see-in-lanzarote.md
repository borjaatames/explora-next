## Lanzarote in 4 days: routes by area

With **four or five days** you see the essentials without rushing. With three, choose between La Graciosa and a beach day; with a week, add walks and an afternoon in Famara or Papagayo.

1. **Volcanic south:** Timanfaya first thing, lunch in Yaiza or La Geria, El Golfo, Los Hervideros and the Salinas de Janubio at sunset.
2. **Manrique's north:** Cueva de los Verdes at your time slot, Jameos del Agua, Mirador del Río and Haría; back via the Jardín de Cactus.
3. **Centre:** César Manrique Foundation in the morning, Teguise and the Castillo de Santa Bárbara, and an afternoon in Famara.
4. **La Graciosa:** first ferry from Órzola, bike to the beaches and back mid-afternoon.
5. **Papagayo and Playa Blanca:** coves in the morning, by boat or kayak, and a free afternoon.

## Timanfaya and the volcanic south

**Timanfaya National Park** is the must-see. Entry to **Montañas del Fuego** costs **€30** (free up to age 6), is sold **online only with date and time** and includes the **Ruta de los Volcanes** by coach, the only way to see the park's interior, plus the geothermal demonstrations at **Islote de Hilario**. You can drive up to the Islote de Hilario car park, but not around the park freely. All the practical details in [visiting Timanfaya](/en/guides/lanzarote/visiting-timanfaya).

South of the park, along the LZ-30, lies **La Geria**: vines planted one by one in ash pits protected by stone walls, and wineries where you can taste volcanic malvasía. Towards the west coast, **El Golfo** hides the **Charco de los Clicos**, a green lagoon beside a black beach; nearby are **Los Hervideros**, where the sea bursts through lava tunnels, and the **Salinas de Janubio**, best at sunset.

## César Manrique's north: Jameos del Agua and Cueva de los Verdes

- **Jameos del Agua** (from €17, 10:00-18:00, last entry 17:15): a volcanic tube turned into a lake, garden, white pool and auditorium. Tiny blind crabs live in the lake, and swimming or throwing coins is forbidden.
- **Cueva de los Verdes** (€17, 9:30-16:45): a kilometre away, visited with a guide for about 50 minutes along the same lava tube, with stairs and low ceilings. As they're neighbours, **combine them**: book the Cueva time first and fit the Jameos around it.
- **Mirador del Río** (€9, 10:00-17:00): looks out from the Risco de Famara over La Graciosa. Pick a clear day.
- **Haría and the Jardín de Cactus:** Haría is the valley of a thousand palms, with the house-museum where Manrique lived; the **Jardín de Cactus** in Guatiza (€9) was his last major work on the island.

Without a car, the [Timanfaya, Jameos del Agua and Cueva de los Verdes tour with tickets](/en/cities/lanzarote/activities/timanfaya-jameos-cueva-de-los-verdes-tour) covers the hardest-to-book sights in one day.

## Teguise, Arrecife, La Graciosa and the beaches

**Teguise** was the island's capital for centuries and keeps its manor houses, churches and white streets; on **Sunday mornings** it hosts Lanzarote's busiest market, so go another day if you want peace. The **César Manrique Foundation** in Tahíche is the house the artist built over five lava bubbles (€10; €17 combined with the Haría house-museum). In **Arrecife**, the **Castillo de San José** houses the International Museum of Contemporary Art (€5.40), and the Charco de San Ginés is the capital's most pleasant corner.

From **Órzola**, the ferry takes **about 25 minutes** to **La Graciosa**, an island with no paved roads that you explore on foot, by bike or by 4x4 taxi. Timetables, prices and beaches in [how to get to La Graciosa](/en/guides/lanzarote/how-to-get-to-la-graciosa).

- **Papagayo coves:** the best for swimming, in the south, with golden sand and calm water. Reach them by a dirt track (non-residents pay **€3 per car**) or by boat from Playa Blanca.
- **Famara:** beneath the cliffs, spectacular for walking and surfing, but with strong currents; not for swimming under a yellow or red flag.
- **Playa Grande (Puerto del Carmen):** the urban beach with the most facilities.
- **Caletón Blanco:** near Órzola, white-sand pools among the lava.

## When to go, getting around and prices

The climate is good all year. According to AEMET, average highs at the airport range from **20.7 °C in January to 29.1 °C in August**, with about **111 mm of rain a year** and almost 3,000 hours of sunshine. What you notice most is the **wind**: the trade wind blows from the north-east almost daily, strongest in summer. **Spring and autumn** are best for walking; in July and August, Timanfaya at midday is a shadeless oven.

- **Car:** the most convenient. Arrecife to Órzola is about 35 minutes; Playa Blanca to Timanfaya, about 25.
- **Buses:** Arrecife Bus links the airport, Arrecife and the resorts, but **doesn't reach Timanfaya** and runs few services to Órzola.
- **Cabildo centre tickets (2026):** Montañas del Fuego €30, Cueva de los Verdes €17, Jameos del Agua from €17, Mirador del Río €9, Jardín de Cactus €9, Castillo de San José €5.40; the Casa Museo del Campesino is free. Under-7s go free and **there are no longer multi-centre passes**.
- **Booking:** Montañas del Fuego and Cueva de los Verdes are **sold online only**, with date and time, and can't be changed or refunded. In summer and at Easter, book days ahead.
- **Sun and wind:** the wind is deceptive and the sun burns even when you don't feel hot. Hat, high-factor sunscreen and a light jacket for the north.

## Where to stay and common mistakes

- **Puerto del Carmen:** the liveliest resort, with an urban beach and a central location.
- **Playa Blanca:** quieter, next to Papagayo and with ferries to Fuerteventura.
- **Costa Teguise:** close to the airport and Arrecife, with constant wind for windsurfing.
- **Rural north and centre:** houses and small hotels in Haría, Teguise or La Geria, ideal with a car.

**Mistakes to avoid:**

1. **Turning up at Timanfaya without a ticket:** it's not sold at the gate, and it isn't valid outside your slot.
2. **Swimming at Famara in rough seas:** the currents are dangerous.
3. **Going up to the Mirador del Río in cloud:** you'll miss the view of La Graciosa.
4. **Visiting Teguise on a Sunday at midday without knowing:** the market fills the streets and car parks.
5. **Missing the last ferry from La Graciosa:** check the return time before heading to the beaches.
