**Lanzarote's volcanic landscape of cones, lava flows and black gravel tracks seems made for a buggy.** There are routes in the north, in the south near Puerto Calero, on-road to the La Geria vineyards and 4x4 trips with a driver that cover half the island. Here's how to choose.

The key: if you want to **drive on tracks**, a 2-3-hour buggy in the north or south. If you'd rather have **roads and scenery** without dust, the on-road buggy to La Geria. If you **don't want to drive**, a 4x4 trip with a driver.

## Types of tour

- **Buggy in the volcanic north (2-3 h):** leaves from the Costa Teguise area and follows dirt tracks between volcanoes and lava fields, with stops at viewpoints. The most popular route.
- **Buggy from Puerto Calero or Puerto del Carmen (2-3 h):** southern tracks with views of the volcanoes and coast, close to southern hotels.
- **On-road buggy to La Geria and El Cuervo volcano (3 h):** you drive on tarmac between vineyards and volcanic cones, stopping to walk up to a crater.
- **Can-Am buggy (3 h):** more powerful buggies for extra adrenaline on tracks.
- **4x4 trip (6 h):** with a driver, through the volcanoes and south coast or the volcanic north, with several stops.

## Tips

- **Bring your licence** if you're driving: without it you can't go.
- **Clothes and shoes** that can get dirty, sunglasses and a scarf or neck gaiter for the volcanic dust.
- **A 4-seat buggy** is cheaper per person for families or groups of friends.
- **Many tours include hotel pick-up;** check the pick-up area in the listing.
- **Stick to marked tracks:** going off them damages a fragile protected landscape.

## When to go

**All year.** In summer it's hot at midday: go early or in the afternoon. In winter temperatures are mild, perfect for a buggy.

To see the national park volcanoes, see [visiting Timanfaya](/en/guides/lanzarote/visiting-timanfaya). If you like wine, the La Geria route fits with our guide to [La Geria wineries](/en/guides/lanzarote/la-geria-wineries-lanzarote).

## In one sentence

To drive on volcanic tracks, a 2-3-hour buggy in the north or from Puerto Calero; for scenery without dust, the on-road buggy to La Geria; and without driving, a half-day 4x4 with a driver.
