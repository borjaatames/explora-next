**La Graciosa is the closest thing to a desert island left in the Canaries.** It lies across from the Famara cliffs, separated from Lanzarote by the El Río strait, and since June 2018 it has officially been the archipelago's **eighth inhabited island**, although administratively it still belongs to Teguise. About seven hundred people live here, almost all in **Caleta de Sebo**, and there are no tarmac roads: only sandy tracks between volcanoes and beaches. It's part of the Chinijo Archipelago Natural Park and surrounded by a marine reserve.

The **ferry on your own** is best if you want to explore the island and reach its beaches. The **catamaran trip** includes pickup, the ferry, paella on board and a swim off Playa Francesa, but on land you only see Caleta de Sebo.

## Getting to Órzola

**Órzola** is the fishing village at Lanzarote's northern tip, about 35 minutes by car from Arrecife on the LZ-1. There's **free parking** in the village, but in summer and at weekends it fills early; the ferry companies have parking for their passengers, on different terms depending on the company.

By bus, **Arrecife Bus line 9** links Arrecife and Órzola a few times a day, with departures that, according to La Graciosa's tourism website, connect with the boats. Check the Arrecife Bus timetable before relying on it.

## The ferry: companies, timetables and fares

**Líneas Romero** and **Biosfera Express** run the route with fast passenger boats. The crossing takes **about 25 minutes** and arrives at Caleta de Sebo harbour.

- **Biosfera Express fares (October 2026):** €16 one way and €29 return per non-resident adult; children €8 and €15. Canary Islands residents: €4 one way and €7.25 return.
- **Líneas Romero:** returns from €28 for adults and from €15 for children aged 2 to 11; Canary Islands residents get 75% off.
- **Biosfera Express timetable:** from Órzola at 8:00, 9:00, 10:30, 11:30, 13:00, 16:30, 17:30, 18:30 and 19:30; from La Graciosa at 7:00, 8:30, 9:30, 10:30, 12:00, 15:30, 16:30, 17:30 and 18:30. Líneas Romero has its own timetable, with sailings throughout the day.
- **Mind the midday gap:** with Biosfera Express there are no sailings from Órzola between 13:00 and 16:30, or from La Graciosa between 12:00 and 15:30.
- **Bikes and pets:** the companies accept them; mention it when you buy your ticket.

Check the **last return sailing** before heading to the beaches: if you miss it you'll have to sleep on the island, and in summer accommodation sells out.

## Getting around the island

On La Graciosa **you can't bring or hire a car**. You have three options:

- **On foot:** the southern beaches are less than an hour from Caleta de Sebo on flat sandy tracks. There's no shade anywhere along the way.
- **By bike:** several hire shops by the port rent mountain and electric bikes. The tracks have stretches of soft sand; an e-bike helps a lot.
- **By 4x4 taxi:** they wait at the harbour rank from 8:00. According to the island's tourism website (visitlagraciosa.com), beach transfers cost from €10 per person return, and the northern loop (Las Conchas, Lambra and Pedro Barba) about €25 per person for 90 minutes.

## The beaches

- **Playa Francesa:** the nearest southern beach, with golden sand and calm water, about 30-40 minutes on foot. It's where the catamarans anchor.
- **La Cocina and Montaña Amarilla:** at the southern tip, below the yellow volcano that gives the area its name. Turquoise water and few people.
- **Las Conchas:** in the north, a little over 5 km from Caleta de Sebo, facing the islet of Montaña Clara. It's the most spectacular, but the National Parks route leaflet warns of **strong currents**, and the island's beaches have **no lifeguard or rescue service**. Better to walk and climb Montaña Bermeja than swim in rough seas.
- **Pedro Barba:** a small hamlet of holiday homes on the east coast, with hardly any permanent residents.

## Practical tips

- **Water, food and sun protection:** outside Caleta de Sebo there are no shops, bars or shade. Bring a cap and more water than you think you'll need.
- **Footwear:** sandals for the sand, but trainers if you're walking the tracks or climbing Montaña Amarilla.
- **Cash:** carry some in case a shop or taxi doesn't take cards.
- **Where to eat:** Caleta de Sebo has several fish restaurants by the harbour; at lunchtime in high season they fill up.
- **Staying overnight:** there are guesthouses and apartments in Caleta de Sebo; a night there lets you enjoy the island without the day-trippers.
- **Respect the surroundings:** it's a protected natural area; stay on the tracks and keep off fragile formations.
