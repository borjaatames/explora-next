**Groups of dolphins live between Lanzarote and Fuerteventura for much of the year**, and boats, catamarans and speedboats leave Puerto del Carmen to look for them. The most popular trip is at sunset: two hours of calm sailing looking for dolphins with the sun going down behind. Here's how to choose yours.

The key: for a **relaxed, scenic outing**, a sunset boat from Puerto del Carmen. For **more adrenaline and distance**, a speedboat. To **combine dolphins with an island**, a half day to Lobos.

## Types of trip

- **Sunset boat (2-2.5 h):** calm sailing along the Puerto del Carmen coast looking for dolphins, with the sunset and often a drink. The most popular and easiest with children.
- **Speedboat (2 h):** small group, more speed and more miles covered looking for the animals. You'll get a little wet.
- **Speedboat to Lobos island (half day):** crosses towards Fuerteventura looking for dolphins and stops on Lobos for a walk and a swim.
- **Electric catamaran from Puerto Calero (4 h):** quiet and designed for low-impact whale and dolphin watching.

## Which species you'll see

- **Bottlenose and common dolphins:** the most frequent.
- **Spotted dolphins and pilot whales:** less frequent.
- **Whales:** rare; not something to expect on these trips.

## How to choose a responsible operator

- **It keeps its distance** and slows down near the animals.
- **It doesn't promise swimming with dolphins:** approaching them in the water is not allowed.
- **Read recent reviews** in the listing: they say a lot about how animals and passengers are treated.

## Practical tips

- **For the sunset trip,** bring a sweatshirt: it gets cool at sea once the sun goes down.
- **If you get seasick,** avoid the speedboat and sit in the middle of the boat.
- **On the speedboat,** protect your phone and camera from spray.
- **Arrive early** at the port: Puerto del Carmen has little parking near the quay.

## When to go

There are trips **all year**. The sea is usually calmer in the morning and late afternoon; in summer the wind blows harder around midday.

To see dolphins without fuss, a 2-hour sunset boat from Puerto del Carmen; for more adventure, a speedboat, and to add an island, a half day to Lobos.
