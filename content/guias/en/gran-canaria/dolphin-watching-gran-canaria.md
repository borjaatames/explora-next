**The waters off southern Gran Canaria are a good place to see wild dolphins.** Off the coast between Arguineguín and Mogán the seabed drops quickly to great depths and groups of dolphins are around for much of the year. Trips are short, affordable and leave from ports a few minutes from Maspalomas. Here's how to choose yours.

The key: if you only want to **see dolphins**, a 2-2.5-hour trip from Puerto Rico or Arguineguín is enough. If you also want to **swim and snorkel**, choose a 3-4-hour catamaran or the Mogán trip with a swim stop.

## Which species you'll see

- **Bottlenose dolphin:** the easiest to see, in small groups close to the coast.
- **Atlantic spotted dolphin:** very active, often rides the bow wave and jumps.
- **Common dolphin and pilot whale:** less frequent, depending on the season and the sea.
- **Whales:** rorquals sometimes pass by, but that's a lucky sighting, not something to expect.

## Types of trip

- **Dolphin-watching boat (2-2.5 h):** straight out to look for dolphins and back. The cheapest option and the best with young children.
- **Catamaran with snorkelling (3-4 h):** looks for dolphins and adds a swim stop with snorkelling gear.
- **Small-group or eco trip (3 h):** fewer passengers, commentary on the animals and more care with distances.
- **Trip from Mogán (4 h):** combines dolphins, the west coast and a swim, with fewer boats around.

## How to choose a responsible operator

- **It keeps its distance:** a well-run boat slows down, doesn't cut across the animals' path and moves away if they seem disturbed.
- **It doesn't promise swimming with dolphins:** approaching them in the water is not allowed.
- **Small groups** usually mean a better experience and less noise.
- **Read recent reviews** in the listing: they say a lot about how the boat treats animals and passengers.

## Practical tips

- **Book a morning trip:** calmer sea and better visibility.
- **Bring** a cap, polarised sunglasses, sunscreen and a sweatshirt.
- **If you get seasick,** choose a big catamaran and sit in the middle.
- **Camera or phone with a strap:** dolphins appear quickly; have burst mode ready.

## When to go

You can see them **all year**. The south is sheltered from the trade wind and the sea is fine for sailing on most days. In summer the afternoon wind is stronger.

To see dolphins, a 2-2.5-hour trip from Puerto Rico or Arguineguín is enough, ideally in the morning; if you want a swim, take a 3-4-hour catamaran with snorkelling.
