**Gran Canaria has warm water all year, good visibility and volcanic seabeds full of life**: parrotfish, rays, schools of grunts and, with luck, turtles. You can take a look with a mask and snorkel at a sheltered beach or do your first scuba dive with no experience at all. Here's how to choose.

The key: if you want to **see fish without fuss**, do a guided snorkel at Las Canteras or on a boat trip. If you'd like to **try diving**, a 3-hour try dive is the safest way to start.

## Snorkelling: where and how

- **Las Canteras (Las Palmas):** a natural rock barrier, La Barra, breaks the waves and creates a very calm lagoon. The best choice for beginners and families, with guided trips and gear.
- **By boat in the south:** catamarans and yachts from Puerto Rico or Mogán stop in clear-water coves. Snorkelling, swimming and sometimes dolphins.
- **Kayak and snorkel in Mogán:** you paddle to sea caves and swim in spots you can't reach on foot.

## Try dive: what to expect

- **Who it's for:** anyone who can swim and is in good health, with no certificate or experience. The minimum age is usually 8-10, depending on the centre.
- **What it's like:** a briefing on land, practice in shallow water and a 30-45-minute dive with the instructor beside you, down to about 12 m at most.
- **Where:** sheltered beaches in the south and east, Puerto Rico or Las Canteras, depending on the centre and the sea.
- **What's included:** all the gear (wetsuit, tank, regulator, mask and fins) and sometimes photos.

## If you're already certified

Gran Canaria has good dives for certified divers, such as the **El Cabrón** reserve in Arinaga, with volcanic arches and caves, or the wrecks at **Sardina del Norte**. Dive centres run shore and boat dives.

## Tips

- **Tell the centre** if you take medication or have ear, heart or breathing problems; you'll be asked to fill in a medical form.
- **Don't fly** for 12-18 hours after diving.
- **Bring** swimwear on, a towel and reef-safe sunscreen.
- **Don't touch or feed the animals** and don't stand on the seabed or reef.
- **Mornings are best:** less wind and better visibility.

## When to go

You can dive **all year**. The water is around 19 °C in winter and 23-24 °C at the end of summer; in winter the centre will give you a thicker wetsuit.
