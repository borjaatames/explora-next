**The south of Gran Canaria is the best part of the island for a boat trip.** Between Maspalomas and Mogán the coast is sheltered from the trade winds, the sea is usually calm and there are coves you can only reach by water. There are trips of every kind: big catamarans with snorkelling, yachts with open bar, tapas cruises and private charters. Here's how to choose.

The key: if you want to **see dolphins and swim** on a budget, a shared 3-4-hour catamaran is enough. If you want **a day at sea** with food, drinks and fewer people, choose a yacht or a small-group catamaran.

## Types of boat

- **Shared catamaran (3-4 h):** the most popular. Wide and stable, with nets to lie on, a swim stop and snorkelling gear. Many look for dolphins on the way.
- **Catamaran or sailboat with food (4-5 h):** add lunch or a barbecue on board and an open bar. Calmer, with fewer passengers.
- **Shared yacht (4 h):** a smaller, more comfortable boat with drinks and food included. A good choice for couples.
- **Tapas cruise (3-4 h):** a relaxed trip from Puerto Rico with Canarian snacks, drinks and a swim.
- **Private yacht:** for families or groups, with a tailor-made route. Priced per boat, not per person.

## Which port to leave from

- **Puerto Rico:** the port with the most departures, 15-20 minutes by car or bus from Playa del Inglés and Maspalomas.
- **Puerto de Mogán:** the prettiest, with trips towards the western cliffs. A good fit if you want to spend the day in the village.
- **Arguineguín:** a small fishing port between Maspalomas and Puerto Rico, home to several dolphin-watching boats.
- **Pasito Blanco:** a marina next to Maspalomas, where some open-bar catamarans start.

A scheduled boat (Líneas Salmón) also runs between Arguineguín, Puerto Rico and Mogán, handy for getting along the coast without a car.

## What's usually included

- **Shared catamaran:** crew, life jacket, snorkelling gear and sometimes a soft drink. Food is usually extra.
- **Trips with food:** lunch or tapas, open bar (soft drinks, beer, wine or sangria), snorkelling gear and sometimes a kayak or paddleboard.
- **Hotel pick-up:** only on some, almost always around Maspalomas, Playa del Inglés and Puerto Rico. Check the listing before booking.

## Tips

- **Book a morning trip** if you get seasick: the wind usually picks up in the afternoon.
- **Bring** swimwear, a towel, reef-safe sunscreen, a cap and a sweatshirt for the way back.
- **With children,** 3-hour trips on a big catamaran with a toilet and shade work best.
- **Choose an operator that respects wildlife:** licensed boats don't chase dolphins or get too close.
- **If the sea is rough,** the operator may cancel; you're usually offered another date or a refund.

## When to go

You can go **all year**. The water is around 19 °C in winter and 23-24 °C at the end of summer. In summer the trade wind blows harder, but the south-west stays sheltered.
