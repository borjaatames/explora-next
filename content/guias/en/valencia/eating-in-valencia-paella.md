## What authentic Valencian paella is

Rule number one: **Valencian paella is not seafood paella**. The original comes from the farm and the market garden and contains **chicken, rabbit, garrofó (a flat white bean), green beans (ferraúra) and, depending on the house, snails**. No seafood, no chorizo, no peas, no onion.

It's made with bomba rice, sofrito, saffron and the prized **socarrat**, the toasted layer at the bottom. It's a **lunchtime** dish, made for sharing.

If you see "paella" with prawns, mussels, chorizo and lemon on top, on a terrace with laminated photos opposite the Cathedral, it isn't Valencian paella: it's a dish for tourists. Walk two streets and everything changes.

## Where to eat paella in Valencia: the Albufera and El Palmar

**Good paella is in the Albufera and El Palmar**, not in the tourist centre:

- **Getting to El Palmar:** EMT bus 24 or 25 from the centre (€1.50, 45 minutes; since June 2026 they run roughly every 25-35 minutes), or 25 minutes by car, with free parking in the village.
- **Opening hours:** most restaurants close at 17:00.
- **After lunch:** a boat trip on the Albufera and sunset from the jetty.

In the city, look for **well-known neighbourhood rice restaurants** (book and go early). For tapas and a variety of rice dishes, the area around the **Central Market**, the **Carmen** quarter and **Ruzafa** are safe bets.

The **Central Market**, one of the largest in Europe, opens Monday to Saturday from 7:30 to 15:00 (closed on Sundays). Its horchatería and the clóchina mussel stalls (May to August) make the best quick stop.

## Valencian rice dishes beyond paella

| Rice dish | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Arroz a banda | Dry, cooked in fish stock; pure taste of the sea |
| Arroz del senyoret | Everything peeled, no shells, "for gentlemen" |
| Arroz al horno | Baked with chickpeas, black pudding and potato; hearty |
| Arroz a la marinera / caldoso | With seafood, done properly |
| Fideuà | Like paella but with noodles instead of rice |

## Beyond rice: horchata and agua de Valencia

- **Horchata with fartons:** Valencia's classic afternoon snack, a cold tiger-nut drink with sweet buns for dipping. The historic horchaterías (Daniel, Vida) are in **Alboraya**, 15 minutes by metro (line 3, Alboraya-Palmaret stop), with the tiger nuts on display. A glass with fartons, **€4-5**.
- **Agua de Valencia:** the local aperitif, with cava, orange juice, vodka and gin. It goes to your head fast; drink with care.
- **All i pebre:** the Albufera's classic eel stew.
- **Esgarraet, titaina and clóchinas** (Valencian mussels, in season).

## Times, prices and how to order

- **Paella is a lunchtime dish:** serious rice restaurants serve it from **13:30 to 16:00** and many don't make it at night. If a terrace offers you paella at 21:00, it's probably reheated.
- **Order for at least two**; it takes **30-45 minutes** because it's cooked to order. Good places ask when you book whether you'll want rice: say yes and you'll skip the wait.
- **Rough prices:** Valencian paella, **€15-22** per person; arroz a banda or del senyoret, €16-20; in El Palmar, set menus with starters, rice and dessert for €25-35. If it's under €12 per person, be wary.
- **Tipping:** not expected; rounding up or leaving €1-2 at a rice table is more than enough.

## Tips

- **In the evening, go for tapas:** ordering paella for dinner marks you as a tourist.
- **Order Valencian paella where they know the difference** from the seafood one.
- **Don't eat rice in Plaza de la Reina:** tourist prices, average quality.
- **Be wary of laminated photos and touts at the door.**
- **Avoid reheated single-portion paella:** good paella is made to order and for sharing.
