## The mascletà in Valencia: how to see (and hear) it properly

It's a daytime gunpowder show where what matters isn't what you see but the **noise and the vibration** you feel in your chest. It lasts a few minutes and ends with the *terratrèmol*, the loudest finale.

- **Arrive more than an hour early** if you want a spot in the streets leading to the square; by 13:30 there's no room.
- **You don't need the front row:** a block away it sounds almost as loud.
- **Fewer crowds:** weekdays early in the month.
- **Open your mouth at the finale:** it eases the pressure on your ears. With kids, bring earplugs.
- **To watch in comfort**, some balconies on the square are rented out; Valencia's tourist office lists them on its website.

## The fallas: what they are and where to see them

Each neighbourhood builds its own papier-mâché and wood monument, some **over twenty metres tall**, to be burned on the night of the 19th. **From the 16th to the 19th** they're all on display in the street and free to see.

- The most spectacular compete in the **Sección Especial**, in neighbourhoods such as Ruzafa, the Ensanche or the centre.
- **Ruzafa's illuminated streets** are worth a stroll at night.
- Beforehand, at the **Ninot Exhibition** in the Museu de les Ciències, you can see the figures each falla presents. The public votes for the one saved from the flames: the *ninot indultat*. In 2026 entry cost **€4 (€2 children)** and it ran from 7 February to 15 March.

## The Flower Offering and the Fallas cremà

- **Offering (17 and 18 March):** thousands of falleros in traditional dress parade with flowers to Plaza de la Virgen, where they form the mantle of the Virgen de los Desamparados. The best spots are **Calle San Vicente, Calle Mar and Calle Caballeros**. Afterwards, you can visit the flower tapestry.
- **Nit del Foc (night of the 18th to the 19th):** the big fireworks display, around 1:30, in the old Turia riverbed.
- **Cremà (19 March):** you don't need to go to the centre. Every neighbourhood burns its own falla, so pick a big one near where you're staying and **arrive half an hour early**. The City Hall falla is the last and the most crowded.

## What to do at Fallas: getting around, sleeping and using your mornings

- **Metro and tram:** the centre is closed to traffic and parking is practically impossible.
- **Hotel:** it's Valencia's busiest week of the year. **Book months ahead.**
- **Almost everything is free:** the mascletà, the Offering, the Nit del Foc, the cremà and walking among the fallas. You only pay for what you eat and drink and the odd paid visit.
- **In the mornings**, before the mascletà, make time for the old town: the **Cathedral**, the **Silk Exchange** and San Nicolás.
- **With kids**, the **Oceanogràfic** is the indoor plan if it rains.
- **Combine trips:** in 2027 Fallas ends on Friday the 19th and Seville's Holy Week starts on Sunday the 21st.

## Common mistakes

- **Turning up to the mascletà at 13:50:** you'll end up three streets away.
- **Only going on the 19th:** you'll see the cremà but miss the fallas on display and the Offering. Ideally, go **from the 16th to the 19th**.
- **Forgetting comfortable shoes and clothes:** you walk a lot and there are firecrackers on every corner.
- **Parking near a falla on the night of the 19th:** streets close and firefighters hose down the façades.
