## Where does the ship dock in Valencia?

Cruise ships use the **Trasmed passenger terminal**, run by the Grimaldi group, at the **northern entrance of the port**, the part closest to the city and open to the public. The cruise berths for 2026 are the Transversal and Poniente quays, next to the terminal, for ships under 300 metres.

If your ship docks at a quay further inside the commercial port, the cruise line runs a bus to the terminal. All onward transport leaves from there.

## From the Valencia cruise terminal to the city centre: taxi or bus

| Option | Time | Price | Keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Taxi or ride-hailing | 15-20 min | About €12-15 | Signposted rank outside the terminal; Uber, Cabify and Bolt too |
| EMT bus, line 4 | About 45 min to Torres de Serranos | €2 | Stop 1306, about a 10-min walk |
| Cruise line shuttle | Varies | Set by the cruise line | Ask where it stops and whether it charges |
| On foot | Over an hour | Free | Not worth it; fine for the beach |

- **Taxi:** the best option as soon as there are two or more of you. The 2026 fare has a **€2** flag fall and **€1.36/km** by day (€2.60 and €1.48 at night and on public holidays). Ask to be dropped at **Plaza de la Reina**, next to the Cathedral.
- **Bus:** leaves from stop **1306, Pont de les Drassanes – Terminal marítima**. **Line 4** heads to Plaza del Ayuntamiento and **line 95** to the beach and the Marina. Single ticket, **€2**.
- **On foot** you reach the Las Arenas and Cabanyal beaches (20-25 minutes) and the Bus Turístic stop at the Marina (about 20 minutes).

## Valencia on a cruise: what to see in 4, 6 or 8 hours

- **4 hours:** taxi to Plaza de la Reina. **Cathedral and Miguelete tower**, Plaza de la Virgen, **Silk Exchange** and **Central Market** (not on Sundays).
- **6 hours:** all of the above, plus the **Carmen district**, the **Torres de Serranos** and a paella lunch.
- **8 hours:** the centre in the morning and the **City of Arts and Sciences** in the afternoon (it lies between the port and the centre, closer to the port); or the centre and paella on the beach, a stone's throw from the port. The Oceanogràfic needs at least 2-3 hours: book online in high season (from €37).

**Guide prices:** Cathedral and Miguelete, **€9** with audio guide (Monday to Saturday, 10:30-18:30); Silk Exchange, **€2**; Torres de Serranos and Torres de Quart, **€2** each, free on Sundays.

If you want a guide, private tours start at the **Torres de Serranos**, about 15 minutes by taxi from the port, and none pick up at the ship. The **2-hour** old town tour costs from about €48 per person for two (about €27 for four); the 4-hour one with the Silk Exchange and horchata, from about €88 (€59 for four). If you'd rather not walk, the 1-hour private tuk-tuk starts at €30 per person, for up to 8 people.

## Ship excursion or on your own

In Valencia there's no need to leave the city: everything important can be seen on your own or on a walking tour.

- **Cruise line excursion:** the ship **waits for its own excursions** if they run late.
- **Private tour in the centre** (from €48 per person, 2 hours): you control the return time, but the ship won't wait.
- **On your own:** taxi and tickets. All the risk is yours, though the transfer is short.

On a 2-4 hour tour in the centre, the real risk is the trip back: leave margin for the taxi.

## What time should you be back on board?

- **All aboard** is usually 30 to 60 minutes before departure, in **ship's time**: it's in the daily programme.
- **Rule of thumb:** be at the terminal **1 hour before all aboard** and leave the centre **1 h 30 before**. If you're relying on the bus, add another 30 minutes.
- If your ship docks far from the terminal, allow for the cruise line's internal bus too.
- Carry your ship card and ID.

## Sundays, holidays and common mistakes

If your call falls on a Sunday, switch the order: City of Arts and Sciences or the beach in the morning, Cathedral in the afternoon.

- **Central Market:** closed on Sundays; open Monday to Saturday, 7:30-15:00.
- **Silk Exchange:** free on Sundays and holidays, but closed in the afternoon.
- **Cathedral and Miguelete:** on Sundays, afternoon visits only.
- **Don't try to walk to the centre:** it's about 5 km through the port area.
- **Paella at lunchtime:** in Valencia it's a midday dish, not dinner.
- **Don't combine the Oceanogràfic and the old town in 4 hours:** there isn't time for both.
- **Don't wait for the last bus back:** with two or more people, a taxi costs about the same and is quicker.
