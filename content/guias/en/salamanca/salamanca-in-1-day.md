Salamanca **works in a single day**. Everything touristy fits in under 1 km² (0.4 mi²), the major monuments are 5–10 minutes apart on foot, and the old town is flat and pedestrian: no public transport needed. With 30,000 students in a city of 145,000, the golden stone comes with atmosphere.

## How to get to Salamanca

- **Avant/Alvia from Madrid Chamartín:** 1h 30min, €25–50. Few daily trains: book ahead, especially at weekends.
- **Car:** 2h 15min via the A-6 and A-50.
- **Avanza bus from Estación Sur:** 2h 30min, €15–20.

By train you get 7–8 real hours; by car or staying overnight you also get the night, when Salamanca is at its best.

## The must-sees, stop by stop

### 10 AM: Plaza Mayor

Baroque, built by the Churriguera family between 1729 and 1755. Spend 30–45 minutes: breakfast on a terrace (coffee and toast, **€4–6**, pricey but worth it), find the medallions (Cervantes, Fray Luis de León, Charles V, Philip V) and take a photo to compare with the lit-up plaza at night. Don't eat lunch here: the tourist-menu restaurants are poor value.

### 10:45 AM: Casa de las Conchas and La Clerecía

**Casa de las Conchas** is a 15th-century palace with a façade of scallop shells, now a public library with **free admission** and a fine Mudéjar patio.

Opposite, the **Clerecía towers (Scala Coeli)** cost **€3.75**, free on Tuesdays 10 AM – noon (except holidays, Holy Week, July and August). This is the best view in Salamanca, with the University, both cathedrals and Plaza Mayor below you.

### Noon: University of Salamanca

The oldest university in Spain still in operation (founded 1218), with its **Plateresque façade** of 1512. Tradition says **find the frog on a skull** among the decoration without help and you pass your exams.

- Edificio Histórico: **€10** general, **€5** reduced (students, retirees, large families).
- **Free on Monday afternoons** (after 2 PM in summer).
- April 1 – October 15: Mon–Sat 10 AM – 8 PM; October 16 – March 31: Mon–Sat 10 AM – 7 PM. Sundays and holidays 10 AM – 2 PM all year.

Inside: the **Aula de Fray Luis de León** with its original 16th-century benches, the chapel, the Paraninfo and the red **vítores** on the walls, painted by new doctors with bull's blood and oil. In depth: [University of Salamanca: how to visit](/en/guides/salamanca/university-of-salamanca-how-to-visit).

### 1:30 PM: the two cathedrals

Two cathedrals joined on the inside, on one **combined €10 ticket** with audio guide (both cathedrals, cloister, chapels and museum).

- **New Cathedral** (Late Gothic and Baroque): on the Puerta de Ramos, find the **astronaut** and the **dragon eating ice cream** added by 20th-century restorers.
- **Old Cathedral** (Romanesque, 12th century): the altarpiece by **Nicolás Florentino** (53 panels, 15th century), the octagonal **Cimborrio del Gallo** dome and the **Chapel of Santa Bárbara**, where doctoral candidates were examined.

Don't see only the New one and leave: the Old one is better.

### 2:30 PM: lunch

- **Calle Van Dyck** (8–10 minutes from Plaza Mayor): where locals eat; serious taverns with tapas and raciones.
- **Calle Meléndez**: creative tapas at student prices.
- **Rúa Mayor and Plaza del Corrillo**: central classics with Guijuelo ham.

Try **hornazo** (Salamanca's stuffed pie; buy it at a bakery, not a tourist kiosk), **Guijuelo Iberian ham**, **farinato** (fried sausage) and **chanfaina** (rice with offal).

### 4 PM: Casa Lis or San Esteban, not both

**Casa Lis** (€4), the Art Nouveau and Art Déco museum with stained glass over the river, free on Thursdays 11 AM – 2 PM; or **San Esteban Convent** (€4), for its Plateresque cloister. Afterwards, wander Rúa Mayor, Plaza de Anaya and the Patio de Escuelas without a plan.

### 6:30 PM: sunset at the Roman Bridge

Ten minutes downhill to the Tormes. The bridge (Roman in origin, rebuilt in the 17th century) gives the classic view of both cathedrals and La Clerecía in golden light. With energy left, climb to the Arrabal neighbourhood on the far bank for another angle.

## What you can skip

The Salamanca Museum, the Iglesia de la Purísima, the Convento de las Dueñas (closed many afternoons; worth it with two days), the Cueva de Salamanca (five minutes is enough) and any trip to Alba de Tormes or La Alberca, which need at least half a day.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving before dark.** The lit-up stone is another city; stay until 9 PM if you can.
- **Skipping the Old Cathedral or La Clerecía**, the two things rushed visitors miss most.
- **Bad shoes.** Expect 10–15 km (6–9 mi) on foot.

## If you stay overnight

Central hotels cost **€60–100** at short notice and the city rarely fills up, except during Lunes de Aguas or the September fairs; early trains run back the next morning. For two days, see [Salamanca weekend trip](/en/guides/salamanca/salamanca-weekend-trip).
