## What to see in Salamanca: the must-sees

**Plaza Mayor.** Probably the most beautiful in Spain, Baroque, by the Churriguera family (1729-1755). Have breakfast on a terrace (coffee and toast, €4-6: pricey, but worth it), look for the medallions (Cervantes, Fray Luis, Charles V) and come back at night, when it's transformed by the lighting.

**Casa de las Conchas and the Clerecía.** The 15th-century house with its scallop-shell façade is now a free public library with a lovely Mudéjar courtyard. Opposite, the **Clerecía towers** (Scala Coeli, **€3.75**; free on Tuesdays 10:00-12:00 outside summer): the best view in Salamanca, with the University, the cathedrals and the Plaza Mayor at your feet.

**University.** Spain's oldest working university (1218), with the 1512 plateresque façade where you have to **find the frog on the skull** without help. Historic Building **€10** (€5 reduced), **free on Monday afternoons**; Monday to Saturday 10:00 to 19:00-20:00 and Sunday mornings only. Inside: Fray Luis's lecture hall with its original benches, the chapel, the great hall and the graduates' vítores painted in bull's-blood red. In depth in [how to visit the University](/en/guides/salamanca/university-of-salamanca-how-to-visit).

**The two cathedrals** (joint ticket **€10** with audio guide). On the New Cathedral's façade (late Gothic and Baroque), look for the **astronaut** and the dragon eating an ice cream added by 20th-century restorers. The **Old Cathedral**, 12th-century Romanesque, is a surprise: Nicolás Florentino's 53-panel altarpiece, the Rooster Tower dome and the Santa Bárbara chapel, where doctoral candidates sat their exams. Don't skip it: most people see the New one and leave.

**Casa Lis or San Esteban** (**€4** each), not both. Casa Lis is the Art Nouveau and Art Deco museum with stained glass above the river (free on Thursdays 11:00-14:00); San Esteban is the Dominican convent with a plateresque cloister.

**Roman Bridge at sunset.** Ten minutes downhill, the full postcard: the cathedrals on the hill and the Clerecía in golden stone. If you have energy left, climb to the Arrabal for another view.

## How to get there

- **Avant or Alvia from Chamartín:** 1 h 30, €25-50. Few departures: book, especially at weekends. There's left luggage at the station.
- **Car:** 2 h 15 on the A-6 and A-50; handy if you add La Alberca or Alba de Tormes.
- **Avanza bus from Estación Sur:** 2 h 30, €15-20.

## Where to eat

- **Calle Van Dyck** (8-10 minutes from the Plaza Mayor): where locals eat, serious tapas and ración taverns.
- **Meléndez:** young crowd and student prices.
- **Rúa Mayor and Plaza del Corrillo:** classics with Guijuelo ham.
- **What to order:** **hornazo** (Salamanca's meat pie; buy it at a bakery, not a kiosk), Guijuelo ham, farinato and chanfaina.
- **In the Plaza Mayor, just coffee or a beer.**

## What you can skip and common mistakes

With only one day, skip the Salamanca Museum, La Purísima, the Las Dueñas convent (it closes many afternoons; with two days, go), the Cueva de Salamanca (five minutes) and any trip to Alba de Tormes or La Alberca, which take half a day.

1. **Staying in the Plaza Mayor:** it's the start, not the visit. And don't eat there.
2. **Skipping the Old Cathedral.**
3. **Not climbing the Clerecía:** €3.75 for the best view.
4. **Not looking for the frog.**
5. **Leaving in daylight:** the floodlit stone at night is another city; hold on until 21:00.

## If you stay overnight

It's worth it. Central hotels go for €60-100 at the last minute (except during Lunes de Aguas or fairs) and return trains run from early morning. The floodlit Plaza Mayor and terraces open until 2:00 are worth the night; with two days, see [Salamanca in a weekend](/en/guides/salamanca/salamanca-weekend-trip).
