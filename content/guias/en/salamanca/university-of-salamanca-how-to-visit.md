## What to see inside the University of Salamanca

- **The cloister**, on two levels with 23 arches per floor, the upper ones Renaissance.
- **Fray Luis de León's lecture hall**, just as it was in the 16th century: benches carved by penknife, worn floor and the pulpit. This is where he returned after five years in prison for translating the Song of Songs and said "As we were saying yesterday…".
- **Unamuno's lecture hall** and the **Paraninfo** (great hall), with 16th-century Flemish tapestries and a coffered ceiling, where in 1936 Unamuno told Millán-Astray "you will win, but you will not convince".
- **The chapel**, 18th century, with a 17th-century organ that still plays on some Sundays.
- **The Historic Library** (1254), with incunabula and unique manuscripts. **It can only be seen from a staircase, through glass**: you can't go in, but the view of the wooden shelves is still impressive.
- **The vítores:** red inscriptions with "VICTOR", a name and a date. When a student earned a doctorate, their name was painted in bull's blood and oil. There's one for Calderón de la Barca.

**The Cielo de Salamanca.** A minute away, in the Escuelas Menores, free and little known: the fragment of the old library's ceiling painted by Fernando Gallego in the 15th century, a map of the night sky with the zodiac and mythological creatures, unique in Spain. It takes fifteen minutes: don't miss it.

## University of Salamanca tickets, prices and opening hours

| Space | Price | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historic Building (Escuelas Mayores) | **€10** (€5 reduced) | Cloister, historic lecture halls, chapel, library |
| Unamuno House Museum | €7 (€3 reduced; €15 combined) | Where he lived as rector |
| Cielo de Salamanca (Escuelas Menores) | Free | 15th-century mural by Fernando Gallego |
| Escuelas Mayores and Menores courtyards | Free | The plateresque façade with the frog |

- **Under 12:** free.
- **Free on Monday afternoons** (from 14:00 in summer), with small print: queues from 45 minutes beforehand, limited capacity and no guarantee of entry in high season. If you're flexible, pay the €10 another day.
- **Hours:** Monday to Saturday **10:00-20:00** from April to mid-October and until **19:00** in winter; Sundays and holidays 10:00-14:00 only. Closed 25 December and 1 and 6 January.

## The plateresque façade and the frog

Before going in, spend 15-20 minutes on the **1512 plateresque façade**, free and always accessible. It has three tiers: at the bottom, the medallion of the Catholic Monarchs; in the middle, Charles V and the imperial coats of arms; at the top, the virtues, the Doctors of the Church and the popes.

And the **frog on a skull**: whoever finds it without help passes their exam, gets married or comes back to Salamanca, depending on who you ask. Just one clue: it's smaller and higher up than you think. If you can't see it in 15 minutes, ask whoever is searching next to you; the secret gets shared.

## How long and how to visit: on your own or with a guide

- **Historic Building only:** 1 h 30 to 2 hours.
- **With the Escuelas Menores and the Cielo:** **2 h 30**, recommended.
- **With the Unamuno House:** about 3 hours.

**On your own** works if you arrive with a little context: read for ten minutes beforehand about Fray Luis, Unamuno and medieval doctorates and the visit changes completely. **With an official guide** (booked at the ticket office or online, 1 h to 1 h 30) you get stories that aren't on the panels, and it works better with teenagers. Night-time free tours **don't go inside**: they only walk around the façade.

## Tips and common mistakes

1. **Don't pay to see the façade:** it's free; the ticket is for the interior.
2. **Don't stop at the Historic Building:** the Cielo de Salamanca and the Escuelas Menores courtyard are a minute away and free.
3. **Look for the frog without rushing.**
4. **Free Monday is in the afternoon**, not the morning.
5. **Don't expect to go into the Library.**
6. **Better during term (October to June):** with 30,000 students, tunas (student minstrel groups) in the Plaza Mayor and busy cafés by Anaya, the city has a pulse. In August, without students, it loses its soul.
