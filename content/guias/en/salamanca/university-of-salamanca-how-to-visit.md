The University of Salamanca is **the oldest university in Spain** (founded 1218) and one of the four oldest in the world, alongside Bologna, Oxford and Paris. What makes the visit special: in the historic classrooms you sit where Fray Luis de León, Nebrija and Unamuno taught, on benches carved with knives by students centuries ago.

## What you actually visit and what it costs

"The University" isn't one building but several spaces:

| Space | Price | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edificio Histórico (Escuelas Mayores) | €10 (€5 reduced) | Cloister, historic classrooms, chapel, library |
| Casa-Museo Unamuno | €7 (€3 reduced) | The house where he lived as rector |
| Edificio Histórico + Casa-Museo Unamuno | €15 | Combined ticket |
| Cielo de Salamanca (Escuelas Menores) | Free | 15th-century mural by Fernando Gallego |
| Patios de Escuelas Mayores and Menores | Free | The Plateresque façade with the frog |

- The **façade, where the frog is, is free and always accessible**. You only pay to go inside.
- Reduced tickets: students, retirees, large families and groups of 20+. **Under-12s and USAL members** go free with proof.

### When it's free

Entry to the Edificio Histórico is **free on Monday afternoons** (from 2 PM in summer, earlier in winter). The fine print: a queue forms 30–45 minutes before, capacity is limited and entry isn't guaranteed in high season. Monday morning is paid. If you're flexible, pay the €10 another day and visit without the crush.

### Hours

- **April 1 – October 15:** Mon–Sat 10 AM – 8 PM; Sundays and holidays 10 AM – 2 PM.
- **October 16 – March 31:** Mon–Sat 10 AM – 7 PM; Sundays and holidays 10 AM – 2 PM.
- **Closed** December 25 and January 1 and 6.

## The Plateresque façade and the frog

Before going in, spend 15–20 minutes in the Patio de Escuelas Mayores on the façade, carved in 1512. It reads in three bands:

- **Lower:** the central medallion of the Catholic Monarchs.
- **Middle:** Emperor Charles V and the imperial coats of arms.
- **Upper:** the virtues, doctors of the Church, popes and prelates.

Then there's the **frog on a skull**. Whoever finds it without help passes their exam, gets married or returns to Salamanca, depending on who you ask. We won't say where it is; only that it's smaller and higher up than you think. Give it 15 minutes with your nose close to the stone; if you still can't see it, ask whoever is searching next to you. The secret gets shared.

## What to see inside the Edificio Histórico

- **The cloister:** two floors with 23 arches each, the upper ones Renaissance. The classrooms open off it.
- **Aula de Fray Luis de León:** the 16th-century classroom as it was, with knife-carved benches, a worn floor and the pulpit. After five years in prison for translating the Song of Songs into Spanish, Fray Luis returned here, opened the book and said: "As we were saying yesterday..."
- **Aula de Unamuno and the Paraninfo:** the ceremonial hall, with 16th-century Flemish tapestries and a coffered ceiling. Here in 1936 Unamuno told General Millán-Astray "You will conquer but you will not convince", which cost him the rectorship and, weeks later, his life.
- **The chapel:** 18th century, with a 17th-century organ still played on some solemn Sundays.
- **The Historic Library** (1254, the oldest university library in Europe), with incunabula and unique manuscripts. **You only see it from a staircase, through glass: you can't go in.** The view of centuries-old wooden shelves is still worth it.
- **The vítores:** red inscriptions reading "VICTOR" with a name and date on the patio and outside walls. When a student earned a doctorate, after a 24-hour exam, their name was painted in bull's blood mixed with oil. There's one for Calderón de la Barca.

## El Cielo de Salamanca (free, don't miss it)

One minute away in the Escuelas Menores, free and little known: the surviving fragment of the old library ceiling, painted in tempera by Fernando Gallego in the 15th century. It shows the night sky with zodiac constellations and mythological creatures, a medieval cosmological map unique in Spain. Allow 15–20 minutes.

## The atmosphere

The University is the living institution that gives Salamanca its pulse: 30,000 students in a small city, tunas (student music groups) playing in Plaza Mayor most afternoons and evenings, Erasmus students from every continent, and cafés near Anaya full until late. Visit during term (October–June) if you can: in August the university is closed and the city loses its life.

## How much time and how to visit

Allow 1.5–2 hours for the Edificio Histórico alone, **2.5 hours** with the Escuelas Menores and the Cielo de Salamanca (the right amount), and 3 hours adding the Casa-Museo Unamuno.

- **Self-guided (€10):** what most people do. It works if you arrive with some context: read ten minutes beforehand about Fray Luis, Unamuno, the Catholic Monarchs and medieval doctorates and the visit changes completely.
- **Official guided tour** (book at the ticket office or online, 1h–1h 30min): best if you know little of the history, are with teenagers, or want the anecdotes that aren't on the panels.
- **Free tours don't go inside.** The night-time free tours only walk around the façade.

## Common visitor mistakes

1. **Seeing only the Edificio Histórico** and missing the free Cielo de Salamanca and Patio de Escuelas Menores, a minute away.
2. **Going on Monday morning expecting free entry.** It's free in the afternoon.
3. **Rushing past the panels.** Every room has its story: Fray Luis, Unamuno, the doctorates.
