## Cádiz cruise terminal: where the ship docks

Cruise ships berth at the quays of the Cádiz-Ciudad dock (Reina Sofía, Reina Victoria, Marqués de Comillas, Ciudad and Alfonso XIII). There are two terminals, both with tourist information, shops and free wifi:

- **Ciudad quay:** opens straight onto Paseo de Canalejas and **Plaza de San Juan de Dios**.
- **Alfonso XIII quay:** the larger one, a few minutes' walk from the same spot.

From either, the centre is about a **5-minute walk**. The train station is about **900 metres** away (11 minutes) and the bus station 1 km.

**Getting around:** on foot, as the old town is flat and compact. **City bus 1** (stop in Plaza de España, 300 metres away) goes to La Victoria beach; there are **taxi** ranks less than 5 minutes from the port; the **train** goes to Jerez and Seville; and the **catamaran** crosses to El Puerto de Santa María in 30 minutes.

## Things to do in Cádiz from the cruise ship

| Time ashore | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| **4 hours** | Cathedral and Clock Tower, El Pópulo and the Roman Theatre, Plaza de las Flores and the Central Market, Torre Tavira and its camera obscura. |
| **6 hours** | All of the above, a fried-fish lunch in La Viña and a walk along La Caleta to Santa Catalina Castle. The bay catamaran trip (from €19) also fits in. |
| **8 hours or more** | Seville on a port excursion, or Jerez by train with a bodega visit. |

The guided visit to **Torre Tavira and the Cathedral** (from €38, 2 hours) starts in Plaza de San Juan de Dios, next to the port. If there are several of you, there's a **private walking tour of the old town** from €180 per group (2 hours, 1 or 2 people) or about €300 for 4, meeting opposite the City Hall, five minutes from the ship. More walking-route ideas in [Cádiz in one day](/en/guides/cadiz/cadiz-in-one-day).

## Seville from Cádiz cruise port (and the risk of missing the ship)

Seville is **about 90 minutes away on the AP-4**. Once there you need at least 4 hours for the Cathedral, the Alcázar and Santa Cruz, so the whole day takes 7-8 hours. Three ways to do it:

**1. Cruise-passenger excursion (from €119 per person).** Picks you up and drops you back at the port, lasts 8 hours and includes a guided walk in English through Santa Cruz, the Archive of the Indies, the Cathedral and the Alcázar, in a group of 10 to 18 on a large coach. **Alcázar entry isn't included**: ask the operator for it as soon as you book. The duration adapts to the actual docking time and comes with the operator's return-to-ship guarantee.

[actividad: sevilla-excursion-cruceristas-puerto-cadiz]

**2. Private excursion (from about €600 per group).** A driver just for you, pick-up at the quay (give the ship's name when booking), a 2-hour guided walk and free time; it lasts 8h 30min. For 4 people, about €880. The version with the **Alcázar and Cathedral with an official guide** costs about €598 for 2 and €840 for 4, but **can't be cancelled**.

**3. By train on your own.** About 20 departures a day, from **1h 25min by Alvia to 1h 50min by Media Distancia**, from about €10 each way. It's the cheapest, but the risk is all yours: if the return train is delayed, the ship sails without you. Only with **9-10 hours in port** and a return train that reaches Cádiz **at least 1h 30min before all aboard**. On Sundays, Seville Cathedral doesn't open to visitors until 14:30.

## Jerez from Cádiz by train

Jerez is the easy escape: about **35 minutes by train** (from about €6 each way) from Cádiz station. With 6 hours there's time for a sherry bodega, the centre and the Alcázar of Jerez. We don't sell Jerez excursions from the port: go independently and book the bodega visit on its website.

## Back to the ship, Sundays and common mistakes

- **All aboard:** usually 30 to 60 minutes before departure, in ship's time (check the daily programme). Around Cádiz, it's enough to be at the terminal **1 hour before**; coming back from Seville or Jerez by train, allow **1h 30min of margin** plus the 11-minute walk. Carry your cruise card and ID.
- **Sundays:** the **Central Market** closes and **Cádiz Cathedral** can only be visited in the afternoon; the Roman Theatre and the Cádiz Museum close on Mondays, not Sundays. In Seville, the Cathedral opens on Sundays from 14:30 to 19:00 (Monday to Saturday, 10:45 to 18:00). Many small shops close; bars and restaurants open as normal.

Mistakes we see often:

1. **Going to Seville by train with less than 9 hours in port.** You'll spend more time on the train than in Seville.
2. **Booking the Seville excursion without requesting Alcázar entry.**
3. **Taking a taxi to the centre.** It's a 5-minute walk.
4. **Going to the Central Market on a Sunday.**
5. **Booking the private trip with tickets without checking cancellation.** It's non-refundable.
