The key to Cádiz isn't a monument, it's the **light** and the **sea on all four sides**. Visitors passing through from Seville take away half the city; those who stay for sunset at La Caleta take away the better half.

## The one-day route: what to see in Cádiz hour by hour

| Time | What |
| --- | --- |
| 9:30 | Cathedral and Clock Tower |
| 10:30 | El Pópulo and the Roman Theatre |
| 11:30 | Torre Tavira and its camera obscura |
| 12:30 | Squares and Central Market |
| 14:00 | Fried fish in La Viña |
| 16:30 | La Caleta and the castles at sunset |

- **Cádiz Cathedral**, with its yellow dome facing the Atlantic. Climb the **Clock Tower** (via a ramp) to see the city hemmed in by the sea, and go down to the crypt where **Manuel de Falla** lies.
- **El Pópulo**, the medieval quarter with its three gates, and the **Roman Theatre of Gades**, one of the largest in Hispania.
- **Torre Tavira**, the tallest of the watchtowers left by trade with the Americas. Its **camera obscura** projects the city live and is the best way to understand an island bristling with lookout towers.
- **The squares** (San Antonio, Mina with the **Cádiz Museum** and its Phoenician sarcophagi, Topete, las Flores) and the **Central Market**, the oldest in Spain.
- **La Caleta**, the cove between the castles of San Sebastián and Santa Catalina (the "Havana" of *Die Another Day*). Sunset with the La Palma bathhouse and the sun dropping into the Atlantic is the classic Cádiz postcard.

## When to go and how to get there

- **April-June and September-October** are ideal. July and August mean great beach days with crowds. November to January is mild and cheap.
- **Carnival** (February/March): one of the most famous in the world, with chirigotas, comparsas and the Teatro Falla, but the city grinds to a halt.
- **Watch out for the levante:** a day of land wind makes the beach impossible. Check the forecast and have an old-town plan B.
- **Train** from Seville in **1h 40min** (Media Distancia, €16) and from Jerez in **40 minutes**, with the station inside the old town.
- **Getting around:** on foot. The old town is **2 km** end to end.

## Where to eat and stay

Seafood and fried-fish cooking: **pescaíto frito**, **tortillitas de camarones** (shrimp fritters), **cazón en adobo** (marinated dogfish) and, in season, **almadraba bluefin tuna**, with a well-chilled Sanlúcar **manzanilla** or a **fino**. Where: the **La Viña** quarter, birthplace of the Carnival, and the fried-fish stalls of the **Central Market**, where you buy the fish and they fry it for you. Avoid the terraces facing the Cathedral.

If you stay over, sleep in the **old town** (Cathedral, El Pópulo, Plaza de San Antonio): everything is walkable and evenings have real local atmosphere. **La Victoria beach**, in the new town, is handy for an urban beach but far from the charm.

## Tips and common mistakes

1. **Just passing through** and missing sunset at La Caleta.
2. **Staying in the new town** and La Victoria beach.
3. **Not climbing any tower:** the Clock Tower or Torre Tavira explain the city better than any map.
4. **Eating on the front line facing the Cathedral.**
5. **Ignoring the levante.**
6. **Confusing Cádiz with the province:** Jerez (wine and horses), Tarifa (wind, with Africa on the horizon) and the white villages are a separate, wonderful trip if you have more days.
