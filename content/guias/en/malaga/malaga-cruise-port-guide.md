Málaga is one of the few Mediterranean ports where **the cruise terminal sits right next to the old town**. You don't need a ship's tour to see the city: the Alcazaba, the Cathedral and the Picasso Museum are all under half an hour's walk from the gangway. A tour only makes sense if you want to leave Málaga, above all for the Alhambra in Granada.

## Where do cruise ships dock in Málaga?

The port has three cruise terminals, all run by Cruceros Málaga:

| Terminal | Ships | Distance to the centre |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Levante (two terminals, four berths) | Large ships, which means most of them | 2-2.5 km to Plaza de la Marina |
| Palmeral de las Sorpresas (Pier 2) | Small and luxury ships, one berth | About 5 minutes' walk to the Cathedral |

Your daily programme tells you which one. At Levante, La Malagueta beach is on your left as you leave and the harbour promenade straight ahead.

## Getting into town

| Option | Time | Cost | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Walk | 25-30 min | Free | Flat promenade past La Farola, Muelle Uno and the Centre Pompidou |
| Shuttle bus to Plaza de la Marina | About 10 min | Around €5 return | Only runs when a ship is at Levante |
| Taxi | About 10 min | €9.45 minimum from the terminal | Worth it for 3 or 4 people |
| Cruise line shuttle | About 10 min | Set by your cruise line | Check the ship's app to see if it's free |

- **The shuttle** is a bus the port runs between the Levante terminals and the Plaza de la Marina pedestrian entrance whenever a ship is in. Cruise lines quote around €5 return; check the price when you get off.
- **Taxis** use the city meter. From the Levante cruise terminal the minimum fare is **€9.45**; on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays tariff 2 applies (€2.27 flag fall and €1.25 per km).
- **Walking** is the best option in good weather, and the promenade is part of the visit.

## What to see in 4, 6 or 8 hours

| Time ashore | Plan | What to book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 hours | Alcazaba (€7), the Roman Theatre from Calle Alcazabilla, the Cathedral (€10) and Calle Larios; back along Muelle Uno | Guided tour of the Alcazaba and Roman Theatre, from €13 |
| 6 hours | All of the above plus Gibralfaro Castle (€10 combined ticket with the Alcazaba), the Picasso Museum (€13) and tapas in the centre | Essential Málaga guided tour, from €32.76 |
| 8 hours | Granada and the Alhambra on a port-pickup tour, or Málaga at an easy pace with grilled sardines and the beach at La Malagueta | Alhambra shore excursion, from €119 |

The Alcazaba opens 9am-6pm from November to March and until 8pm from April to October, with last entry an hour before closing. The Cathedral opens 10am-8pm Monday to Friday and until 6pm on Saturdays.

## Is the Alhambra worth it from the ship?

Granada is **1 h 30 by road** and the Alhambra takes about 3 hours. With the return journey that's 6 hours at the very least, so don't try it with less than 8 hours in port.

Our **cruise-passenger shore excursion** lasts 8 hours, picks you up and drops you back at the port, has an English-speaking guide and groups of up to 18 inside the Alhambra. It costs **€119 per person**, but **the ticket is not included**: as soon as you book, send the operator each traveller's full name, passport number and date of birth so they can get it for you (€23). Nasrid Palaces slots sell out, so do this weeks ahead.

## Ship's tour, cruise-passenger tour or private?

| Option | Price | If something runs late | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cruise line excursion | Whatever your cruise line charges | The ship waits for its own tours | Anyone who wants zero risk |
| Cruise-passenger tour | €119 + €23 ticket | The operator works to your docking time and includes a back-to-ship guarantee | Couples and solo travellers |
| Private | €397 per person | The driver works to your timetable, but the ship won't wait | Tickets included and your own guide |
| Independent | Shuttle and tickets | All the risk is yours | Málaga itself, not Granada |

The ship only waits if the tour is its own. With anyone else, punctuality is down to the operator, so choose one built for cruise passengers and read what its guarantee covers.

## What time should you be back on board?

- **All aboard** is the latest time to be on the ship, usually 30-60 minutes before sailing. It's printed in the daily programme and given in **ship's time**.
- **Rule of thumb:** on your own, be at the terminal **an hour before all aboard**. If you're relying on the shuttle, catch it 30 minutes earlier still, because there's a queue at the end of the day.
- Take your **cruise card** and photo ID for port security, and note the port agent's phone number from the programme.

## Sundays and public holidays

- **Cathedral:** on Sundays and holy days, visits only from 2pm to 6pm, and not during Mass.
- **Alcazaba and Gibralfaro:** open, and **free on Sundays from 2pm**, so busier in the afternoon.
- **Picasso Museum:** open daily (10am-6pm from November to February, until 7pm or 8pm the rest of the year). Free for the last two hours on Sundays. Closed 25 December and 1 and 6 January.
- **Atarazanas market:** closed on Sundays.
- **Shops:** under the city's Cruise Shops scheme, more than 40 shops in the old town, Soho and La Malagueta open on Sundays and holidays when a cruise ship is in, each setting its own hours.

## Common mistakes

1. **Heading to Granada independently without a ticket.** Without a Nasrid Palaces slot, the trip loses most of its point.
2. **Assuming the ticket is included** in the cruise-passenger tour. You have to request it from the operator.
3. **Waiting for the last shuttle.** Head back with an hour to spare.
4. **Walking up to Gibralfaro at midday in summer.** It's a steep climb: take a taxi up and walk down.
5. **Planning the Cathedral for a Sunday morning.** It doesn't open to visitors until 2pm.
