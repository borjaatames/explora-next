The Picasso Museum Málaga, in the Palacio de Buenavista on Calle San Agustín, is one of the city's must-sees. At Easter, in summer and on days with several cruise ships in port, some time slots can sell out online. Unlike other in-demand museums, though, the official site leaves a door open here: the box office. These are your options, in the order worth trying them.

## 1. The official website: prices and hours

Official sales are at `tickets.museopicassomalaga.org`, linked from `museopicassomalaga.org`.

- **Single ticket: €13.** Collection and temporary exhibitions, with an **audio guide** in a web app that runs in your phone's browser, no download needed.
- **Reduced: €11.** Over-65s, European Youth Card holders and students under 26.
- **Free:** under-17s, disabled visitors plus one companion, people registered as unemployed in Spain, University of Málaga students, teachers, ICOM members and journalists.

The museum opens **every day from 10am**, and closing time changes with the season:

| Months | Hours | Free on Sundays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| November to February | 10am-6pm | 4pm-6pm |
| March to June | 10am-7pm | 5pm-7pm |
| July and August | 10am-8pm | 6pm-8pm |
| September and October | 10am-7pm | 5pm-7pm |

Last entry is **30 minutes before closing**. Special dates: on 24 and 31 December and 5 January it opens 10am-3pm; it's closed on 25 December and 1 and 6 January.

If your slot is sold out, check others the same day. With the audio guide the visit takes an hour and a half to two hours, so moving to first thing or mid-afternoon won't wreck your day.

## 2. The box office

This is the big difference from other popular museums: the Picasso Museum's website says explicitly that **if online tickets are sold out, you can also buy them at the box office at quiet times**. Your best bet is to turn up at opening (10am) or in the last hour and a half before closing. On the busiest days, don't count on it.

## 3. Free Sundays and free days

Entry is free **every Sunday for the last two opening hours** (see the table above for each month). The box office stops selling **half an hour before closing**, so in November, for example, you need to get in between 4pm and 5:30pm.

It's also free all day on **28 February** (Andalusia Day), **18 May** (International Museum Day), **27 September** (World Tourism Day) and **27 October** (the museum's anniversary).

Two hours on a Sunday afternoon cover the core of the collection, not the temporary exhibitions at leisure. And it's the busiest time of the week.

## 4. A guided visit with entry included

If you want a guaranteed visit and to understand Picasso in his home town, the [Picasso route in Málaga](/en/cities/malaga/activities/picasso-route-malaga-private-tour) is a two-and-a-half-hour private tour: the church of Santiago, where he was baptised, the **Picasso Birthplace Museum** and the **Picasso Museum**, with both tickets included. It costs from €166 with free cancellation up to 24 hours before.

## 5. If you can't get in: nearby alternatives

- **Picasso Birthplace Museum** on Plaza de la Merced, about 5 minutes' walk: the house where the painter was born in 1881.
- **Centre Pompidou Málaga**, the "Cube" at the port: around 70 works from the Pompidou in Paris, Picasso among them. Entry from €9.
- **The Alcazaba and Roman Theatre**, three minutes from the museum: if your day suddenly frees up, it's the other essential sight in the centre.

## When it sells out and how far ahead to book

The official site doesn't publish a booking window. This is our advice:

- **Easter, July, August and cruise days:** book a few days ahead and go for the first slot.
- **Sunday afternoons:** crowds build for the free hours; if you don't mind paying €13, go in the morning.
- **Weekdays from November to February:** rarely a problem; the box office is usually enough.

## Common mistakes

1. **Arriving in the last half hour on Sunday for free entry.** The box office has already closed: it stops selling 30 minutes before closing.
2. **Using summer hours in winter.** From November to February it closes at 6pm, not 7pm.
3. **Going on 1 or 6 January or Christmas Day.** It's closed.
4. **Mixing up the Picasso Museum and the Birthplace Museum.** They're two separate museums with separate tickets, five minutes apart.
5. **Buying from sites that look like the official one.** The official shop is `tickets.museopicassomalaga.org`.

## In a nutshell

If the Picasso Museum Málaga is sold out, try another time on the official site or the box office at a quiet moment; Sundays are free for the last two hours, and for a no-risk visit the private Picasso route includes entry.
