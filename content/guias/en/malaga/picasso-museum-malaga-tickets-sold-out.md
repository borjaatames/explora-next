## Picasso Museum Malaga tickets: price

Official sales are at **tickets.museopicassomalaga.org**, linked from museopicassomalaga.org.

- **Single ticket: €13.** Collection and temporary exhibitions, with the **audio guide** in a web app that opens in your phone's browser, no download needed.
- **Reduced: €11.** Over-65s, European Youth Card holders and students under 26.
- **Free:** under-17s, people with disabilities plus one companion, jobseekers registered with SEPE, University of Málaga students, teachers, ICOM members and journalists.

Beware of websites imitating the official one: the real one is tickets.museopicassomalaga.org.

## Picasso Museum Malaga opening hours

Open **every day from 10:00**; closing time changes with the season:

| Months | Hours | Free on Sundays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| November to February | 10:00–18:00 | 16:00–18:00 |
| March to June | 10:00–19:00 | 17:00–19:00 |
| July and August | 10:00–20:00 | 18:00–20:00 |
| September and October | 10:00–19:00 | 17:00–19:00 |

Last entry is **30 minutes before closing**. On 24 and 31 December and 5 January it opens 10:00 to 15:00; it is **closed on 25 December and 1 and 6 January**.

## If tickets are sold out

At Easter, in summer and on days with several cruise ships in port, online sales can sell out for some time slots. Try these in order:

1. **Another time the same day.** The visit takes an hour and a half to two hours, so moving to first thing or mid-afternoon won't wreck your day.
2. **The ticket office.** This is the big difference from other in-demand museums: the official website says explicitly that **if online tickets are sold out, they can also be bought at the ticket office at quiet times**. Your best bet is to arrive at 10:00 or in the last hour and a half before closing. On the busiest days, don't count on it.
3. **A guided visit with the ticket included.** The private Picasso route lasts two and a half hours: the church of Santiago, where he was baptised, the **Picasso Birthplace Museum** and the **Picasso Museum**, with both tickets included. From €166, with free cancellation up to 24 hours before.

## Picasso Museum Malaga free: Sundays and special days

It's free **every Sunday for the last two hours** (see the table by month). Since the ticket office stops selling **half an hour before closing**, in November, for example, the useful window to get in is 16:00 to 17:30.

It is also free all day on **28 February** (Andalusia Day), **18 May** (International Museum Day), **27 September** (World Tourism Day) and **27 October** (the museum's anniversary).

Two hours on a Sunday afternoon is enough for the highlights of the collection, not for the temporary shows at leisure. And it's the busiest time of the week.

## When it sells out and how far ahead to buy

The official website doesn't publish a sales window. Our advice:

- **Easter, July, August and cruise days:** buy a few days ahead and pick first thing.
- **Sunday afternoons:** crowds build for the free slot; if you don't mind paying €13, go in the morning.
- **Weekdays from November to February:** rarely a problem; the ticket office is usually enough.

## Nearby alternatives and tips

If you get nothing:

- **Picasso Birthplace Museum** on Plaza de la Merced, about 5 minutes' walk: the house where the painter was born in 1881. Note it's a different museum with a different ticket.
- **Centre Pompidou Málaga**, the "Cube" in the port: around 70 works from the Pompidou in Paris, Picasso among them. From €9.
- **The Alcazaba and the Roman Theatre**, 3 minutes away: the other must-see of the centre.

Mistakes we often see: turning up on Sunday in the last half hour (the ticket office has stopped selling), using summer closing times in winter (November to February it closes at 18:00) and going on 1 or 6 January or Christmas Day, when it's closed. To fit it in with the rest of the city, see [Málaga in two days](/en/guides/malaga/malaga-in-two-days).
