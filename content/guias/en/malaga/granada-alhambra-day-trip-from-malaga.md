## What you'll see at the Alhambra

The complex has four areas, all included in the General ticket:

- **The Alcazaba**, the military section and the oldest part.
- **The Nasrid Palaces**, with the Mexuar, the Comares Palace and the Court of the Lions. Entry is **at a fixed time** printed on your ticket.
- **The Generalife**, the leisure estate with its gardens and water channels.
- **The Palace of Charles V**, a Renaissance building put up after the Catholic Monarchs took Granada in 1492.

Allow **about 3 hours** to see it all.

The General daytime ticket costs **€22.27**. In high season it sells out **weeks ahead**: buy it before you book anything else.

## Granada from Málaga in a day: the independent plan

1. **Buy your ticket first** on the official website and pick a Nasrid Palaces time around midday so you arrive with time to spare.
2. **Leave Málaga at 8:00:** you'll reach Granada around 9:30.
3. **Go up to the Alhambra** by C30 or C32 minibus from Plaza Isabel la Católica (€1.60, every 8 minutes), by taxi from the centre (€8-10) or on foot up the Cuesta de Gomérez, about 20 minutes.
4. **Alhambra:** about 3 hours (Alcazaba, Nasrid Palaces, Palace of Charles V and Generalife).
5. **Have lunch and head down to the Albaicín** to see the Alhambra from the San Nicolás viewpoint.
6. **Head back mid-afternoon** so you don't arrive after dark.

## Alhambra day trip from Málaga: the private option

It lasts **about 9 hours**: pick-up in Málaga, a **guided visit of about 3 hours** at the Alhambra with the general ticket (Nasrid Palaces, Alcazaba and Generalife), free time for lunch (not included) and a **guided walk through the Albaicín**. If you're staying overnight in Granada, you can finish there. It's the easiest option if you don't want to battle for tickets or drive.

Guided tours and day trips usually have **their own allocation** even when the official website is sold out, and the **night visit** to the Nasrid Palaces usually has more availability.
