**Málaga is easy to see in a day, as long as you do it in the right order.** The old town is small and flat, and everything that matters (the Alcazaba, Gibralfaro, the Cathedral and the Picasso Museum) sits within 2 km. Most people get it backwards: they arrive at 11:00, start with the Cathedral and climb to Gibralfaro in the midday sun. This is the route we would give a friend with one day and no time for trips out of town. With two days, see [Málaga in two days](/en/guides/malaga/malaga-in-two-days).

## One day in Málaga, hour by hour

| Time | What | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9:00 | Alcazaba | €7 (€10 with Gibralfaro) |
| 10:15 | Walk up to Gibralfaro castle | Included in combined ticket |
| 11:30 | Roman Theatre | Free |
| 12:00 | Cathedral | €10 with audio guide |
| 13:30 | Atarazanas market and lunch | Pay as you go |
| 15:30 | Picasso Museum and Plaza de la Merced | €13 |
| 17:30 | Muelle Uno and Paseo del Parque | Free |
| 18:30 | Sunset and sardines at La Malagueta | €10-15 |

These are winter times (November to March). In summer sunset comes around 21:30: take a long lunch and leave the Picasso for later in the afternoon, when the heat drops.

## The best things to do in Málaga in a day

**The Alcazaba (1 hr 15).** A Moorish palace-fortress climbing the hillside in terraces of courtyards, fountains and gardens, and the best preserved of its kind in Spain. It opens at 9:00, when it is close to empty. **€7, or €10 including Gibralfaro**, on the official site, [alcazabaygibralfaro.malaga.eu](https://alcazabaygibralfaro.malaga.eu). For context rather than just walls, the [guided tour of the Alcazaba and Roman Theatre](/en/cities/malaga/activities/alcazaba-roman-theatre-malaga-guided-tour) starts at €13 with entry included.

**Gibralfaro (45 min).** A 20 to 30 minute climb along the walls with no shade, or bus 35 if you would rather not. There is not much inside the castle; you are paying for the view over the port, the bullring, the beach and the whole bay. With the combined ticket, it is an easy yes.

**The Roman Theatre (20 min).** Right below the Alcazaba, visible from Calle Alcazabilla. The small visitor centre is free, open Tuesday to Saturday from 10:00 to 18:00 and Sunday until 16:00, and **closed on Mondays**.

**Málaga Cathedral (1 hr).** Locals call it *La Manquita*, "the one-armed lady", because its second tower was never built. **€10 with an audio guide**; open weekdays 10:00 to 20:00, Saturdays until 18:00 and **Sundays only from 14:00 to 18:00**. The **rooftop tour is suspended** until the roof works finish, expected at the end of 2027. The [skip-the-line guided tour](/en/cities/malaga/activities/malaga-cathedral-skip-the-line-guided-tour) starts at €24, and the [Cathedral, Alcazaba and Roman Theatre combo](/en/cities/malaga/activities/malaga-essentials-cathedral-alcazaba-theatre) covers the whole morning with tickets.

**The Picasso Museum (1 hr 30).** Over 200 works in the Buenavista Palace, a few minutes from the house where Picasso was born. **€13**, free for under-17s and for everyone on Sundays in the last two hours. Open 10:00 to 18:00 from November to February, until 19:00 from March to June and in September and October, and until 20:00 in July and August. Book a [timed ticket with audio guide](/en/cities/malaga/activities/picasso-museum-malaga-ticket); if it is sold out, read [what to do when the Picasso Museum is sold out](/en/guides/malaga/picasso-museum-malaga-tickets-sold-out).

**Muelle Uno and La Malagueta.** The old port turned into a waterfront promenade, with the Centre Pompidou's glass cube at the end, and the city beach ten minutes further on. No tickets, no opening hours: it is where the day ends. To see the sunset from the water, the [catamaran cruise around the bay](/en/cities/malaga/activities/malaga-catamaran-sunset-cruise) starts at €13.

**What we would skip with only one day:** the Pompidou, the Carmen Thyssen and Picasso's birthplace. All good, but they only fit if you drop something above.

## On a cruise stop

Málaga is one of the easiest cruise ports in Spain: the centre is walkable from the ship. With 6 to 8 hours, follow the route up to the Picasso and walk back along Muelle Uno. With 4, stick to the Alcazaba, the Roman Theatre and the Cathedral. Terminal distances and shuttle prices are in our [Málaga cruise port guide](/en/guides/malaga/malaga-cruise-port-guide).

## Where to eat

- **Atarazanas market:** fried fish, anchovies and marinated olives at the stalls. Mornings only (until about 15:00) and **closed on Sundays**.
- **Around Plaza de la Merced and Calle Carretería:** standing-room tapas bars at local prices, ten minutes from the Picasso.
- **La Malagueta, in the evening:** *espetos*, sardines grilled on a skewer over a wood fire in a beach bar. Simple, and the most Málaga thing you will eat.

Skip the laminated-menu terraces facing the Cathedral and on Calle Larios. More in [eating in Málaga](/en/guides/malaga/eating-in-malaga), or let someone take you round on the [private tapas tour of Atarazanas](/en/cities/malaga/activities/private-tapas-tour-malaga-atarazanas).

## Common mistakes

1. **Climbing Gibralfaro at noon in summer.** Go first thing, or take bus 35.
2. **Planning the Cathedral for a Sunday morning.** It only opens to visitors at 14:00.
3. **Visiting the Roman Theatre on a Monday.** You can see it from the street, but the visitor centre is shut.
4. **Counting on the Cathedral rooftops.** Not until the works are done.
5. **Squeezing in the Caminito del Rey or Ronda.** It does not fit in the same day: see [day trips from Málaga](/en/guides/malaga/day-trips-from-malaga) instead.

## In one sentence

The best way to spend one day in Málaga is the Alcazaba early, the Cathedral at midday, Picasso in the afternoon and the sea at the end: in that order you see almost everything that matters without taking a single taxi.
