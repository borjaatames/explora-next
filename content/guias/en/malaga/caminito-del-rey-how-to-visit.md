## What you'll see

The Caminito is a **one-way** route of about 7.7 km through the **Gaitanes gorge**: around **3 km of walkways** anchored to the rock, more than 100 metres above the river, plus access paths. You walk it in one direction only, **from north (Ardales) to south (El Chorro)**.

It was built for workers at the hydroelectric plant in the early 20th century and inaugurated by King Alfonso XIII, hence the name. It reopened in 2015 with a new walkway, now completely safe and with a helmet included.

**Difficulty:** physically easy (hardly any climbing and the walkways are flat), but **not for anyone with vertigo**: some stretches have a sheer drop beneath your feet, always protected.

## Caminito del Rey tickets and prices

The golden rule: **no ticket, no entry**, and tickets sell out weeks or months ahead. If your trip revolves around the Caminito, book it before your accommodation and **plan your trip around that date**, not the other way round.

- **General ticket:** **€10** (maximum 10 per purchase), with a fixed entry time, on the official website caminitodelrey.info.
- **Official guided visit:** **€18**, groups of up to 30, in Spanish and English.
- **Shuttle bus** between the south (El Chorro) and north (Ardales) entrances: **€2.50**; best bought with your ticket.
- **Parking:** €2 a day.
- **Day trip from Málaga** with transport and ticket: from €72.
- **Capacity:** up to 1,500 people a day. Tickets go on sale by semester; spring and autumn dates sell out in days, high summer and winter weekdays less so.

## Opening hours, ages and rules

- **Opening hours:** Tuesday to Sunday, with entry slots every half hour from **9:00 to 14:50 or 17:20**, depending on the season. **Closed on Mondays** (except a few it opens as an exception: check the booking calendar) and on days of strong wind or heavy rain; you can then move your ticket to another date from your personal account, subject to availability.
- **Minimum age: 8**, no exceptions (bring ID or a family record book), and minors must always be accompanied. There's no upper limit, but it's 7.7 km with no benches or shade.
- **Total time:** 3-4 hours including the access paths (1.5-2.7 km from each car park, depending on the entrance) and the shuttle.
- **Helmet compulsory** (provided on site) and **closed shoes**. Umbrellas, metal-tipped poles, drones and pets are not allowed.
- **Toilets:** only at the entrances, not along the route.

## Getting to the Caminito del Rey from Málaga

**By train.** Renfe to **El Chorro–Caminito del Rey**: about 50 minutes, from around €5 each way. From the station, the **shuttle bus** connects with the north entrance, where the route starts. Allow the whole day.

**By car.** 59 km, just over 50 minutes, to the Ardales/El Chorro area. Park at the **north entrance car park** and, when you finish in the south, take the shuttle back to your car; it runs every 30 minutes from 8:30 to 16:30 from the south side.

As the route is one-way, **the shuttle is essential**: buy it with your ticket.

## What to bring and tips

- **Water and a snack:** there are no shops inside.
- **Cap, sunglasses and sunscreen in summer:** much of it is in full sun.
- **Keep your phone secure:** plenty of people drop theirs into the gorge.
- **Check the wind forecast:** it closes in strong wind; have a plan B.
- **Don't go with serious vertigo:** however safe it is, you'll have a bad time.
- **Allow plenty of time:** between access, route and shuttle, a long half-day.

It's a **full-day plan** within a two- or three-day break; we suggest it as day 2 in [Málaga in two days](/en/guides/malaga/malaga-in-two-days). Afterwards, it's worth having lunch in **El Chorro** or **Álora** and seeing the area's turquoise reservoirs.
