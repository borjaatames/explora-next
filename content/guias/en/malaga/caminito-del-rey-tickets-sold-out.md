## 1. Check the official website again

The official website is caminitodelrey.info. There's **no trickle of cancellations** here, because tickets can't be changed or refunded, but before you give up on your date:

- **Check both ticket types.** The **general ticket** costs **€10** and the **official guided visit** **€18** (groups of up to 30, in Spanish and English). Each has its own time-slot calendar.
- **Check every time slot.** Tickets have a fixed entry time, from 9:00, and they don't all sell out at once.
- **Check when new dates are released.** Tickets go on sale in blocks. In October 2026 the website says dates after 2 May will be released later: if you're travelling after that, buy as soon as they open.
- **Check Mondays.** The Caminito opens Tuesday to Sunday all year (except 24, 25 and 31 December and 1 January), and on some Mondays too.
- **Buy the shuttle bus with your ticket** (€2.50): the route is one-way and you need it to get back.

**Spring and autumn** dates are the first to go.

## 2. Caminito del Rey box office: the north entrance

The official website says you can also buy at the **north entrance box office**, "subject to availability". It gives no detail on how many tickets are sold there, and in high season you'll usually arrive to find nothing left. The Visitor Reception Centre **does not sell tickets**.

If you want to try, do it on a **weekday out of season** and with a back-up plan.

## 3. No tickets? A ticket with a guide, or a tour that includes it

This is the safest option when the official website has nothing: operators book their places in advance and sell you the visit with the ticket already included. You pay more than on the official website, but you get in.

- **Ticket with an official guide, no transport (from €30):** if you're driving or taking the train to El Chorro.
- **Tour with transport from Málaga (from €72):** return coach, ticket and guide. The easy option if you don't have a car.

Before paying, check the listing clearly says it **includes Caminito entry** and buy on a well-known platform: the official website warns that it doesn't guarantee tickets bought outside authorised sales points.

## 4. If there's no way in: what to do instead

Without a ticket you can't get through access control. What you can do:

- **Visit the reservoirs** around Ardales and El Chorro, with the turquoise water you see in every photo, and have lunch in El Chorro or Álora.
- **Switch trips.** From Málaga, **El Torcal de Antequera** is the province's other great rock landscape, and it pairs with the **Antequera dolmens**, a World Heritage Site.
- **Ronda**, with its Puente Nuevo over the gorge, is the alternative if what drew you was the drop of the gorge; it's often combined with Setenil de las Bodegas.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Buying on websites that imitate the official one.** The official one is caminitodelrey.info; outside authorised channels your ticket isn't guaranteed.
2. **Booking the trip first and looking for tickets afterwards.** Caminito date first, everything else after.
3. **Forgetting ID.** Each ticket must be personalised before downloading and official ID is checked at the entrance, children included.
4. **Bringing children under 8.** They can't enter, not even with a resale ticket.

Prices, opening hours, getting there and what to bring are in [how to visit the Caminito del Rey](/en/guides/malaga/caminito-del-rey-how-to-visit).
