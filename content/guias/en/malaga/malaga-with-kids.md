## What to do in Málaga with kids

The key is to alternate: **a morning of castle or old town in short doses and an afternoon of beach or animals**. That way Málaga goes a long way without stress.

- **Beaches.** **La Malagueta** is at the foot of the centre; **Pedregalejo** and **El Palo** are quieter, with smaller waves and beach bars grilling espetos (sardine skewers). Sand, promenade and a swim: the star plan.
- **Alcazaba and Gibralfaro.** Kids love exploring the **fortress** and climbing to the **castle** along the walls. Sell it as an explorers' adventure; there is a lift up to the Alcazaba if the heat is on.
- **Museums for little ones.** **Museo Alborania** (sea and boats), **Principia** (hands-on science) and the **OXO Video Game Museum** work very well from age 5-6.

## Bioparc Fuengirola

An immersion zoo with no visible bars (gorillas, tigers, Madagascar) that ranks among the best in Spain. Allow **half a day**.

- **Price:** €29.50 adults and **€23 children aged 3 to 9** (2026); under-3s free.
- **Getting there:** C-1 local train from María Zambrano or Centro-Alameda to Fuengirola (**45 minutes, €3.60**) and a 10-minute walk.
- **Hours:** opens at 10:00 all year. In July and August it closes at 23:00, so an evening visit beats the heat.
- It fills up on long weekends and in summer: book ahead.

## Benalmádena cable car

It climbs Mount Calamorro with spectacular views and, at the top, **birds of prey shows** at set times (check them when you buy).

- **Price:** return from **€12.90 adults and €9.90 children** online; €4-5 more at the ticket office.
- **Getting there:** local train to Arroyo de la Miel and a 10-minute walk.

## Prices, hours and getting around as a family

- **Alcazaba and Gibralfaro:** combined ticket **€10** (€7 each separately; reduced €5 and €3) and **free for everyone on Sundays from 14:00**. **Bus 35** goes up to Gibralfaro castle from the Alameda (every 45 minutes); walking down along Paseo de Don Juan Temboury is a pleasant 20 minutes with views.
- **Beaches:** La Malagueta has lifeguards, showers, a kids' area and beach bars. The water is 18 °C in May and 24 °C in August.
- **Getting around:** the centre is pedestrian and flat, good for pushchairs. EMT buses cost €1.40 and under-4s ride free. The local train reaches the airport in 12 minutes.

**When to come:** March to June and September-October are ideal (good weather, pleasant water and fewer people in autumn). Winter is one of the mildest in Spain. In July and August, beach yes, but **from 14:00 to 18:00 the centre is an oven** for small children: beach or museum at those hours.

## Málaga with family: a weekend plan

- **Day 1.** Morning at the Alcazaba and Gibralfaro; afternoon on La Malagueta beach.
- **Day 2.** Morning at Bioparc Fuengirola; afternoon in Pedregalejo with espetos.
- **Extra day.** Benalmádena cable car or an interactive museum.

**Where to eat:** the **beach bars** (chiringuitos) of Pedregalejo and El Palo are ideal: space, sardine skewers and fried fish that kids happily eat. In the centre, **Atarazanas Market** for nibbles and the taverns on Calle Carretería. Skip the tourist terraces facing the Cathedral.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Caminito del Rey, only from age 8.** Younger children are not allowed and it runs along a cliff edge. For older kids, see [how to visit the Caminito del Rey](/en/guides/malaga/caminito-del-rey-how-to-visit).
- **Don't chain art museums.** The Picasso or the Pompidou tire little ones; keep visits short.
- **Don't walk up to Gibralfaro at midday in summer:** the slope has no shade. Take bus 35.
- **Cap, water and sunscreen** always: the Costa del Sol sun is unforgiving.
- **Don't count on Tívoli World:** the Benalmádena theme park has been closed for years.
