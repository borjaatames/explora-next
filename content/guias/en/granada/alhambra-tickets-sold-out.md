## 1. Last-minute tickets on the official site

The official ticket site is tickets.alhambra-patronato.es. The general ticket costs **€22.27** and includes the Nasrid Palaces with a fixed time, the Alcazaba, the Partal and the Generalife.

- **The evening before, between 20:00 and 22:00:** according to Turismo de Granada, last-minute tickets for the next day can appear at that time.
- **Check several days in a row:** cancellations and group returns are released.
- **Be flexible on your Nasrid Palaces time:** the first morning and last afternoon slots go first; midday ones last a bit longer.
- **Ticket office on the day:** sometimes a few are left, but there's no guarantee. It's not a plan.

## 2. The Granada Card and the Alhambra

The **Granada Card** keeps its own quota of Alhambra tickets, so it may have availability when the official site doesn't.

- The **48- and 72-hour** cards include the general ticket.
- The **24-hour** card includes the Nasrid Palaces night visit.
- Check availability on the Granadatur website or by phone.

It's worth it if you're also visiting the cathedral, the Royal Chapel and other monuments.

## 3. A guided tour with the ticket included

It's the safest option when everything else fails: authorised operators book their allocations in advance and sell you the tour with the Nasrid Palaces time already assigned. It costs more than the ticket alone (from about €50), but saves you the search and someone explains what you're seeing.

Before paying, check the listing clearly says **"includes Nasrid Palaces"** and that the ticket is included. Some tours only cover the gardens or the free area.

## 4. The night visit or the gardens

If there's nothing left for daytime, look at these two official tickets, which usually sell out later:

- **Nasrid Palaces night visit (€12.73):** the illuminated palaces, without the Alcazaba or Generalife. It lasts an hour and a half and is a very different, calmer experience.
- **Gardens, Generalife and Alcazaba (€12.73):** everything except the Nasrid Palaces. That's what you miss most, but the Generalife and the Alcazaba are worth the visit.

## 5. What you can see of the Alhambra without a ticket

Even if you get nothing, don't leave Granada without going up the hill:

- The **Alhambra woods** and the Cuesta de Gomérez, from Plaza Nueva.
- The **Puerta de la Justicia** and the **Puerta del Vino**.
- The **Palace of Charles V**, free to enter.
- The **Mirador de San Nicolás**, in the Albaicín, with the best view of the whole Alhambra.

With the official **Andalusian Monuments** ticket (€8.48) you can visit other Nasrid sites in the Albaicín, such as El Bañuelo or the Dar al-Horra Palace.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Buying on sites that imitate the official one.** They add surcharges and sometimes sell tickets that don't work.
2. **Booking a tour without checking it includes the Nasrid Palaces.** It's the most expensive mistake: you pay for a guide and don't get into the palaces.
3. **Being late for your Nasrid Palaces time.** You have a 30-minute window, and walking from the site entrance takes 30 to 45 minutes.
4. **Forgetting your ID.** Tickets are in your name and checked at the entrance. More in [our guide to Alhambra ticket names and ID](/en/guides/granada/alhambra-tickets-name-passport-id).

Prices, opening hours and what to see inside are in [how to visit the Alhambra](/en/guides/granada/alhambra-how-to-visit). For the rest of the city, a two-day route fits the Alhambra together with the Albaicín, Sacromonte and the Cartuja.
