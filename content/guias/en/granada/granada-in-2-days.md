## First: the Alhambra ticket

Buy it on the official website, tickets.alhambra-patronato.es: **€22.27**, with a **fixed time for the Nasrid Palaces** and only half an hour's margin; arrive later and you lose the Nasrid Palaces.

- Tickets go on sale up to **three months ahead** and sell out about three weeks before in spring and autumn.
- **Under-12s are free**, but need their own ticket. ID or passport is checked.
- **If it's sold out:** the Nasrid Palaces night visit (€12.73) or a guided tour with its own quota. Never buy from resellers.

More detail in [how to visit the Alhambra](/en/guides/granada/alhambra-how-to-visit).

## Day 1: Alhambra, Albaicín and Sacromonte

| Time | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| 8:30-12:30 | The full Alhambra |
| 12:30-14:00 | Down the Cuesta de Gomérez (15 min) or bus C30 |
| 14:00-16:00 | Tapas on Navas or Elvira |
| 16:00-19:00 | Carrera del Darro, Bañuelo and up to the Albaicín |
| 19:30-21:00 | Sunset at San Nicolás or San Cristóbal |
| 21:30-23:30 | Zambra in the Sacromonte |

**Alhambra.** Take the first slot, with the Nasrid Palaces between 9:00 and 9:30, and go against the flow of the groups: **Alcazaba** and Torre de la Vela (45 min), the **Nasrid Palaces**, arriving 15 minutes before your time (90 min; what look like drawings on the walls are poems), and the **Generalife** (60 min). In total, 3.5 to 4 hours.

**Tapas.** Three beers make a meal. Los Diamantes (Navas 28) for fried fish; Casa Julio (Hermosa 5) for anchovies; La Riviera (Elvira 30) for more elaborate tapas. Arrive before 14:00 or after 15:30.

**Carrera del Darro and Albaicín.** Granada's most beautiful street, with the river on one side and the Alhambra above; stop at the **Bañuelo** (11th-century Arab baths, €5). Climb to the Albaicín on foot via the Cuesta del Chapiz (25 minutes uphill) or on the **C32 minibus** from Plaza Nueva.

**Sunset.** **San Nicolás** is the postcard view: get there 45 minutes early. If the crowd spoils it, **San Cristóbal**, 10 minutes further up, has half the people.

**Zambra.** The flamenco of the Sacromonte caves, intimate and rooted in the gypsy wedding. Cueva de la Rocío (€28-30 with a drink), Los Tarantos (€28) or María la Canastera (€22-26). Book ahead, as weekends sell out, and take a taxi back (€5-7). To choose, see [the cave comparison](/en/guides/granada/flamenco-cave-sacromonte-which-to-choose).

## Day 2: historic centre and Cartuja

| Time | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| 9:30-11:30 | Royal Chapel and Cathedral |
| 11:30-13:00 | Alcaicería, Bib-Rambla, Madraza and Corral del Carbón |
| 14:00-16:00 | Lunch in the Realejo |
| 16:30-18:00 | Cartuja Monastery |
| 18:30-23:00 | Stroll and dinner |

**Royal Chapel** (€7, 60 minutes): the tombs of the Catholic Monarchs, Joanna the Mad and Philip the Handsome, Isabella's crown and sceptre, and panels by Memling and Botticelli. Next door, the white, light-filled Renaissance **Cathedral**: €7 (€10 until 28 November 2026, for the Kerygma exhibition), with a combined ticket available. Tuesdays are busier because Granada residents get in free.

**Alcaicería, Bib-Rambla, Madraza and Corral del Carbón.** The Alcaicería is pure souvenirs; in Bib-Rambla, have a beer but don't eat. The **Madraza** (Nasrid oratory) and the **Corral del Carbón** (Spain's only surviving Nasrid warehouse) are free and take 15 minutes each.

**Realejo.** The old Jewish quarter, cheaper and better for lunch than the centre: Calle Molinos and Campo del Príncipe. Order plato alpujarreño, remojón or tortilla del Sacromonte at Bodegas Castañeda or Taberna La Tana.

**Cartuja.** The stop 80% of visitors skip because it's outside the centre, and the highlight of the day: its 1742 sacristy is a peak of Spanish Baroque. Bus 8 or U3 from Gran Vía (15 minutes); €7, 60 minutes. **It closes at midday on Saturdays.**

**Dinner.** Damasqueros (Realejo, set menu €45-55) or a carmen with the floodlit Alhambra, such as Mirador de Aixa (€60-80). For dessert, a pionono at Casa Ysla. With an hour to spare, **San Jerónimo** (€7) has the city's finest Renaissance altarpiece and no tourists.

## When to go, getting there and getting around

| Season | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| March to June | Ideal: snow on Sierra Nevada, the Generalife in bloom |
| September and October | Ideal: pleasant temperatures |
| November | The secret month: 10-18 °C, few people, cheap hotels |
| December and January | Dry cold, crystal-clear mornings, white mountains |
| July and August | Avoid: 38-40 °C and the Alhambra in full sun |

- **AVE from Madrid:** 3 h 15, from €26-30 booked ahead; the station is 25 minutes' walk from the centre or €7 by taxi.
- **By air:** bus 245 to the centre in 45 minutes (€3.10); taxi €30-35. **Alsa buses:** from Málaga (1 h 30) and Seville (3 h).
- **Car:** the centre is a restricted zone with cameras and automatic fines, and car parks cost €25-30 a day. Leave it at the hotel or in San Agustín and forget about it.
- **City bus:** €1.60 (under €0.90 with the Credibús card). The **C30, C31 and C32 minibuses** go up to the Alhambra, the Albaicín and the Sacromonte from Plaza Nueva.

## Where to eat and stay

- **The tapa is free.** If they don't bring one, leave.
- **Meal times:** lunch from 14:00 to 16:00 and dinner from 21:00 to 23:00.
- **The price of a beer tells you the place:** €1.80-2.50 is an honest tavern; €3.50, a tourist one. Avoid the laminated menus on Plaza Nueva and opposite the Alhambra.

To stay, the **centre** (Plaza Nueva-Gran Vía) is 5 minutes from everything, at €90-180 a night: the practical choice for two days. The **Realejo** is more local and quiet, and the **lower Albaicín** has cármenes with the Alhambra visible from bed, pricey and hilly. Avoid Recogidas, Camino de Ronda, the Zaidín and the station area.

## Common mistakes and dates to plan around

1. **Not booking the Alhambra in advance** or **arriving late for the Nasrid Palaces.**
2. **The Alhambra at midday in summer:** go first thing or at night.
3. **Climbing the Albaicín in sandals or smooth soles.**
4. **Alhambra and Cathedral on the same day:** you'll be exhausted.
5. **Sacromonte packages with a set dinner** or the tourist train: they make no sense in such a compact city.
6. **Paying to "enter" the Mirador de San Nicolás:** it's public.
7. **Not booking the zambra.**

**Dates to plan around:** **Holy Week** (processions through the Albaicín, full hotels), the **Toma de Granada** (2 January, altered opening hours) and the **Music and Dance Festival** (June-July, with concerts in the Palace of Charles V and the Generalife).
