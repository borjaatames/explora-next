## Granada Christmas lights and markets

In 2025 the lights were switched on on **Saturday 29 November**, with dancing and local carols at 20:00, the switch-on at 20:30 and choirs around Bib-Rambla, Puerta Real, Zacatín and the Fuente de las Batallas.

- **Light and carol show:** daily at 19:00, 20:00 and 21:00 on Plaza del Carmen, in front of the City Hall.
- **Lighting hours:** 18:30 to 1:00; until 3:00 on Fridays, Saturdays and the eve of holidays, and until 5:00 on the 24th and 31st.
- **Markets:** crafts on **Puerta Real** and nativity scenes on **Bib-Rambla**, which in 2025 also launched a **convent-sweets market**.

**A December evening, done right:** head up to the **Mirador de San Nicolás** around 17:00 (sunset is about 18:00) to see the Alhambra at dusk; walk down the Carrera del Darro to Plaza Nueva; at 19:00, catch the first light show on Plaza del Carmen; then browse the Bib-Rambla and Puerta Real markets, go for tapas (in Granada the tapa comes with your drink) and, at 22:00, see flamenco in the Sacromonte.

## Sierra Nevada at Christmas and plans with kids

The resort is about **30 km from Granada**. In 2025-26 it opened on **29 November**, with runs in Borreguiles and snow cannons, and new lighting on the El Río runs for **Saturday night skiing**. For 2026-27 it has announced new snow machines to open the beginners' area earlier, but no date. The December long weekends and the week between Christmas and Three Kings are the busiest days. If you don't ski, the **Alpujarra** in winter, with fires lit and snow on the peaks, is a quieter day trip.

**With kids:**

- **Inverland**, with an ice rink and sledge slide, and the **Big Wheel** next door.
- The **light show** on Plaza del Carmen, short and free.
- The **Royal Postman**, who collects letters in the City Hall courtyard.
- In Sierra Nevada, sledging at **Mirlo Blanco** and beginner runs in Borreguiles.

## New Year's Eve, the Toma (2 January) and the Three Kings parade

- **New Year's chimes:** on **Plaza del Carmen**, in front of the City Hall.
- **2 January, Día de la Toma:** a local holiday marking the Catholic Monarchs' entry in 1492. Civic ceremony with the waving of the banner at the City Hall and a service in the Royal Chapel. That day the **Alcazaba is free** from 8:30 to 18:00 to ring the bell of the **Torre de la Vela**: tradition says single women who ring it marry within the year.
- **Three Kings parade (5 January):** at 17:00 from Avenida de la Constitución along Gran Vía, Reyes Católicos and the Acera del Darro; around 19:30, a drone show at the Puente Blanco, ending with the Kings on the City Hall balcony. With kids, find a spot on Gran Vía, near the start of the route.

## Flamenco and common mistakes

The **Sacromonte** caves are the night-time plan par excellence: Cueva La Rocío has a one-hour show from about €28 with a drink. In December it's worth booking, and bring a coat for the walk up and back.

- **Trying the Alhambra on 25 December or 1 January:** it's closed.
- **Buying Alhambra tickets at the last minute:** at Christmas they sell out weeks ahead.
- **Going up to the Albaicín at 18:30:** in December it's already dark; for sunset light, go up around 17:00.
- **Driving to Sierra Nevada without chains or checking the DGT:** after snowfall, the road may require them.
- **Assuming 2 January is a working day:** in Granada it's a holiday and many shops close.
