A single day in Granada is short but enough for what makes it unique: the Alhambra, the Albaicín looking back at the Alhambra, and a flamenco show in a cave. It all hinges on something you settle weeks in advance: **an Alhambra ticket with a Nasrid Palaces time slot**. With that in hand, the day organises itself.

## The day at a glance

| Time | Plan | Getting around |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8:30-12:30 | Alhambra: Alcazaba, Nasrid Palaces, Generalife | C30 or C32 minibus (€1.60) |
| 12:30-13:00 | Walk down Cuesta de Gomérez | On foot, 15 min |
| 13:30-15:15 | Tapas on Calle Navas or Elvira | On foot |
| 15:30-16:30 | Royal Chapel and Cathedral | On foot, 5 min |
| 16:45-18:00 | Alcaicería, Carrera del Darro and El Bañuelo | On foot |
| 18:00-19:30 | Up to the Albaicín, sunset at San Nicolás | C32 or a 25-min climb |
| 21:30 | Zambra in the Sacromonte | On foot, taxi back (€5-7) |

## Morning: the Alhambra

**8:30 — Into the complex.** Open 8:30-18:00 from 15 October to 31 March and until 20:00 the rest of the year. Take the **C30 or C32 minibus** from Plaza Isabel la Católica (€1.60) or a taxi (€8-10); on foot up the Cuesta de Gomérez it's a 25-30 minute climb.

**Alhambra night visit tickets sold out?** Here are [the alternatives that still work](/en/guides/granada/alhambra-night-visit-tickets-sold-out).

The golden rule: your **Nasrid Palaces** time is fixed and there are only **30 minutes' grace**. It's a 30-45 minute walk from the entrance to the palaces, so plan your route around that slot:

- **Nasrid Palaces** (1 h 30): the Court of the Myrtles, the Hall of Ambassadors, the Court of the Lions. Look up: the ceilings are the highlight.
- **Alcazaba** (45 min): the Torre de la Vela has the best view of Granada and the Sierra Nevada.
- **Generalife** (1 hour): the summer palace with the Patio de la Acequia.

About 4 hours in total. Bring your **passport or ID**: tickets are named. If you want to understand what you're looking at (the "patterns" on the walls are poems), the most popular guided tour includes entry:

## Lunch: free tapas

**13:30.** Walk down the Cuesta de Gomérez (15 minutes) to Plaza Nueva and carry on to **Calle Navas** or **Elvira**. In Granada **a tapa comes free with every drink**: three small beers (€1.80-2.50 each) make a meal. Arrive before 14:00 or after 15:30 to find space at the bar. If you'd rather be taken to the good bars, a private tapas tour with a local food guide makes three stops in 2 h 30.

## Afternoon: the old centre and the Carrera del Darro

**15:30 — Royal Chapel** (€7, 1 hour): the tombs of Ferdinand and Isabella, Joanna the Mad and Philip the Handsome, plus Isabella's crown and sceptre. Next door, the Renaissance **Cathedral** (€7; €10 until 28 November 2026 because of a temporary exhibition). Short on time? Pick the Royal Chapel.

**16:45 — Alcaicería and Carrera del Darro.** Cut through the Alcaicería (pure souvenirs, five minutes) and head to Plaza Nueva for the **Carrera del Darro**, Granada's loveliest street, with the river on one side and the Alhambra above. Stop at **El Bañuelo**, 11th-century Arab baths (€5, 15 minutes).

## Sunset: the Albaicín and San Nicolás

**18:00.** At the end of the Paseo de los Tristes, climb into the Albaicín via Cuesta del Chapiz (25 minutes uphill) or take the **C32 minibus** from Plaza Nueva. Wander up to the **Mirador de San Nicolás** and arrive 45 minutes before sunset: the Alhambra turns red with the Sierra Nevada behind it. If it's packed, the San Cristóbal viewpoint, 10 minutes higher, has half the crowd. For a guided walk, the Albaicín and Sacromonte sunset tour starts at €15; privately, the four-cultures tour (from €180 per group) covers the centre, Albaicín and Sacromonte:

## Evening: a zambra in the Sacromonte

**21:30.** From San Nicolás it's 15-20 minutes on foot to the **Sacromonte** caves. The zambra is cave flamenco, intimate and with gypsy roots: €22-30 with a drink. Book, especially at weekends, and take a taxi back (€5-7). To pick a cave, see [which Sacromonte cave show to choose](/en/guides/granada/flamenco-cave-sacromonte-which-to-choose).

## Day-trippers and families

- **From Málaga:** direct Avant train in 1 h 18 or the Alsa coach in 1 h 45 (about €13). Book an Alhambra slot from 10:30 and drop the zambra; options in [Granada and the Alhambra from Málaga](/en/guides/malaga/granada-alhambra-day-trip-from-malaga).
- **With children:** under-12s enter the Alhambra free but **need their own named ticket**. Use the minibuses for every climb and swap the zambra for an early dinner. More in [Granada with kids](/en/guides/granada/granada-with-kids).
- **If it rains:** the Alhambra stays open and the Nasrid Palaces are indoors; wear shoes with grip, as the Albaicín's cobbles get slippery.

## Common mistakes

1. **Turning up without an Alhambra ticket** expecting to buy one there.
2. **Missing your Nasrid Palaces slot:** after 30 minutes you're not let in.
3. **Going up to the Albaicín at midday** and leaving before sunset: you miss the best bit.
4. **Eating on the Plaza Nueva terraces**, where tapas aren't free.
5. **Accepting "Alhambra tours" from touts** with no accreditation.

## In a nutshell

The Alhambra in the morning, tapas at lunch, the Darro in the afternoon and San Nicolás at sunset: a well-planned day in Granada is one of the best in Spain. If you can stay another, [Granada in 2 days](/en/guides/granada/granada-in-2-days) adds the Cartuja and the Realejo.
