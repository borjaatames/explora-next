## The 4 Sacromonte flamenco caves compared

On paper all four offer the same thing: gypsy zambra, singing and dancing inside a cave carved into the hillside. The difference is in the details.

| Cave | Price from | Length | Atmosphere | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cueva del Zincalé | €20 | 50 min | Run directly by the performers, artisanal feel | Watching the family who runs it perform |
| Cueva Los Amayas | €25 | 1 h | No stage or microphones, 3 metres from the dancers, drink included | Purists: the most traditional format |
| Cueva La Faraona | €16 | 1 h | The smallest (25 seats), a metre and a half from the artists | The lowest price without giving up closeness |
| Cuevas Los Tarantos | €28 | 1 h | Open since 1972, drink included, large cast, 2,093 reviews | A first time: the option with the most margin for error |

## Which flamenco cave to choose in Granada

- **First time and don't want to risk it?** [Los Tarantos](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cuevas-tarantos-sacromonte). It's the biggest and most established (over 50 years running), includes a drink and has the most reviews.
- **Looking for the most intimate experience?** La Faraona. With only 25 seats and no stage, you're a metre and a half from the performers. It's also the cheapest.
- **Want neighbourhood flamenco with nothing staged for tourists?** Los Amayas: no stage, no microphones, in the district where this style was born.
- **Like the idea of a show run by the performing family itself?** El Zincalé fits that best. It's the shortest show (50 minutes) and a 5-minute walk from Los Amayas.

## Los Tarantos or Los Amayas?

This is the most common dilemma, since both include a drink. **Los Tarantos** is the safe bet: bigger, with a large cast and half a century of history. **Los Amayas** is smaller and barer: no stage or microphones, about 3 metres from the dancers, and slightly cheaper (€25 vs €28). If reputation matters most, Los Tarantos; if authenticity and closeness matter most, Los Amayas.

## What zambra is and what to expect

- **Zambra is not a tablao:** it's the gypsy flamenco of the Sacromonte caves, with less stage and more group dancing, clapping and shouts of encouragement.
- **The audience sits on benches** along the cave, one or two metres from the performers. If you're after armchairs, air conditioning and microphones, this isn't it.
- **Photos:** no flash, and only if the cave allows it; ask when you arrive. Filming is banned in several.
- **With dinner:** some caves offer dinner before the show. It's simple; if you want to eat well, go for tapas first in the lower Albaicín or on Paseo de los Tristes.
- **Children:** they're allowed, but the cave is small, dark and loud. From about 8 they enjoy it; younger ones tend to be scared by the footwork.

## Show times, booking and tips

- **Two shows a night:** the first around 20:00-21:00 and the second from 22:00. In summer the second is cooler and livelier.
- **Book 2-3 days ahead** in high season (Easter, summer): the caves are small and fill up fast, especially Los Tarantos and La Faraona. All have free cancellation up to 24 hours before.
- **Arrive 20 minutes early:** seating is first come, first served and the cave is narrow; latecomers end up at the back.
- **What to bring:** comfortable shoes (Camino del Sacromonte is steep and cobbled), a jacket in winter (the cave is cool) and cash for drinks if they aren't included.

## How to get to Sacromonte

Sacromonte is a hillside district on the edge of the centre, and public transport is limited at night. **None of the four tickets includes transport** or hotel pick-up, and there's nowhere to park in the neighbourhood.

- **On foot:** 20-25 minutes uphill from Plaza Nueva along Paseo de los Tristes, beautiful at sunset.
- **Taxi:** 10-15 minutes from Plaza Nueva. The easiest way back at night.
- **C34 minibus:** leaves Plaza Nueva roughly every 20 minutes; check the time of the last service.

If you arrive early, the view from Sacromonte at sunset with the Alhambra behind is free and one of the best viewpoints in Granada.
