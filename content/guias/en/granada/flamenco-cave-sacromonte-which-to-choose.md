If you've searched "flamenco Sacromonte Granada" you'll have found four different caves offering, on paper, the same thing: gypsy zambra, about an hour of singing and dancing, inside a cave carved into the Sacromonte hillside. The prices are similar (between €20 and €28), the photos look alike and so do the descriptions on most travel search sites. The real difference is in the details, and this guide exists so you don't have to open all four listings and compare them yourself.

## The 4 caves, compared

| Cave | Price from | Duration | Atmosphere | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Cueva del Zincalé](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cueva-zincale-sacromonte) | €20 | 50 min | Run directly by the performing artists, artisanal feel | Wanting the closest thing to "watching the family who runs it perform" |
| [Cueva Los Amayas](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cueva-los-amayas-sacromonte) | €25 | 1h | No stage, no PA system, 3 metres from the dancers, drink included | The purist who wants the most traditional format, no staging |
| [Cueva La Faraona](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cueva-la-faraona-sacromonte) | €20 | 1h | The smallest (25 seats), a metre and a half from the artists | The lowest price in the group without giving up closeness |
| [Cuevas Los Tarantos](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cuevas-tarantos-sacromonte) | €28 | 1h | Running since 1972, drink included, larger cast, 2,093 reviews | First time seeing flamenco in a cave: the safest bet |

All four offer **free cancellation up to 24 hours before** and none includes transport.

## How to choose in 30 seconds

- **First time and don't want to risk it?** Los Tarantos. It's the biggest and the most established (running for over 50 years), with a drink included and the most reviews of the four (2,093, at 4.5 out of 5).
- **Looking for the most intimate experience possible?** La Faraona. With only 25 seats and no stage, you're literally a step and a half from the performers. It's also the cheapest, but no drink is included.
- **Want flamenco the way locals see it, with nothing staged for tourists?** Los Amayas: no stage, no microphones, 60 seats, a drink included and views of the Alhambra and Generalife from the cave.
- **Like the idea of a show run by the performing family itself?** El Zincalé fits that best. It's the shortest show (50 minutes) and a 5-minute walk from Los Amayas.

None of the four is objectively "the best" — the real trade-offs are room size, whether a drink is included, and how much weight you give to reputation (reviews) versus exclusivity (a small room).

## What to know before booking

- **Zambra is not a tablao.** It's the gypsy flamenco of the Sacromonte caves: less stage, more group dancing, clapping and shouts of encouragement. The audience sits on benches or small chairs along the cave walls, one or two metres from the artists. If you want armchairs, air conditioning and microphones, this isn't it. Reviews of Los Tarantos and Los Amayas mention tight seating and some partly blocked views.
- **Language doesn't matter much.** Los Amayas lists Spanish and English, and the other three Spanish only, but the singing is in Andalusian Spanish everywhere and there's little talking between numbers.
- **Showtimes:** caves usually run two shows a night, the first around 20:00–21:00 and the second from 22:00. In summer the later one is cooler and livelier. Check the exact times when you book.
- **Arrive 20 minutes early.** Seats aren't numbered and the caves are narrow, so latecomers end up at the back.
- **What to bring:** comfortable shoes (the Camino del Sacromonte is steep and cobbled), a light layer in winter (caves stay cool) and cash for drinks at Zincalé and La Faraona.
- **Dinner:** Los Tarantos can serve a traditional dinner beforehand, paid separately at the venue. If eating well matters, have tapas in the lower Albaicín or on the Paseo de los Tristes first.
- **Children** get in at all four, but the caves are small, dark and loud. Children of about 8 and over tend to enjoy it; younger ones are often frightened by the footwork.
- **Photos:** no flash, and only where the cave allows it; ask at the door. Some ban filming.
- **Accessibility:** none of the caves suits wheelchair users; the lanes are steep and uneven. If that matters, choose a central tablao in Granada instead.

## Getting there and when to book

Sacromonte is a hillside neighbourhood on the edge of Granada's old town. On foot from Plaza Nueva it's 20–40 minutes uphill depending on the cave, via the Paseo de los Tristes and the Cuesta del Chapiz, and the walk is lovely at sunset. The C34 minibus leaves from Plaza Nueva about every 20 minutes. Coming back at night, a taxi (10–15 minutes to Plaza Nueva) is easiest. Parking is very limited, so don't drive.

Book at least 2–3 days ahead in high season (Easter week, summer): these are small caves and they fill up fast, especially Los Tarantos and La Faraona. With 25 seats, La Faraona can sell out on any weekend.

If you want to explore the neighbourhood before or after the show, the sunset view of Sacromonte with the Alhambra in the background is free and one of the best viewpoints in Granada. A guided [Albaicín and Sacromonte sunset tour](/en/cities/granada/activities/albaicin-sacromonte-sunset-tour) (from €15, 2–2.5 hours) times the Mirador de San Nicolás for sunset and loops back to Plaza Nueva, where the C34 starts. Outside summer, when sunset is early, it leaves time for the late show.
