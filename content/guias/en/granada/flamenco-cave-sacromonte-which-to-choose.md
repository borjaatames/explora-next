If you've searched "flamenco Sacromonte Granada" you'll have found four different caves offering, on paper, the same thing: gypsy zambra, about an hour of singing and dancing, inside a cave carved into the Sacromonte hillside. The prices are similar (between €18 and €28), the photos look alike and so do the descriptions on most travel search sites. The real difference is in the details, and this guide exists so you don't have to open all four listings and compare them yourself.

## The 4 caves, compared

| Cave | Price from | Duration | Atmosphere | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Cueva del Zincalé](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cueva-zincale-sacromonte) | €20 | 50 min | Run directly by the performing artists, artisanal feel | Wanting the closest thing to "watching the family who runs it perform" |
| [Cueva Los Amayas](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cueva-los-amayas-sacromonte) | €23 | 1h | No stage, no PA system, 3 metres from the dancers | The purist who wants the most traditional format, no staging |
| [Cueva La Faraona](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cueva-la-faraona-sacromonte) | €18 | 1h | The smallest (25 seats), a metre and a half from the artists | The lowest price in the group without giving up closeness |
| [Cuevas Los Tarantos](/en/cities/granada/activities/flamenco-show-cuevas-tarantos-sacromonte) | €28 | 1h | Running since 1972, drink included, larger cast, 2,093 reviews | First time seeing flamenco in a cave: the safest bet |

## How to choose in 30 seconds

- **First time and don't want to risk it?** Los Tarantos. It's the biggest, the most established (running for over 50 years) and the only one with a drink included in the price — which is also why it has almost ten times more reviews than the other three combined.
- **Looking for the most intimate experience possible?** La Faraona. With only 25 seats and no stage, you're literally a step and a half from the performers. It's also the cheapest.
- **Want flamenco the way locals see it, with nothing staged for tourists?** Los Amayas: no stage, no microphones, in the neighbourhood where this style was born.
- **Price doesn't matter and you like the idea of a show run by the performing family itself?** El Zincalé fits that best, and it's a 5-minute walk from Los Amayas if you want to compare the neighbourhood.

None of the four is objectively "the best" — the real trade-offs are room size, whether a drink is included, and how much weight you give to reputation (reviews) versus exclusivity (a small room).

## Getting there and when to book

Sacromonte is a hillside neighbourhood on the edge of Granada's old town, and public transport at night is limited. Take a taxi (10-15 minutes from Plaza Nueva) or check whether your ticket includes hotel pickup — several of these shows offer it as an option. Book at least 2-3 days ahead in high season (Easter week, summer): these are small caves and they fill up fast, especially Los Tarantos and La Faraona.

If you want to explore the neighbourhood before or after the show, the sunset view of Sacromonte with the Alhambra in the background is free and one of the best viewpoints in Granada.
