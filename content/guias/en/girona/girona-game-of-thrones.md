## Game of Thrones locations in Girona: from the cathedral to Braavos

- **Cathedral steps:** the exterior of the Great Sept of Baelor in King's Landing. Jaime Lannister rides up them with the Tyrell army.
- **El Call (Jewish Quarter):** the alleys of Braavos where Arya flees.
- **Pujada de Sant Domènec:** the steps where Arya, chased by the Waif, crashes into a crate of oranges.
- **Sant Pere de Galligants:** the monastery that played the Citadel in Oldtown, where Sam and Gilly arrive.
- **Pont de Pedra and the Onyar houses:** Braavos scenes.
- **Passeig Arqueològic:** the city walls, backdrop to several sequences.

Note: **not everything was filmed in Girona**. The Braavos scenes mix Girona with Šibenik (Croatia): if you can't find a location, it's probably not here.

## The walking route through the locations

| Order | Stop |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Pont de Pedra and the Onyar houses |
| 2 | El Call and Plaça dels Jurats |
| 3 | Steps and Cathedral |
| 4 | Pujada de Sant Domènec |
| 5 | Arab Baths and Sant Pere de Galligants |
| 6 | Passeig Arqueològic (city walls) |

It takes about **2 hours** at an easy pace, within a 500-metre radius. Start at **9:00** and you'll reach the steps before the groups and finish on the walls by mid-morning. The Pujada de Sant Domènec steps and the climb to the walls are steep: with a pushchair or reduced mobility, skip those two.

## Prices, times and getting there

- **The locations are open streets:** seeing them is **free**.
- **If you go inside the monuments:** the Cathedral has a joint ticket with Sant Feliu (**€7.50** in 2026, with audio guide); the Arab Baths and Sant Pere de Galligants, now the Archaeology Museum of Catalonia, cost a few euros.
- **The Cathedral steps** (90 of them) fill with groups from 11:00. For a crowd-free photo, go **before 9:30 or after 19:00**.
- **From Barcelona:** the AVE gets you there in 38 minutes and the station is a 15-minute walk from the first location. It's a morning, leaving the afternoon for the city.

## Guided tour or on your own?

You can see everything on your own, but without the show's stills beside you the scenes are hard to place: many spots were digitally altered.

- **Fans travelling as one or two:** the **small-group tour**. Two hours, maximum 10 people, and the guide shows you each scene on an iPad on the spot.
- **Families or groups of four or more:** the **private tour**. The €260 is per group (up to 8 people), so per head it works out similar. Same company and same route, 2 h 30, at your own pace.
- **More interested in Girona than the show:** the Barri Vell walking tour, 2 h 30 of history (the walls, the cathedral, El Call, the Eiffel bridge) that passes several locations.

All three start from Plaça de Sant Feliu, a 15-minute walk from the station, and stay outside: monument tickets aren't included. None is wheelchair accessible.

## Tips

- **Don't go at midday:** the steps fill up and your photos will have a hundred people in them.
- **Respect El Call:** it's a lived-in neighbourhood with narrow alleys. No shouting or endless posing.
- **Don't just stick to the locations:** the cathedral interior, El Call and the walls are as good as the show. The route almost exactly matches [what to see in Girona in one day](/en/guides/girona/what-to-see-in-girona-in-one-day).
