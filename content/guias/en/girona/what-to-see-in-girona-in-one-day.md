**Girona is easy to see in a day.** Its old town is compact and walkable: the cathedral, the Call, the colourful Onyar houses and the city walls. It's 38 minutes from Barcelona by high-speed train.

## Getting there and when to go

- **By train from Barcelona:** AVE or Avant from Sants in 38 minutes, with departures all morning; the regional train takes 1 h 20 and costs about half. The station is a 15-minute walk from the old town.
- **By car:** don't drive into the Barri Vell, which is pedestrianised and steep. Park at La Devesa or the station car park and cross the river.
- **Tickets worth knowing about:** the Cathedral and Sant Feliu share one ticket (€7.50 as of 2026, audio guide included); the walls are free, the Arab Baths cost a few euros and the Call is an open neighbourhood. The Museum of Jewish History costs €4.
- **Best time of year:** April to June and September to October. During **Temps de Flors** (mid-May, nine days) courtyards and monuments are covered in flowers: beautiful, but the city is packed and hotels sell out weeks ahead. In winter there are no queues.
- **Sant Narcís festival** (late October): fairground, concerts and a local atmosphere, without the May crowds.

## One-day route

| Time | Stop |
| --- | --- |
| 9:30 | Onyar houses and the Eiffel iron bridge |
| 10:00 | Cathedral and steps |
| 11:30 | The Call (Jewish quarter) |
| 13:00 | Arab Baths and Sant Pere de Galligants |
| 14:00 | Lunch in the Barri Vell |
| 16:30 | Walk along the city walls |

- **Onyar houses:** the best view is from the bridges, especially the red iron one built by Eiffel's firm. Fifteen minutes is enough.
- **Cathedral:** its Gothic nave is the widest in the world, at 23 metres, and its treasury holds the Romanesque Tapestry of Creation. Its steps were the Great Sept of Baelor in Game of Thrones. On **Sundays and holidays it opens at 12:00**, so swap it with the Call that day.
- **The Call:** one of Europe's best-preserved medieval Jewish quarters. Walk the Carrer de la Força and its side alleys, and add the Museum of Jewish History if the quarter's story hooks you.
- **Arab Baths:** a 12th-century bathhouse, small and quick to see (15–30 minutes). Sant Pere de Galligants, across the street, was the Citadel in Oldtown.
- **City walls:** the best viewpoint in town, and few people climb up. Best at sunset. The walk is free but has steep steps.

The route covers every [Game of Thrones location](/en/guides/girona/girona-game-of-thrones), so you don't need a separate morning for them.

## Eating

Look for Empordà cooking in the Barri Vell, one street back from the Rambla (the Rambla terraces cost more). Try a **xuixo**, the local cream-filled pastry, and an ice cream at **Rocambolesc**.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Treating it as a half-hour stop** between Barcelona and the Costa Brava: Girona deserves a full day.
2. **Skipping the cathedral interior:** the nave and the Tapestry of Creation are worth the ticket.
3. **Eating at the first terrace on the Rambla:** one street back, the food improves and prices drop.
4. **Skipping the walls:** they're the best viewpoint and hardly anyone climbs up. Go in the evening light.
5. **Coming only for the Game of Thrones photos:** the city is much more than a film set.
6. **Coming during Temps de Flors without booking:** hotels and restaurants fill up.
