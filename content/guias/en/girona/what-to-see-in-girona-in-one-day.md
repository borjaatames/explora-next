## The one-day route through Girona

| Time | Stop |
| --- | --- |
| 9:30 | Onyar houses and Eiffel's iron bridge |
| 10:00 | Cathedral and steps |
| 11:30 | El Call (Jewish Quarter) |
| 13:00 | Arab Baths and Sant Pere de Galligants |
| 14:00 | Lunch in the Barri Vell |
| 16:30 | Walk along the city walls |

It's all on foot, with hills. On Sundays and holidays, swap the Cathedral and El Call: the Cathedral doesn't open until 12:00.

## What to see in Girona: from the cathedral to El Call

- **Onyar houses:** the best view is from the bridges, especially the red iron one built by Eiffel's company. Fifteen minutes is enough.
- **Cathedral:** its Gothic nave is the **widest in the world, at 23 metres**, and its treasury holds the Romanesque **Tapestry of Creation**. Both justify the ticket.
- **El Call:** one of the best-preserved medieval Jewish quarters in Europe. Walk Carrer de la Força and its alleys, and add the Museum of Jewish History if the neighbourhood's story grabs you.
- **Arab Baths:** 12th-century baths, small and quick to see (15–30 minutes). Sant Pere de Galligants is opposite.
- **City walls:** the best viewpoint in the city, and hardly anyone goes up. Best at sunset. The walk is free but has steep steps.

## Girona and Game of Thrones

The route passes all the [Game of Thrones locations](/en/guides/girona/girona-game-of-thrones), so you don't need another morning for them: the Cathedral steps were the Great Sept of Baelor and Sant Pere de Galligants the Citadel in Oldtown. Just don't come only for the show photos: the city is much more than a film set.

## Getting there, prices and when to go

- **By train from Barcelona:** AVE or Avant from Sants in **38 minutes**, with departures all morning; the regional train takes 1 h 20 and costs half. The station is a 15-minute walk from the old town.
- **By car:** don't drive into the Barri Vell, which is pedestrianised and hilly. Park at the Devesa car park or the station and cross the river.
- **Tickets:** the Cathedral and Sant Feliu share a joint ticket (**€7.50** in 2026, audio guide included). The walls are free, the Arab Baths cost a few euros, El Call is an open neighbourhood and the Museum of Jewish History costs €4.
- **Best time:** April to June and September to October. During **Temps de Flors** (mid-May, nine days) courtyards and monuments are covered in flowers: beautiful, but the city gets crowded and hotels sell out weeks ahead. In winter there are no queues.
- **Sant Narcís festivities** (late October): fairground, concerts and local atmosphere, without the May crowds.

## Where to eat

Look for Empordà cooking in the Barri Vell, **one street back from the Rambla**: the Rambla terraces are pricier. Try a **xuixo**, the local cream-filled pastry, and an ice cream from **Rocambolesc**.

## Tips

- **Give it the whole day:** it's not a half-hour stop between Barcelona and the Costa Brava.
- **Start with a walking tour** if you want context: 2 h 30 along the walls, the cathedral, El Call and the Eiffel bridge, and at the end the guide tells you what to see inside and where to eat. Tickets are extra. If you prefer your own pace, the DareMapp app audio guide costs €5.
- **Don't skip the walls** or the cathedral interior.
- **During Temps de Flors, book** your hotel and restaurant early.
- **If you have time to spare,** see the [Costa Brava day trips from Girona](/en/guides/girona/costa-brava-day-trips-from-girona).
