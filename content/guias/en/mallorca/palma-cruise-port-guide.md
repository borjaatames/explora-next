## Where do cruise ships dock in Palma? Poniente quay and Dique del Oeste

Palma has two cruise areas, both west of the city:

| Area | Terminals | Distance to the cathedral | How to get there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Poniente quay** (Avinguda Gabriel Roca) | Maritime stations 1 to 4 | About 3 km | 40-min walk, EMT bus 1 or taxi |
| **Dique del Oeste** (Dic de l'Oest) | Stations 5 and 6, for the largest ships | About 4-5 km | EMT bus 1, cruise line shuttle or taxi |

Check your ship's daily programme to see which one you get: the big ships usually go to the Dique del Oeste, which is further out and not one for walking.

## How to get from the cruise port to Palma old town

- **EMT bus 1.** Links the Dique del Oeste and the maritime stations with the Paseo Marítimo, Passeig de Mallorca and **Plaça d'Espanya** (station for the Sóller train and buses around the island). The full route takes about 30 minutes. A ticket costs **€2**, paid on board in cash or by card; the city council has proposed raising it to €3 for passengers without a transport card, so it may change. For the cathedral, get off on the Paseo Marítimo or Passeig de Mallorca and walk 10-15 minutes.
- **Cruise line shuttle.** Many lines run a bus to the centre, sometimes included, sometimes paid. Ask on board about the price and the return point.
- **Taxi.** There is a rank at the terminals. On the official tariff (€2.50 flag fall, €1.20 per km and a €4.65 port supplement), the cathedral costs **around €11-15**. Queues form when several ships are in.
- **Bus Nàutic.** On 1 November 2026 two port boat lines aimed at cruise passengers started running, at **€10 per trip** (€18 return). As the service is new, confirm stops and times at the port.
- **On foot.** From Poniente, the Paseo Marítimo is flat with views of the bay, but there is little shade in summer.

## What to do in Palma on a cruise, by time ashore

Always count your real time ashore: from stepping off the ship until an hour before "all aboard".

| Time ashore | Suggested plan |
| --- | --- |
| **4 hours** | Bus or taxi to the centre, the **cathedral** inside (1 h), the Almudaina Palace from outside, Parc de la Mar and a stroll through the old town to the Born. |
| **6 hours** | All of the above plus a **guided old town tour** or tapas route, and **Bellver Castle** on the way back, on the hill above the port. |
| **8 hours or more** | **Sóller train** and tram to Port de Sóller, or **Valldemossa** by bus; or the **Drach Caves** on a half-day trip. |

**Cathedral (La Seu).** From April to November it opens Monday to Friday 10:00-17:15 and Saturday 10:00-14:15; from 1 December to 31 March it closes at 15:15 on weekdays. **No sightseeing on Sundays.** On a short call, buy your ticket in advance and skip the ticket office queue.

**Old town.** Behind the cathedral begin the grand courtyards, the Arab Baths and the streets leading to Plaza Mayor and the Born. With a guide you get the picture in under two hours.

**Bellver Castle.** The round castle with views over the bay costs **€4** and **closes on Mondays**. Tuesday to Saturday it opens 10:00-18:00 and on Sundays 10:00-15:00 with free entry. No city bus goes up to the gate: from the port, a taxi is the practical option.

**Valldemossa on your own.** **TIB bus 203** leaves the Estació Intermodal (Plaça d'Espanya) for Valldemossa, Deià and Sóller. The Royal Charterhouse takes about an hour.

## The Sóller train from the port

The wooden train leaves from **Plaça d'Espanya**, the last stop of EMT bus 1, and takes about **an hour** to Sóller. In 2026 it costs **€23 one way and €30 return**; the tram to Port de Sóller is €10 more, and the combined train and tram ticket €40.

- **Departures from Palma (2026):** 10:10, 10:50, 12:15, 13:30 and 15:10.
- **Returns from Sóller:** 12:15, 14:00, 17:15 (to be confirmed at the ticket office) and 18:00.
- **Realistic plan:** the 10:10 or 10:50 train, lunch in Sóller or at the port, and back on the 14:00 (reaching Palma around 15:00). With "all aboard" before 17:00, don't try it.
- **Winter:** the train stops for a few weeks of maintenance. In 2025-2026 it did not run from 24 November to 1 February, and for 2026-2027 the closure is announced from 22 November to early February.

If you'd rather not depend on timetables, there are bus-and-tram tours of Valldemossa and Sóller lasting about 6 hours; check that the pick-up point works from the port.

## Mallorca shore excursions: ship's tour or independent?

| | Ship's excursion | Independent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Price** | The highest | Bus €2, taxi €11-15, tours from €39 |
| **If you're late** | The ship waits for you | You're responsible for getting back |
| **Flexibility** | Fixed times and stops | You decide |
| **When it pays off** | Far-off places on a short call | Palma city, Bellver, Sóller with 8 h or more |

In Palma, the city is perfectly easy to see on your own. Old town walking tours last between 1 h 45 min and 3 hours. **Drach Caves** trips leave the centre at 9:30 and return in the early afternoon: they only work if you dock early and sail in the afternoon.

## What time to be back on board, and other tips

- **"All aboard"** is usually 30 to 60 minutes before sailing; the exact time is in the daily programme. That is the deadline, not the departure time.
- Allow **30 minutes** by bus from the centre to the Dique del Oeste, more if there are taxi queues. If you go to Sóller or Valldemossa, leave **at least an hour and a half of margin**.
- **Don't count on walking from the Dique del Oeste:** it is 4-5 km and the first stretch is unpleasant.
- **Sundays and holidays:** the cathedral is closed to visitors, Bellver opens 10:00-15:00 for free, and EMT and TIB buses run reduced timetables (bus 1 stops earlier at weekends). If you return late, keep a taxi in mind.
- **Don't cut it fine:** taxi and shuttle queues grow when several ships are in port.
