Palma is one of the easiest cruise calls in the Mediterranean: the port sits inside the bay, you can see the cathedral from the deck and you can be in the old town within 20-30 minutes without a ship's tour. This guide covers where your ship docks, how to get into town and what fits into the time you have.

## Where do cruise ships dock in Palma?

Palma has two cruise areas, both west of the city centre:

| Area | Terminals | Distance to the cathedral | How to get in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Poniente quay** (Avinguda Gabriel Roca) | Terminals 1 to 4 | About 3 km | 40-min walk, EMT bus 1 or taxi |
| **Dique del Oeste** (Dic de l'Oest) | Terminals 5 and 6, for the largest ships | About 4-5 km | EMT bus 1, cruise line shuttle or taxi |

Your ship's daily programme tells you which one you're at. Big ships usually go to the Dique del Oeste, which is further out and not a pleasant walk.

## How do you get from the cruise port to Palma old town?

- **EMT bus line 1.** It links the Dique del Oeste and the passenger terminals with the seafront, Passeig de Mallorca and **Plaça d'Espanya** (for the Sóller train and island buses). The full run takes about 30 minutes. A single ticket costs **€2**, paid on board in cash or by card; the city council has proposed raising it to €3 for passengers without a travel card, so it may change. For the cathedral, get off on the Paseo Marítimo or Passeig de Mallorca and walk 10-15 minutes.
- **Cruise line shuttle.** Many lines run a bus into town, sometimes included and sometimes charged. Check the price and pick-up point on board.
- **Taxi.** There's a rank at the terminals. On the official 2025 tariff (€2.50 flag fall, €1.20 per km and a €4.65 port supplement), the cathedral costs **around €11-15**. Queues build up when several ships are in.
- **Bus Nàutic.** On 1 November 2026 the port launched two boat lines aimed at cruise passengers, at **€10 one way** (€18 return). It's a brand-new service, so check stops and times at the port.
- **On foot.** From Poniente, the seafront is flat with views across the bay, but there's little shade in summer.

## What to see in Palma in 4, 6 or 8 hours

Count your real time ashore: from stepping off the ship to an hour before all aboard.

| Time ashore | Suggested plan |
| --- | --- |
| **4 hours** | Bus or taxi into town, the **cathedral** inside (1 hr), the Almudaina Palace from outside, Parc de la Mar and a stroll through the old town to the Born. Back with time to spare. |
| **6 hours** | All of the above plus a **guided old town walk** or a tapas route, and **Bellver Castle** on the way back: it's on the hill above the port, a few minutes by taxi. |
| **8 hours or more** | The **Sóller train** from Plaça d'Espanya and the tram to Port de Sóller, or **Valldemossa** by bus; or a half-day trip to the **Drach Caves**. |

### Cathedral, old town and Bellver

- **Cathedral (La Seu).** April to November: Monday to Friday 10:00-17:15, Saturday 10:00-14:15. From 1 December to 31 March it closes at 15:15 on weekdays. **No sightseeing visits on Sundays.** On a short call, buy your ticket in advance to skip the ticket office queue.

- **Old town.** Behind the cathedral you'll find the grand courtyards, the Arab Baths and the lanes up to Plaça Major and the Born. With a guide, you'll get it in under two hours.

- **Bellver Castle.** The round castle with bay views costs **€4**, is **closed on Mondays** and on Sundays opens 10:00-15:00 with free entry. Tuesday to Saturday it's open 10:00-18:00. No city bus goes up to the gate, so a taxi from the port is the practical option.

### The Sóller train on a port day

The wooden train leaves from **Plaça d'Espanya**, the last stop of EMT bus 1, and takes about **an hour** to Sóller. In 2026 it costs **€23 one way and €30 return**; the tram to Port de Sóller is another €10, and the combined train and tram ticket is €40.

- **Departures from Palma (2026 timetable):** 10:10, 10:50, 12:15, 13:30 and 15:10.
- **Return trains from Sóller:** 12:15, 14:00, 17:15 (to be confirmed at the ticket office) and 18:00.
- **A realistic plan:** take the 10:10 or 10:50, have lunch in Sóller or the port and return on the 14:00 (into Palma around 15:00). If all aboard is before 17:00, don't try it.
- **Winter:** the line closes for a few weeks of maintenance. In 2025-2026 it didn't run from 24 November to 1 February, and for 2026-2027 the closure has been announced from 22 November until early February.

If you'd rather not juggle timetables, the bus-and-tram tour of Valldemossa and Sóller takes 6 hours; check when booking that the pick-up point works from the port.

### Valldemossa on your own

**TIB bus 203** leaves from the Estació Intermodal (Plaça d'Espanya) for Valldemossa, Deià and Sóller. In Valldemossa, the Royal Charterhouse takes about an hour.

## What time should you be back on board?

- **All aboard** is usually 30 to 60 minutes before sailing; the exact time is in the daily programme. That's your deadline, not the departure time.
- Allow **30 minutes** from the centre to the Dique del Oeste by bus, more if the taxi rank is busy.
- If you go to Sóller or Valldemossa, leave **at least 90 minutes of slack**: return trains and buses fill up.

Palma itself is easy to see on your own. The ship's tour only makes sense for going far with little time.

## Sundays and public holidays

- The **cathedral is closed to visitors** on Sundays.
- **Bellver** opens 10:00-15:00 and is free.
- EMT and TIB buses run reduced timetables and bus 1 stops earlier at weekends; if you're heading back late, have a taxi in mind.

## Common mistakes

1. **Planning to walk from the Dique del Oeste.** It's 4-5 km and the first stretch is unappealing.
2. **Doing Sóller with less than 8 hours.** Between bus, train and queues, it isn't worth it.
3. **Saving the cathedral for a Sunday.** You can only go inside Monday to Saturday.
4. **Cutting it fine for all aboard.** Taxi and shuttle queues grow when several ships are in.
