## What to eat in Mallorca: the essentials

- **Ensaïmada.** The spiral pastry par excellence, made with lard (*saïm*). Plain or filled with pumpkin jam (*cabello de ángel*); there's also a savoury one with sobrasada. For breakfast or an afternoon snack.
- **Sobrasada.** The spreadable cured pork-and-paprika sausage, with a Protected Designation of Origin. Eaten on its own, with bread, or warm with honey, a surprising combination.
- **Pa amb oli.** Brown bread with oil, rubbed ramellet tomato, salt and, on top, cheese or ham. Simple and addictive.
- **Tumbet.** Layered baked vegetables (potato, aubergine, pepper) with tomato sauce. The star side dish.
- **Frit and arròs brut.** The **frit mallorquí** (offal with potato and vegetables) and **arròs brut**, a soupy "dirty" rice with meat and game, eaten with a spoon.

## Where to eat in Mallorca

The rule: **the further you get from the Magaluf or s'Arenal seafront** and the deeper into the villages, the better you eat.

| Place | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Cellers (Inca, Sineu, Algaida) | Traditional Mallorcan cooking |
| Mercat de l'Olivar / Santa Catalina (Palma) | Tapas and fresh produce |
| Village markets (Sineu, Wednesdays) | Local atmosphere and produce |
| Tramuntana villages | Sóller oranges, oil, ice creams |

**Palma's markets.** **Mercat de l'Olivar** is where Palma residents shop: it opens Monday to Saturday from 7:00 and closes at 14:30 on weekdays (with a partial reopening on Friday afternoon until 20:00) and at 15:00 on Saturdays. **Santa Catalina** is smaller, with more bars for tapas. Both are closed on Sundays.

**Village markets**, in the morning (stalls pack up around 14:00): **Sineu** on Wednesdays (the most authentic, still with livestock), **Inca** on Thursdays, **Santa Maria** on Sundays, **Alcúdia** on Tuesdays and Sundays and **Pollença** on Sundays.

## Prices, times and booking

- **Set lunch at a celler:** €15-20.
- **Pa amb oli with ham:** €9-12. **Portion of frit:** €10-14.
- **Single ensaïmada:** €2.50-3.50; a large one to take away, €12-20 depending on the filling.
- **Times:** lunch from **13:30 to 15:30** and dinner **from 20:30**; outside those hours, village bars usually serve only snacks.
- **Cellers:** they close one or two weekdays and some shut for holidays in August. Phone first.
- **Booking:** in July and August, always, even for lunch. The good places on the coast fill up days in advance.

## Wine, sweets and seasonal treats

To drink, wines from the DO **Binissalem** (with the Manto Negro grape) and **Pla i Llevant**, increasingly good. As a digestif, **Mallorcan herb liqueur** (*hierbas*: sweet, dry or mixed).

Besides the ensaïmada, there's **gató** (almond sponge served with ice cream) and, at Easter, **robiols** and **crespells**. The almond is a star: the island fills with blossom in January and February.

## Taking an ensaïmada home

- The octagonal box travels fine as **hand luggage**.
- Buy it on your last day at a long-established bakery, such as **Ca'n Joan de s'Aigo** or **Fornet de la Soca** in Palma. Don't buy it just anywhere: you'll notice.
- If you won't eat it within two days, choose the **plain** one.

## Common mistakes

1. **Eating on the seafront in Magaluf or s'Arenal:** international menus and tourist prices.
2. **Ordering "paella" at a place with laminated photo menus.** It isn't the island's cooking; look for arròs brut or tumbet.
3. **Skipping a celler.** That's where you really eat Mallorcan.
4. **Not trying sobrasada with honey.**
