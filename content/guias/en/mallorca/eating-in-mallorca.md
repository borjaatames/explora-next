**Mallorcan cooking is country and sea, hearty and very good**, but the tourist zones of the bay are full of international menus of frozen paella and pizza. To eat genuine Mallorcan food you have to head inland, to the **cellers** and village restaurants. This guide gives you the keys and the dishes you can't miss.

The rule: the further you get from the Magaluf or s'Arenal seafront and the deeper into the villages (Sineu, Inca, Algaida), the better you eat.

## The essentials

**Ensaïmada.** The spiral pastry par excellence, made with lard (*saïm*). Plain or filled with pumpkin jam (*cabello de ángel*); there's also a savoury one with sobrasada. For breakfast or an afternoon snack.

**Sobrasada.** The spreadable cured pork-and-paprika sausage, with a Protected Designation of Origin. Eaten on its own, with bread, or warm with honey.

**Pa amb oli.** Brown bread with oil, rubbed ramellet tomato, salt and, on top, cheese or ham. Simple and addictive.

**Tumbet.** Layered baked vegetables (potato, aubergine, pepper) with tomato sauce. The star side dish.

**Frit and arròs brut.** The **frit mallorquí** (offal with potato and vegetables) and the **arròs brut**, a soupy "dirty" rice with meat and game, eaten with a spoon.

## Where to eat well

| Place | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Cellers (Inca, Sineu, Algaida) | Traditional Mallorcan cooking |
| Mercat de l'Olivar / Santa Catalina (Palma) | Tapas and fresh produce |
| Village markets (Sineu, Wednesdays) | Local atmosphere and produce |
| Tramuntana villages | Sóller oranges, oil, ice creams |

**Cellers** are old wine cellars turned into restaurants, usually with big barrels along the walls. They close one or two weekdays, and some shut for holidays in August, so phone before you drive out.

## Palma's markets

- **Mercat de l'Olivar** is where Palma residents shop, with tasting and tapas stalls inside. It opens Monday to Saturday from 7:00; it closes at 14:30 on weekdays (with a partial reopening on Friday afternoon until 20:00) and at 15:00 on Saturdays. Closed Sundays.
- **Mercat de Santa Catalina** is smaller and has more bars for tapas. Also closed on Sundays.

## Village market days

Go in the morning: stalls pack up around 14:00.

- **Sineu:** Wednesday. The most authentic, still with livestock.
- **Inca:** Thursday.
- **Santa Maria del Camí:** Sunday.
- **Alcúdia:** Tuesday and Sunday.
- **Pollença:** Sunday.

A Wednesday at Sineu market followed by lunch at a celler in the village is the easiest way to taste the island's cooking in one go.

## To drink

Wines from the DO **Binissalem** (with the Manto Negro grape) and **Pla i Llevant**, increasingly good. As a digestif, the **Mallorcan herb liqueur** (*hierbas*: sweet, dry or mixed), very typical.

## Sweets and seasonal

Besides the ensaïmada, the **gató** (almond sponge served with ice cream), and at Easter the **robiols** and **crespells**. The almond is a star (the island fills with blossom in January-February).

## Taking an ensaïmada home

- The octagonal box travels fine as **hand luggage**.
- Buy it on your last day at a bakery with a long track record, such as **Ca'n Joan de s'Aigo** or **Fornet de la Soca** in Palma.
- If you won't eat it within two days, choose the **plain** one; filled ones keep less well.

## Times and booking

- **Lunch** runs from 13:30 to 15:30 and **dinner** from 20:30. Outside those hours, village bars usually serve only snacks.
- **July and August:** book always, even for lunch. The good places on the coast fill up days in advance.

## What we don't recommend

1. **Eating on the Magaluf or s'Arenal seafront.** International menus and tourist prices.
2. **Ordering "paella" at a place with laminated photos.** It's not the island's cooking; look for arròs brut or tumbet.
3. **Skipping a celler.** It's where you really eat Mallorcan.
4. **Driving to a celler without calling.** Closing days vary and many shut in August.

## Common mistakes

> **⚠️ Warning:** the slip-ups we see most.

1. **Staying only on the tourist coast to eat.** Inland improves everything.
2. **Not trying sobrasada with honey.** A surprising combination.
3. **Buying ensaïmada just anywhere.** Buy it from a well-regarded bakery; you can tell.
