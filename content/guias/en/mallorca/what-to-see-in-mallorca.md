## Mallorca in three days or a week: how long and when to go

**Two or three days** cover the essentials: one for Palma, one for the Tramuntana (Valldemossa, Sóller and the viewpoints) and one for the caves and coves of the east. **A week** adds the north and unhurried beach time. Less than two days falls short.

| Season | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **May-June and September-October** | Ideal: warm sea, calm coves, lower prices |
| **July and August** | Overcrowded and expensive: book everything |
| **January and February** | Almond blossom, but much of the coast closed |
| **March, April and November** | Hiking in the Tramuntana, with a cold sea |

## What to see in Mallorca by area

### Palma de Mallorca

- The **Cathedral (La Seu)**, Gothic and overlooking the sea, with Gaudí's intervention and Miquel Barceló's ceramic chapel. Beside it, the **Almudaina Palace**.
- The **old town** of grand courtyards, the **Passeig del Born** and the **Arab Baths**.
- **Castell de Bellver**, a circular castle with views over the bay.
- For food and going out, the **Santa Catalina** district and its market.

### The Serra de Tramuntana

A World Heritage Site for its landscape of terraces and olive groves:

- **Valldemossa** and the **Charterhouse** where Chopin and George Sand spent a winter.
- **Deià**, an artists' village perched above the sea, and the **Sa Foradada** viewpoint.
- **Sóller** and its **port**, linked by a classic tram; from Palma you get there on the **1912 wooden train**.
- **Fornalutx**, one of the prettiest villages in Spain.
- The **Sa Calobra** road, with its "tie-knot" hairpin, dropping to the **Torrent de Pareis**. Spectacular and extremely twisty: if you get carsick, go on an excursion.

### Drach Caves and the coves of the east

The **Drach Caves** (Porto Cristo) hold one of the largest underground lakes in Europe, with a classical music concert from boats. Nearby are postcard coves: **Mondragó** (a natural park), the iconic **Caló des Moro**, **Cala Varques** and, to the south, the long, wild **Es Trenc**. The small ones have tiny car parks: arrive **before 9:00** or go out of season.

### The north: Alcúdia and Formentor

The walled town of **Alcúdia**, with Roman Pollentia beside it; the family beaches of **Muro and Alcúdia**; **Pollença**, with the 365 Calvari steps; and **Cape Formentor**, the lighthouse at the end of the range.

| Place | Price | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palma Cathedral | €10, free under 10; terraces €20 extra, guided and booked | Mon-Sat 10:00-17:15 (18:15 in summer); closed to tourists on Sundays |
| Almudaina | €7, free for EU citizens Wednesday and Thursday afternoons | Closed Mondays |
| Castell de Bellver | €4, free on Sundays; bus 4 or 20 min on foot | Closed Mondays |
| Sóller train | €30 return; tram to the port extra, €10 per trip | Departures 10:10, 10:50, 12:15, 13:30 and 15:10 |
| Drach Caves | €18.50 online (€11.50 ages 3-12); **book, it sells out** | Entries at 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16 and 17 h from April to October; four in winter |
| Cape Formentor | 15 May to 18 October (10:00-22:00), cars only until the beach car park is full; lighthouse for authorised vehicles only | TIB bus 334 from Alcúdia and Port de Pollença, or boat |

On **2 February and 11 November**, at sunrise, the sun draws "the eight" of the rose window inside the cathedral (it opens at 8:00).

## Getting there and getting around

- **By air:** Palma airport is **8 km from the centre** (bus or taxi).
- **By ferry:** from Barcelona, Valencia or Dénia (3 h 30 min to 8 h), ideal if you want to bring a car.
- **Hire car:** almost essential, as public transport reaches the Tramuntana and the coves poorly. It costs **€30-60 a day**; in summer book weeks ahead and request a child seat in advance.
- **Public transport:** **TIB** buses leave Palma's intermodal station for the whole island (€2-10) and trains to Inca, Manacor and Sa Pobla cost €3-5.

## Where to stay and what to eat

**Palma** is the most versatile base; the **Tramuntana** (Sóller, Deià) suits mountains and charming villages; the **north** and **east** (Porto Cristo, Cala d'Or) are best for family beaches. Avoid Magaluf or s'Arenal unless you've come for package sun and parties.

Country and sea cooking: **ensaïmada**, **sobrasada**, **pa amb oli**, **tumbet**, **frit mallorquí**, **arròs brut**, **Binissalem** wines and herb liqueur as a digestif. For genuine Mallorcan food, look for *cellers* and village restaurants inland (Sineu, Algaida), not the bay's seafront.

## Tips and common mistakes

1. **Don't stay only on Palma bay or in Magaluf:** you miss the Tramuntana, the coves and the villages.
2. **Book the car early in summer:** it sells out and prices soar.
3. **Don't try to see everything in two days.** The island is large: pick areas.
4. **Don't skip the Tramuntana**, which sets Mallorca apart from any beach destination.
5. **Avoid the small coves at midday in August** and don't turn up at the **Drach Caves without booking**.
