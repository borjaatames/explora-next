**Mallorca has some of the most beautiful coves in the Mediterranean**, but also the worst parking problem in Spain in summer. This guide sorts them by area, tells you which have services and which are wild, and how to get there without fighting for a spot. The key: go early or out of season.

Not all are for the same thing: some are long and family-friendly, others small and photogenic (and jammed), and a few wild without a single beach bar. Choose by the day.

## The essentials

**Es Trenc (south).** The most famous wild beach: white sand and Caribbean water, no buildings, beside the salt flats. Hardly any services; bring shade and water.

**Cala Mondragó (south-east).** Inside a **natural park**, with two calm beaches (s'Amarador and Mondragó). Turquoise water and a protected setting; ideal for families.

**Caló des Moro (south-east).** The cove of the photos: tiny, wedged between rocks, with impossible water. Beautiful but it **gets jammed**; the parking is minuscule and you climb down steps.

**Sa Calobra and Cala Tuent (Tramuntana).** Spectacular coves at the foot of the mountains, at the end of the most vertiginous road on the island. Arrive early or come by boat. From Sa Calobra, an easy path through two rock tunnels leads to the mouth of the **Torrent de Pareis** gorge.

**Playa de Muro / Alcúdia (north).** The great family beach: fine sand, long and shallow, with all the services.

## How to avoid the queues

> **Tip:** the small coves (Caló des Moro, Cala Varques) have minimal parking. Arrive **before 9:00** or **after 18:00**, or go by boat/excursion. At midday in August there's no room even for a towel.

## Getting there: parking and access rules

- **Formentor (2026 rules):** from 15 May to 18 October, 10:00 to 22:00, private cars may drive to the Formentor beach car park only until it is full; then a barrier closes the road. The stretch to the lighthouse is for authorised vehicles only, with fines for anyone else. The alternative is TIB bus line 334 from Alcúdia and Port de Pollença, with free parking on the Ma-2200 service road in Port de Pollença. Stopping on the road outside marked bays is also fined. Another option is the speedboat trip from Alcúdia, which stops at the beach and passes below the lighthouse.
- **Caló des Moro:** the streets leading to the cove are reserved for residents' vehicles. Park at the entrance to Cala Llombards and walk about 1.5 km (20–25 minutes); the final descent is over rocks. Not for pushchairs or reduced mobility.
- **Sa Calobra without the bends:** the boat from Port de Sóller (45–60 minutes under the cliffs; depending on the return sailing, the outing takes about 4 to 7 hours) saves you the 12 km of the island's most twisting road.
- **Es Trenc and Mondragó:** the paid car parks closest to the sand fill by late morning in July and August. Never leave the car on the verge or a private track.

## Water, prices and what to bring

- **Sea temperature:** 24–27 °C from July to September; below 20 °C before June.
- **Jellyfish** arrive with the easterly (levante) wind. Check the lifeguard flags before going in.
- **Sun loungers:** two loungers and a parasol cost €18–30 a day on the beaches with services. On the wild ones there isn't even water: bring 2 litres per person and a parasol.
- **Nudism** is accepted on the quieter stretches of Es Trenc, Cala Varques and part of Cala Mesquida.

## What we don't recommend

1. **Going to the small coves at midday in summer.** No parking and overflowing.
2. **Expecting services at the wild ones.** Es Trenc has only three kiosks and Cala Varques none; bring water and shade.
3. **Stepping carelessly on the posidonia.** The underwater meadows (protected by law across the Balearics) are what give the crystal-clear water.
4. **Climbing down to Caló des Moro with reduced mobility.** It's a descent over rocks and steps.

## Common mistakes

1. **Driving to the Formentor lighthouse in summer.** From 10:00 to 22:00 that stretch is barred to private cars; take bus 334.
2. **Forgetting water shoes.** Some coves have rocks or pebbles.
3. **Not checking the wind.** Depending on the direction, one cove or another will be sheltered or choppy.

## In one sentence

The best coves in Mallorca range from the wild Caribbean of Es Trenc to the turquoise of Mondragó and the drama of Sa Calobra; the difference between enjoying or enduring them is going early.
