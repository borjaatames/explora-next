## Altea and the Peñón de Ifach in Calpe

**Altea** is one of the prettiest whitewashed towns on the coast, with cobbled streets and a church with **blue domes** looking out to sea. Right next door, **Calpe** is dominated by the **Peñón de Ifach**, a natural park you can climb. They are side by side: together they make a better plan than separately.

- **By TRAM:** line 1 to Benidorm, then change to **line 9**, a slower, scenic train that runs every hour. Ticket to Calpe, **€6-7**.
- **Climbing the Peñón:** 1 h 30 min on a path with a final stretch on rock. You need a **free advance booking** on the natural park's website (limited numbers) and it closes when it rains.
- **What to bring:** closed shoes (no flip-flops) and 1.5 litres of water. Avoid midday in summer.

## Guadalest and the Fuentes del Algar

One hour away by car, **Guadalest** is a mountain village with a castle perched on the rock and postcard views over a **turquoise reservoir**. It is one of the most visited inland villages in Spain.

- **Getting there:** by car, on the winding CV-70 from Benidorm. Paid car parks at the entrance to the village (**€3-5**).
- **Opening hours:** the castle and museums open from **10:00 to 18:00-20:00** depending on the season.
- **Combine it:** the village is small and 2 hours is plenty. 15 minutes away are the **Fuentes del Algar** (**€5-6** entry), where you can swim in cold-water pools.

## Benidorm

**1 h 10 min by TRAM** (45 minutes by car) is the Mediterranean's most famous **skyline**, with huge beaches (Levante and Poniente) and theme parks. It is a very different experience from the rest of the Costa Blanca: ideal with kids or for a lively beach day.

- **Parks:** Terra Mítica (from **€29**), Aqualandia (from **€30**) and Mundomar. Bought online a few days ahead they are **10-20% cheaper**; book them for bank holidays and summer.
- **Shuttle buses** run to the parks from central Benidorm.

## Elche and Jávea

- **Elche**, **20 minutes by Cercanías** (line C-1, every 30 minutes), is home to the **Palm Grove**, a World Heritage Site that is free to walk around. The **Huerto del Cura** costs **€6**. The Misteri is only performed on **14 and 15 August**.
- **Jávea (Xàbia)**, one hour away on the AP-7, offers coves, the Cap de Sant Antoni and a charming old fishing quarter. There is no practical public transport from Alicante: you need a **car**. In August the coves (Granadella, Portitxol) close car access once the car park fills up, mid-morning.

## Getting around the Costa Blanca and tips

- **TRAM line 1** leaves **Luceros station every 30 minutes** and links Alicante with El Campello, Villajoyosa and Benidorm. Single ticket to Benidorm, **around €4**. It saves you driving and parking on the north coast, but on summer weekends it is packed: allow extra time.
- **A car** gives you more freedom for Guadalest, Jávea and the inland villages.
- **Don't combine two distant trips** on the same day.
- **Don't underestimate the heat** in Guadalest and on the Ifach at midday in summer.
