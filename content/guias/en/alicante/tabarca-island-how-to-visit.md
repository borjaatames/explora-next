## What to see and do on Tabarca Island

- **The walled village.** The 18th-century walls (Charles III repopulated the island with Genoese families), the church of San Pedro y San Pablo, the Torre de San José and the lighthouse.
- **The beach.** The stretch of sand between the village and the rest of the island, with calm water. It has a lifeguard and showers in summer.
- **Snorkelling in the marine reserve.** The main attraction: posidonia meadows and fish in crystal-clear water. Bring a mask and snorkel.
- **A walk round the island.** It is tiny (**1.8 km**) and you can walk all of it, with rocky coves on the wild side. There are no cars.

## Tabarca boat: prices and timetable

- **From Alicante (Kontiki):** daily departures at **10:45 and 12:15**; returns from Tabarca usually at **16:00 and 17:30** (Mondays, 16:00 and 17:00). Kontiki lists **€24** return per adult, free for under-5s; booked online it costs **from €25** with an open return.
- **From Santa Pola:** **from €9** return, about 30 minutes' crossing and departures every hour in season (fewer outside it).
- **Watch the return:** the ticket is a return with the same company. Check the time of **its last boat**, which from Alicante can be at 17:00 and earlier out of season. Confirm the times at the ticket office when you collect your tickets.

## Tabarca marine reserve rules

- **No fishing, collecting shells or pulling up posidonia**, and no anchoring outside the buoys. There are patrols and fines.
- With an **easterly wind (levante)** the water gets cloudy; with a **westerly (poniente)** it is perfect for snorkelling.
- The coves on the east side have no lifeguard.

## Where to eat

The local dish is **caldero**: a soupy rice with rockfish and all i oli. There are restaurants in the village; in high season, **book ahead**. If you want the island almost to yourself, there are a couple of small hotels: at 19:00, when the last boat leaves, Tabarca is a different place.

## Tips

- **When to go:** **May to October** for swimming and snorkelling. In **July and August** it gets crowded: take the first boat. In spring and autumn it is quieter, but some services close.
- **Bring cash:** there is **no cash machine** and several bars only take cash. There is no pharmacy either.
- **Bring your own shade:** water, a cap and sunscreen. Outside the village there is hardly any shade and the reflection off the water burns twice as fast.
- **Bring your snorkel and water shoes** for the rocky coves.
- Tabarca works well as the second day of [what to see in Alicante](/en/guides/alicante/what-to-see-in-alicante).
