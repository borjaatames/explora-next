## Day 1: Alicante in one day

| Time | What |
| --- | --- |
| 9:30 | Santa Bárbara Castle (lift from the Postiguet) |
| 11:30 | Santa Cruz quarter, San Nicolás and Santa María |
| 13:00 | Explanada de España and the port |
| 14:00 | Rice lunch |
| 16:30 | MARQ, MACA or Mercado Central |
| 18:00 | Swim at the Postiguet or TRAM to San Juan |

**Santa Bárbara Castle**, on Mount Benacantil, is one of the largest medieval fortresses in Spain, with 360° views over the bay. Go up before the heat, and in summer take the lift: walking up at midday is a mistake.

**The Santa Cruz quarter** runs down from the castle with white houses, flowerpots and steep lanes. Pass the **Co-cathedral of San Nicolás** and the **Basilica of Santa María**, the city's oldest church.

**The Explanada**, the palm-lined promenade with its wavy mosaic of six and a half million tiles, leads you to the port. In the afternoon, the **MARQ** (Archaeological Museum, awarded European Museum of the Year) or the **MACA** (Picasso, Dalí, Miró, free), and a swim to round off the day.

## Day 2: Tabarca Island or the Costa Blanca

The boat to **Tabarca Island** leaves from the port: Spain's smallest inhabited island and a marine reserve, with crystal-clear water for snorkelling, a walled village and rice dishes by the sea. The last boat back to Alicante leaves at **17:30** (Mondays, 17:00) and **it is cancelled when the sea is rough**: check first. Timetables and tips in the [Tabarca guide](/en/guides/alicante/tabarca-island-how-to-visit).

## When to go, getting there and getting around

- **When:** **April-June and September-October** are ideal. From **November to March** it has one of the warmest winters in Spain. July and August are great for the beach but middays are tough.
- **Festivals:** the **Hogueras de San Juan (20-24 June)** are the big one: papier-mâché monuments that are burnt, mascletàs and fireworks, like the Fallas in summer. If you're not coming for them, accommodation prices soar. There is also Holy Week in the old town and **Moors and Christians** in the province's towns (Alcoy, between April and May).
- **Getting there:** **AVE** high-speed train from Madrid in 2 h 20 min (the station is a 15-minute walk from the centre); from Valencia, under 2 hours. From the **airport**, a bus to the centre takes 20 minutes.
- **Getting around:** the centre is walkable and the **TRAM** links it with San Juan beach and runs up the coast to Benidorm. You don't need a car.

## Where to stay and where to eat

Stay in the **centre** (Explanada, old town, port): everything is walkable and there's nightlife. **San Juan beach** is ideal for a long, quiet beach stay, 15-20 minutes by TRAM. Avoid developments far from the TRAM unless you have a car.

Alicante is **rice** country, and not just paella:

- **A banda**, cooked in fish stock.
- **Del senyoret**, with everything peeled.
- **Soupy (caldoso)** and **with a crust (con costra)**.

Add **salted fish** (mojama, hueva), coca and **Jijona turrón**. Avoid the rice restaurants on the port front with photos on the menu: one or two streets towards the old town and you'll eat a proper rice. And don't order seafood paella expecting the best: here a banda and senyoret rule.

## Tips

- **Don't treat Alicante as a stopover airport** or confuse it with Benidorm.
- **Go up to the castle early** and, in summer, take the lift.
- **Don't skip Tabarca**, but check the sea before you go.
- **Use the TRAM** for San Juan beach and the north coast.
