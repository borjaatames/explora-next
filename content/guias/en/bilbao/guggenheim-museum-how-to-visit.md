**The Guggenheim isn't just a museum: it's the building that changed Bilbao.** Frank Gehry's titanium work, opened in 1997, turned an industrial city into a global design icon, the famous "Bilbao effect". So the visit has two parts you shouldn't confuse: **the building and its sculptures (free, outside)** and **the galleries (paid, inside)**. This guide helps you make the most of both.

Which one fits:

- **You speak Spanish:** the €49 tour is 1.5-2 hours with an art historian, skip-the-line ticket included, and you can stay in the museum until closing afterwards.
- **You want English or another language:** the VIP tour caps groups at 8 (often 2-4), runs in a single language with no translating, and covers the exterior and the galleries in 2-2.5 hours.
- **On your own:** the included audio guide is good. A guide earns its price mainly if modern art usually leaves you cold.

Both tours have free cancellation up to 24 hours before.

The golden rule: even if you don't go in, the exterior alone is worth the trip. And if you do go in, head for what really matters, not "seeing everything".

## What to see outside (free)

The building itself, reflected in the river, is the attraction. Around it, with no ticket:

- **'Puppy'**, Jeff Koons' giant flower dog, at the main entrance.
- **'Maman'**, Louise Bourgeois' bronze spider, by the river.
- Koons' **'Tulips'** and Anish Kapoor's **'Tall Tree & the Eye'**.
- Yves Klein's **fire fountain**, which lights up at dusk, and Fujiko Nakaya's **fog sculpture**, which wraps the building at set times (ask at the information desk; it's usually hourly). Watch both from the riverside walkway.

**Tip:** the best time for the exterior is **sunset**, when the titanium turns golden and the colours reflect in the water. Walk to the opposite bank of the river for the classic full-building photo.

## What to see inside

If you go in, prioritise:

- **Richard Serra's 'The Matter of Time'**, on the ground floor: enormous sheets of curved steel you walk through. It's the must-see permanent work and the most impressive.
- The **atrium**, the spectacular 50-metre central void.
- The **temporary exhibitions**, which rotate and are usually top-tier. Check the listings before going: they can tip the balance of the visit, and they also set the ticket price.

Allow **2-2.5 hours** inside. The audio guide is included and worth it.

## Tickets and hours

| Detail | Info |
| --- | --- |
| Adult ticket | **€12-18**, depending on the exhibitions on view (€12 as of October 2026) |
| Reduced | Half price for students aged 18-25 and over-65s |
| Under-18s | **Free**, but they still need a ticket |
| Combo with the Fine Arts Museum | €16 adults, €8 reduced (as of autumn 2026) |
| Audio guide | Included with every ticket |
| Hours | Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00-19:00; until 20:00 at Easter and in summer |
| Mondays | Closed, except in summer and on some holiday Mondays |
| Always closed | 25 December and 1 January |

- **Buy online** on the official website with a time slot and walk straight in. The box office works, but in high season and at weekends there's a queue. Issued tickets are non-refundable.
- **Best time:** first thing on a weekday morning, or after 17:00. Saturday around midday is the busiest.
- **Cloakroom:** free and compulsory for backpacks and umbrellas. Pushchairs are allowed.
- **Getting there:** metro to Moyua (lines 1 and 2) and a 7-minute walk, or the tram to the Guggenheim stop by the door. From the Old Town it's a 20-minute walk along the river.

## How to combine it

The Guggenheim is in the Ensanche, by the river, a stroll from the Old Town. Five minutes away is the **Fine Arts Museum**, excellent and far less visited. If you like art, it's the perfect pairing, and the combo ticket saves money; the Fine Arts Museum reopened on 6 October 2026 after an expansion closure. We slot both in as the first stop in [Bilbao in one day](/en/guides/bilbao/bilbao-in-one-day).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Thinking you have to go in to enjoy it.** The exterior and the sculptures are free and spectacular.
2. **Buying the ticket at the box office in season.** An unnecessary queue; book online.
3. **Trying to "see everything" in a rush.** Head for Serra, the atrium and the temporary show that interests you.
4. **Going on a Monday** without checking the calendar.
5. **Not checking the temporary exhibition.** It can be the best of the visit or of no interest to you.
6. **Skipping the Fine Arts Museum for lack of time.** It's next door and well worth it.
7. **Paying for children.** Under-18s are free; just add their tickets at no cost.

## In one sentence

The Guggenheim is enjoyed free outside (Puppy, Maman and the titanium at sunset) and with criteria inside, with Serra's 'The Matter of Time' as the great must-see. It's the heart of any visit to [Bilbao](/en/guides/bilbao/bilbao-in-one-day).
