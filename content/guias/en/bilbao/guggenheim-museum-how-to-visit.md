The Guggenheim offers two visits you shouldn't confuse: **the building and its sculptures, free on the outside**, and **the galleries, ticketed, on the inside**. Even if you don't go in, the outside is worth the trip.

## What to see inside the Guggenheim Museum

Don't try to "see everything" in a rush. Prioritise:

- **The Matter of Time, by Richard Serra**, on the ground floor: huge sheets of curved steel you walk through. It's the essential permanent work and the most impressive.
- **The atrium**, the spectacular 50-metre central void.
- **The temporary exhibitions**, which rotate and are usually first-rate. Check what's on before you go: it can tip the balance of your visit.

Allow **2-2.5 hours** inside. The audio guide is included and worth it.

## Puppy and the free outside

The building itself, reflected in the estuary, is the attraction. Around it, without a ticket:

- **Puppy**, Jeff Koons's giant flower dog, at the main entrance.
- **Maman**, Louise Bourgeois's bronze spider, by the estuary.
- **Tulips**, by Koons, and **Tall Tree & The Eye**, by Anish Kapoor.
- Yves Klein's **fire fountain**, lit at nightfall, and Fujiko Nakaya's **fog sculpture**, which wraps the building at set times (usually every hour; ask at the desk). Watch them from the riverside walkway.

The best time for the outside is **sunset**, when the titanium turns golden and the colours reflect in the water.

## Guggenheim tickets and opening hours

- **Standard ticket:** **€12-18** depending on the exhibitions (€12 in October 2026), audio guide included. Reduced for students and over-65s. **Under-18s free.**
- **Buy online with a time slot** and walk straight in; the box office works, but there's a wait in high season.
- **Opening hours:** Tuesday to Sunday, **10:00 to 19:00**, until 20:00 in summer.
- **Closed on Mondays**, except in summer (15 June to 7 September), Easter and some public holidays; always closed on 25 December and 1 January.
- **Best time:** first thing on a weekday or after 17:00. Saturday midday is the busiest.
- **Cloakroom:** free and compulsory for backpacks and umbrellas. Pushchairs are allowed.

## Getting there and what to combine it with

- **Metro:** Moyua (L1 and L2) and a 7-minute walk.
- **Tram:** Guggenheim stop, right at the door.
- **On foot:** 20 minutes from the Old Town along the estuary.

The museum is in the Ensanche district. Five minutes away is the **Fine Arts Museum (Bellas Artes)**, excellent and far less visited: if you like art, it's the perfect pairing. We fit it in as the first stop in [Bilbao in one day](/en/guides/bilbao/bilbao-in-one-day).

## Tips and common mistakes

1. **Thinking you have to go in to enjoy it.** The outside and the sculptures are free and spectacular.
2. **Buying at the box office in high season.** An unnecessary queue: book online.
3. **Not checking the temporary exhibition.** It may be the highlight or not interest you; find out first.
4. **Going on a Monday** without checking the calendar.
5. **Skipping the Fine Arts Museum for lack of time.** It's next door and well worth it.
6. **Forgetting under-18s get in free.** Don't buy extra tickets for the family.
