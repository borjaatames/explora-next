## Tickets sold out: try another time on the official site

Official sales are on **guggenheim-bilbao.eus**, and the museum recommends buying only there to avoid fraud. The Guggenheim almost never runs out of places for the whole day: what fills up, especially at weekends, Easter, in summer and on rainy days, is **the time you wanted**.

- **Look at other times on the same day.** First thing in the morning or late afternoon there's usually more room than between 11:00 and 14:00.
- **Buy once your date is fixed:** the museum **doesn't refund tickets once issued**.

## Prices and opening hours

On the day we checked:

- **Standard:** €12.
- **Reduced:** €6, for students aged 18 to 26, over-65s and pensioners, with ID at the box office.
- **Free:** under-18s.
- **All tickets include an audio guide.** Prices may vary with the exhibitions, so check the price for your chosen day.
- **Joint ticket with the Fine Arts Museum (Bellas Artes):** €16 for adults.

**Opening hours:** Tuesday to Sunday, **10:00 to 19:00**. At Easter and in summer (15 June to 13 September in 2026) it closes at **20:00**, and on 24 and 31 December at **17:00**.

**Closed on Mondays**, except those listed in its calendar: for the rest of 2026, **7 and 28 December**. It's also closed on **25 December and 1 January**.

## Guggenheim box office queues: when to go

If you can't find a slot online, the box office sells while there's capacity. It closes **half an hour before the museum** (18:30 on normal hours) and the galleries are cleared a quarter of an hour before closing. In high season, go at the **10:00 opening**: that's when it's quietest.

A tip if you're cutting it fine: on the **last day of each temporary exhibition**, from **16:00 to 19:00**, tickets are **50% off**. It does tend to be busier that day, though.

## Guided tour with entry: the last-minute option

When the official site doesn't have the time you want, operators usually have places because they book their allocations in advance. You pay more than for the ticket alone, but you go in with the group at a fixed time and someone explains what you're looking at. The guided tour with entry starts **from €49**, and the VIP small-group tour **from €95**.

Before paying, check that the listing says **"museum entry included"** and look at the cancellation policy: some tours only cover the outside.

## If you can't get in: the free outdoor artworks

Even without a ticket, you don't miss out on the building. Several works from the collection are outside and free to see:

- **Puppy**, by Jeff Koons, the flower dog in the entrance square.
- **Maman**, Louise Bourgeois's giant spider, on the terrace by the estuary.
- **Fog sculpture**, by Fujiko Nakaya, by the estuary: it comes on **every hour on the hour for eight minutes**.
- **Tall Tree & The Eye**, by Anish Kapoor, 73 mirrored steel spheres on the pond.

Walking around the museum along the riverside takes **half an hour**, and the **La Salve bridge** gives you the best view of the building.

## Tips and mistakes to avoid

1. **Buying on websites that imitate the official one.** The museum itself warns about ticket fraud. The official one is guggenheim-bilbao.eus.
2. **Turning up on a Monday without checking.** It's closed on almost every Monday of the year.
3. **Arriving at 18:30 expecting to get in.** The box office has already closed and the galleries empty at 18:45.
4. **Buying without a fixed date.** Issued tickets aren't refunded.
