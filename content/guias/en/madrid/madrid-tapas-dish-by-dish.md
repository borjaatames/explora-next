If you only have time for seven stops in Madrid, here's the short list: **Las Bravas** (bravas), **El Brillante** (bocadillo de calamares), **Casa Dani** (tortilla), **El Pedrusco de Aldealcorvo** (torreznos), **Malacatín** (callos), **Lhardy** (ham) and **Casa Julio** (croquetas). Each does one dish better than almost anyone in the city. To fit them into a longer trip, see [Madrid in 3 days](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-in-3-days) or [Madrid on a budget](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-on-a-budget).

## Quick summary: what to order and where

| Dish | Place | Area | What to order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patatas bravas | Las Bravas | Sol (Pasaje Matheu, 5 and Callejón del Gato, 3) | A ración of bravas, no alioli |
| Bocadillo de calamares | El Brillante | Atocha (Pza. Emperador Carlos V, 8) | The regular one, no lemon or mayo, standing |
| Tortilla de patatas | Casa Dani | Mercado de la Paz (Ayala, 28) | A ración of tortilla, before 2 PM |
| Torreznos | El Pedrusco de Aldealcorvo | Chamberí (Juan de Austria, 27) | Whole torreznos, not diced into taquitos |
| Callos a la madrileña | Malacatín | La Latina (Ruda, 5) | Callos; at lunchtime, the cocido |
| Ham and cured meats | Lhardy | Sol (Carrera de San Jerónimo, 8) | Iberian ham at the standing counter |
| Croquetas | Casa Julio | Malasaña (Madera, 37) | A mixed half ración |

## What it costs and how it works

- **Prices:** ración of bravas €5–8; bocadillo de calamares €4–6; a slice (pincho) of tortilla €3–4.50; torreznos €7–10; callos €10–14; acorn-fed ham €18–28 per 100 g. A caña (small beer) €2–3; house wine or tap vermouth €3–4. A crawl of four or five bars: **€25–35 per person**.
- **The free tapa with your drink** has almost disappeared from the center (you'll get a few olives or crisps at most); you still see it in Lavapiés, Chamberí and Argüelles.
- **Timing:** bars are packed from 1:30 to 3:30 PM and from 9 to 11 PM; between 4 and 8 PM many kitchens close. On Sundays, vermouth in La Latina runs until about 5 PM.
- **Bar or table:** at the classics you eat standing; a table costs 10–20% more. **Tip** by leaving your change. **Cards** are accepted almost everywhere. **Book** only at the sit-down restaurants: Cava Baja on a Friday at 9:30 PM without a booking means an hour's wait.

## Dish by dish

**Bravas: Las Bravas.** Its brava sauce has been a registered trademark since **1959**, and much of what Spain now calls "salsa brava" descends from it: genuinely spicy, tomato-free, paprika-based, over irregular chunks of fried potato. Two locations 200 meters apart, same recipe: if one has a queue, try the other. Standing only. If the bar isn't on Pasaje Matheu or Callejón del Gato, it isn't Las Bravas, whatever the sign says.

**Calamari sandwich: El Brillante.** Next to Atocha since **1961** and full at any hour; the bread holds the oil without going soggy. You eat at the counter. For a more old-school, cheaper version, try Casa Rúa or La Ideal by Plaza Mayor.

**Tortilla: Casa Dani.** Inside the Mercado de la Paz (Salamanca district), open Monday to Friday **7 AM–8 PM** and Saturday 7 AM–5 PM; closed Sunday. They make hundreds a day, so yours is fresh from the pan: juicy, almost runny in the center, with onion by default. There's a queue at 2 PM and by mid-afternoon it can be sold out.

**Torreznos: El Pedrusco de Aldealcorvo.** Originally from Soria; this asador uses Iberian pork cured for two months, roasted in a wood oven and fried to order. Order them whole, not diced. Central alternative: **Casa Mortero** (Zorrilla, 9), served with patatas meneás. Both are sit-down restaurants, not bars: book at weekends.

**Callos: Malacatín.** Open since **1895**, fourth generation; closed **Tuesdays**. At weekday lunchtime, order the cocido served in three courses (soup, chickpeas, meats), best broth on the 2022 Cocido Route. Portions are generous; book at weekends. Alternative: **Casa Lucio** (Cava Baja, 35), famous for huevos estrellados and with callos just as serious.

**Ham: Lhardy.** Open since **1839**, with a ground-floor counter where you eat standing: hand-carved Iberian ham, cured meats and hot consommé in a cup. To sit down with a full acorn-fed menu from Jabugo, **Cinco Jotas** has its own restaurants (Jorge Juan, Padre Damián, Santa Ana); pricier.

**Croquetas: Casa Julio.** Made fresh daily, in flavors well beyond ham: morcilla with quince, spinach with raisins and gorgonzola, wild mushroom with leek. Order a mixed half ración. For **cod croquetas**, go to **Casa Labra** (Tetuán, 12), open since 1860 and where the PSOE was founded in 1879: standing at the bar only, daily 11:30 AM–3:30 PM and 6:30–10:30 PM, with a fast-moving queue. Don't order ham croquetas there.

## Prefer tapas by neighborhood?

- **La Latina and Cava Baja:** Malacatín and Casa Lucio, plus Posada de la Villa (Cava Baja, 9; roast lamb) and Taberna La Chata (Cava Baja, 24; free tapa with every drink).
- **Malasaña:** Casa Julio and **Bodega de La Ardosa** (Colón, 13; since 1892), with tap vermouth and a tortilla that rivals Casa Dani's.
- **Chueca:** on Gravina, Bodega Ángel Sierra (tap vermouth with pickles) and El Cisne Azul (mushrooms); Bocaíto (Libertad, 4–6) for pintxos; La Trastienda in the Mercado de San Antón for a dozen croqueta flavors.
- **Salamanca:** pricier and sit-down: Jurucha (Ayala, 19), La Maruca (Velázquez, 54), Sagardi (Castellana, 13).

## What to skip and common mistakes

- **Bars with a laminated photo of bravas or calamares** in the window.
- **Display-case tortilla** without asking when it was made; many central bars still have the previous lunch's.
- **Sol on a Saturday at 2 PM:** Las Bravas, Casa Labra and El Brillante all have queues. Go at 12:30 PM or after 4 PM.
- **Callos on a tourist set menu:** they take hours to cook, so these are reheated.
