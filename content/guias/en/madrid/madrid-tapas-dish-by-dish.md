## Dish by dish: bravas, calamari, tortilla, torreznos, callos and croquetas

**Where to eat patatas bravas in Madrid: Las Bravas.** Its sauce has been a registered trademark since 1959, and much of what Spain calls "salsa brava" descends from it: genuinely spicy, tomato-free, over irregular chunks of potato. Two locations 200 meters apart, same owner; if one has a queue, try the other. Standing only, in and out. If the bar isn't on Pasaje Matheu or Callejón del Gato, it isn't Las Bravas.

**Calamari sandwich: El Brillante.** Next to Atocha since 1961 and full at any hour. The key is the bread, which holds the oil without going soggy. You eat at the counter; for something more old-school and cheaper, Casa Rúa or La Ideal by the Plaza Mayor.

**Tortilla: Casa Dani.** Inside the Mercado de la Paz, Monday to Friday 7:00-20:00 and Saturdays until 17:00. They make hundreds a day, so yours has just come out of the pan: juicy, almost runny in the center. It comes with onion by default. There's a queue at 14:00 and by mid-afternoon it may have run out.

**Torreznos: El Pedrusco de Aldealcorvo.** Torreznos come from Soria, but Madrid adopted them, and this asador treats them like its suckling pig: Iberian pork cured for two months, roasted in a wood oven and fried to order. Central alternative: **Casa Mortero** (Zorrilla, 9), with patatas meneás. Both are restaurants, not bars: book at weekends.

**Callos a la madrileña: Malacatín.** Since 1895, fourth generation, same recipe; open every day (Sunday to Tuesday, until 17:00 only). At weekday lunchtime, the cocido in three courses is one of the most honest meals in Madrid (best broth on the 2022 Cocido Route). Generous portions; book at weekends. Alternative: **Casa Lucio** (Cava Baja, 35), famous for huevos estrellados and with callos just as serious.

**Ham: Lhardy.** Since 1839, with a ground-floor counter where you eat standing: hand-carved ham, cured meats and consommé in a cup. To sit down with an acorn-fed menu from Jabugo, **Cinco Jotas** (Jorge Juan, Padre Damián, Santa Ana), pricier.

**Best croquetas in Madrid: Casa Julio.** A béchamel that holds together and flavors beyond ham: morcilla with quince, spinach with gorgonzola, wild mushroom. Order a mixed half ración. For **cod croquetas**, go to **Casa Labra** (Tetuán, 12), from 1860 and where the PSOE was founded; standing at the bar, 11:30-15:30 and 18:30-22:30, with a short, fast queue.

## What it costs and how it works

- **Prices:** ración of bravas €5-8; bocadillo de calamares €4-6; a pincho of tortilla €3-4.50; torreznos €7-10; callos €10-14; acorn-fed ham €18-28 per 100 g. A caña €2-3; house wine or tap vermouth €3-4. A crawl of four or five bars: **€25-35 per person**.
- **The free tapa with your drink** has almost disappeared from the center (a few olives or crisps); you still see it in Lavapiés, Chamberí or Argüelles.
- **Timing:** bars are packed from 13:30 to 15:30 and from 21:00 to 23:00; between 16:00 and 20:00 many kitchens close. On Sundays, vermouth in La Latina until 17:00.
- **Bar or table:** at the classics you eat standing; a table costs 10-20% more. **Tip** by leaving your change. **Cards** are accepted almost everywhere. **Book** only at sit-down restaurants: Cava Baja on a Friday at 21:30 without a booking means an hour's wait.

## Prefer tapas by neighborhood?

- **La Latina and Cava Baja:** Malacatín and Casa Lucio, and on the same street Posada de la Villa (roast lamb) and La Chata (free tapa with your drink). On Sunday at midday, the most traditional vermouth.
- **Malasaña:** Casa Julio and **Bodega de La Ardosa** (Colón, 13), from 1892, with tap vermouth and a tortilla that rivals Casa Dani's.
- **Chueca:** on Gravina, Bodega Ángel Sierra (vermouth and pickles) and El Cisne Azul (mushrooms); Bocaíto (Libertad, 4) for pinchos; La Trastienda, in the Mercado de San Antón, a dozen different croquetas.
- **Salamanca:** pricier and sit-down. Jurucha (Ayala, 19), La Maruca (Velázquez, 54), Sagardi (Castellana, 13).

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Be wary of bars with a photo of bravas in the window**, and of display-case tortilla if you don't know when it was made.
- **Avoid Sol on a Saturday at 14:00:** Las Bravas, Casa Labra and El Brillante all have queues. Go at 12:30 or after 16:00.
- **Skip callos on a tourist set menu:** they take hours to cook, so at any hour they're reheated.
- **Don't look for a table at Las Bravas:** it's a standing bar. And don't confuse it with any bar serving bravas.
- **Don't go to Casa Dani mid-afternoon:** the tortilla runs out.
- **Book at Malacatín or Casa Lucio** at weekends.
- **At Casa Labra, order cod**, not ham croquetas.
