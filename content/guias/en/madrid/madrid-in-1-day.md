One day in Madrid isn't enough for everything, but it is enough for what sets the city apart: **the Prado**, Habsburg Madrid and the best sunset in the centre. The key is to walk it **east to west**, from the Prado to the Temple of Debod, without doubling back and with barely any metro.

## The day at a glance

| Time | Plan | Getting there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10:00-12:30 | Prado Museum | — |
| 12:45-14:00 | Barrio de las Letras | 5 min walk |
| 14:00-15:30 | Lunch in the streets off Huertas | — |
| 15:30-16:15 | Puerta del Sol and Plaza Mayor | 10-15 min walk |
| 16:15-16:45 | San Miguel market and Plaza de la Villa | 3 min walk |
| 16:45-18:00 | Royal Palace and Almudena Cathedral | 10 min walk |
| 18:00-19:00 | Sunset at the Temple of Debod | 15 min walk |
| 19:15-20:30 | Plaza de España and Gran Vía to Callao | 20 min walk |
| 21:00 | Dinner in La Latina | 15 min walk or metro |

The afternoon timings are set for November, when the sun goes down around 18:00. April to September, flip the ending: Gran Vía first and Debod at sunset, after 21:00.

## Morning: the Prado

**10:00 — Prado Museum**, at opening. Open Monday to Saturday 10:00-20:00 and Sundays and holidays 10:00-19:00. Two and a half well-spent hours: *Las Meninas* and the Velázquez room, Goya's *Black Paintings*, Bosch's *Garden of Earthly Delights*, El Greco and Titian. Don't try to see it all. Arriving by train? Atocha is 10-15 minutes away on foot.

[actividad: museo-prado-entrada]

If you'd like someone to take you straight to the key works and explain them, a guided visit with entry starts at €49; privately, with an official guide and hotel pick-up, there's a private Prado visit from €146.

**12:45 — Barrio de las Letras**, five minutes across the Paseo del Prado: lines by Cervantes and Lope de Vega set into the pavement of Calle Huertas, Plaza de Santa Ana and the outside of Lope de Vega's house.

## Lunch

**14:00.** In the streets crossing Huertas (Echegaray, Ventura de la Vega, Prado), not on Huertas itself. A €14-18 set menu or tapas at the bar. Madrid lunches from 14:00 to 16:00.

## Afternoon: Sol, Plaza Mayor and the Royal Palace

**15:30 — Puerta del Sol** (10 minutes' walk): kilometre zero and the bear statue, ten minutes and move on. **Plaza Mayor**, five minutes along Calle Mayor: enter through the Arco de Cuchilleros and don't sit on its terraces.

**16:15 — San Miguel market** (half an hour to browse; eating here is expensive) and **Plaza de la Villa**, the oldest square in the centre.

**16:45 — Royal Palace.** October to March it opens Monday to Saturday 10:00-18:00 and Sundays until 16:00, and the ticket office shuts an hour before closing: get there before 17:00. Tickets €18; EU and Ibero-American citizens go in **free Monday to Thursday 16:00-18:00** (17:00-19:00 April to September), queuing at the ticket office. Allow 45-60 minutes inside, then **Almudena Cathedral** opposite and the Sabatini Gardens next door. For the whole afternoon with a local guide who shows you the corners guidebooks miss:

[actividad: paseo-privado-madrid-secretos-imprescindibles]

**18:00 — Temple of Debod**, 15 minutes' walk along Calle Ferraz. A 2nd-century BC Egyptian temple with the Royal Palace and the mountains behind; arrive 30-45 minutes before sunset. Free.

## Evening: Gran Vía and La Latina

**19:15 — Gran Vía.** From Debod, walk down to Plaza de España and along the Gran Vía to Callao (20 minutes) with the buildings lit up. If your legs give out, a private tuk-tuk does the old centre and Gran Vía in 1 h 15 (from €79 per tuk-tuk):

**21:00 — Dinner in La Latina**: Cava Baja and Cava Alta, classic tapas and huevos rotos. Madrid doesn't dine before 9 pm. If you'd rather go with a guide, there's a tapas walk through three taverns starting at Plaza de Santa Ana; and to end the day with flamenco, Tablao Las Carboneras is a few steps from Plaza Mayor (from €45):

## Variations

- **No museums:** swap the Prado for the **Retiro** (free; the Crystal Palace and the boating lake, 1 h 30) and the Puerta de Alcalá; the rest of the day stays the same.
- **With children:** the Retiro with rowing boats (€4-6 per half hour) instead of the Prado, the Royal Palace with its armoury, and Debod. More in [Madrid with kids](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-with-kids).
- **If it rains:** the Prado in the morning, the Thyssen (€14) after lunch, the Royal Palace at the end of the day and an early dinner in La Latina.
- **Mondays:** everything on this route is open; the Reina Sofía closes on Tuesdays, which doesn't affect this plan.

## Common mistakes

1. **Visiting the Prado in the free hours** on a one-day trip: queues and packed rooms.
2. **Reaching the Royal Palace at 17:30** in winter: the ticket office has closed.
3. **Eating on Plaza Mayor** or in the San Miguel market.
4. **Taking the metro for 10-minute hops:** walking is quicker in the centre.
5. **Dinner at 8 pm** in an empty restaurant.

## In a nutshell

From the Prado to Debod on foot, with the Royal Palace late in the day and dinner in La Latina: a day that explains Madrid without silly rushing.
