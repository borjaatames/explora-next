Madrid is one of the few European capitals where you can spend several days paying almost no entry fees and still see the best of it. The trick isn't ticking off a list of free things: it's knowing which ones are worth it, at what time, and the few things worth paying for.

## Free museums

### Museums with free hours

| Museum | Free hours |
| --- | --- |
| Prado | Mon–Sat 6–8 PM; Sun and holidays 5–7 PM |
| Reina Sofía | Mon and Wed–Sat 7–9 PM; Sun 12:30–2:30 PM |
| Thyssen-Bornemisza | Mon 12–4 PM (permanent collection) |
| Arqueológico, Sorolla, Antropología, Museo del Traje | Sat from 2 PM and Sun mornings |
| Real Academia de Bellas Artes de San Fernando | Wednesdays |
| Fundación Mapfre | Mondays |
| Lázaro Galdiano | Last hour of the day |

The state museums share the same pattern (Saturday afternoon, Sunday morning), so you can combine two in one weekend. The Real Academia is the other central museum with Goyas, and almost nobody goes.

At the Prado, the free slot saves you €15 but means queues and packed rooms. If you'll only see it once in your life, pay and walk in at 10 AM ([how the three art museums compare](/en/guides/madrid/prado-vs-reina-sofia-vs-thyssen)). If you have no other option, arrive 30 minutes before the free hour starts.

### Always free

- **Templo de Debod:** a 2nd-century BC Egyptian temple, a gift from Egypt. Go at sunset; at midday it's a temple between brick walls.
- **Faro de Moncloa:** 92 meters, 360° views by elevator, free since 2024.
- **Museo del Romanticismo** (Chamberí): a 19th-century mansion with its rooms recreated. One hour is enough; closed Mondays.
- **CaixaForum:** the Herzog & de Meuron building and its vertical garden are free; some temporary shows charge.
- **Conde Duque** and **Sala Alcalá 31** (rotating contemporary exhibitions) and the **Museo Geominero** (minerals and fossils in a striking building).

## Viewpoints and outdoor spaces

- **Templo de Debod and Parque del Oeste at sunset:** Royal Palace, the sierra and an orange sky. If Debod is crowded, walk 50 meters up into the park for the same view.
- **Cerro del Tío Pío** (Vallecas; metro Portazgo or Buenos Aires, then 10 minutes' walk): the whole skyline from the southeast at sunset.
- **Retiro:** the Crystal Palace and Velázquez Palace (free Reina Sofía exhibitions), the rose garden in May and October, the Fallen Angel statue, the Bosque del Recuerdo memorial to the 11 March 2004 victims, and the boating lake (boats €4–6 are the only paid part).
- **Madrid Río:** 30 km of bike path along the Manzanares with Royal Palace views; start at Puente del Rey (metro Príncipe Pío).
- **Casa de Campo:** 1,700 hectares of woodland (five times Central Park), with a lake and terraces (metro Lago). The cable car isn't worth it.
- **El Capricho** (metro El Capricho): the city's most beautiful and least-visited park, an 18th-century garden with a pond, a small temple and a Civil War bunker. **Weekends and holidays only.**
- **Sabatini Gardens and Campo del Moro:** the Royal Palace gardens, both free; Campo del Moro, entered from Paseo de la Virgen del Puerto, is nearly empty. The Botanical Garden (€6) isn't free.

## Recurring free events

- **Veranos de la Villa** (July–August): free concerts, open-air cinema and dance across the city. **Conde Duque** and **Matadero** run free or nominally priced concerts and cinema all year.
- **Fundación Juan March:** free concerts and talks in an excellent auditorium.
- **El Rastro** (Sunday mornings, La Latina) and the **Mercado de Motores** (second weekend of each month, at the Railway Museum): go for the atmosphere more than the shopping.
- **Andén 0**, Chamberí's ghost metro station from 1919: free, Friday afternoons and Saturdays.

## Neighborhoods to walk

- **Barrio de las Letras:** literary quotes set in the pavement, Plaza de Santa Ana, and the Valle-Inclán mirrors on Callejón del Gato.
- **Lavapiés:** the most diverse neighborhood; best on a Saturday afternoon.
- **Malasaña:** Plaza del Dos de Mayo, Calle Espíritu Santo, Calle Pez.
- **Chamberí:** Plaza de Olavide and the market; our favorite for a calm walk.
- **Conde Duque:** Plaza de las Comendadoras at sunset.
- **Madrid de los Austrias:** Plaza Mayor, Plaza de la Villa, Plaza del Cordón, without going into anything.

## Common mistakes

1. **Seeing the Prado only in the free slot** without expecting the crowds.
2. **Visiting Debod at midday.**
3. **Doing every free plan around Sol:** the free and distinctive stuff is in Chamberí, Lavapiés and La Latina.
4. **Parks at 2 PM in July and August:** go 9 AM–noon or after 7 PM.
5. **Confusing free with cheap:** a €30 paella on Plaza Mayor undoes the savings. Eat a €10–12 set lunch in Chamberí, a calamari sandwich in a neighborhood bar, or picnic in Retiro.
6. **"Free entry" clubs in Sol and Gran Vía:** €10 for the first drink in a mediocre venue. A neighborhood bar pours a beer for €2.50.

## What is worth paying for

1. **The Prado at 10 AM (€15).** The best ticket in Madrid: Velázquez with nobody in front of you.
2. **El Escorial and the Valley of Cuelgamuros** (€14 and €9 entry). Fifty kilometers away and a different world. Without a car, day tours with all transport run €50–70.
3. **An honest flamenco show (€30–40),** not the Plaza Mayor dinner shows: see [which tablao to choose](/en/guides/madrid/flamenco-madrid-which-tablao-to-choose).
4. **The Círculo de Bellas Artes rooftop (€5):** better views than any trendy rooftop bar, for a fraction of the price.
