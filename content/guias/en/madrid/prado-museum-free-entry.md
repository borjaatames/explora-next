The Prado Museum has free hours every single day, and half of Madrid knows it. So the useful question isn't just when, but **how long you'll queue and what you'll have time to see**. Here are the free entry times checked on the official website, who always gets in free and how to make the most of the hour and a half you get inside.

## Prado Museum free hours

| Day | Free hours | Last entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday to Saturday | 18:00 to 20:00 | 19:30 |
| Sundays and public holidays | 17:00 to 19:00 | 18:30 |
| 6 January, 24 and 31 December | Closes at 14:00 | — |
| 1 January, 1 May and 25 December | Closed | — |

Three things the table doesn't tell you:

- **Only the permanent collection is free.** Temporary exhibitions aren't included, though they're half price during those hours.
- **Entry closes half an hour before closing** and the galleries are cleared 10 minutes before closing. Go in at 18:15 and you have just under an hour and a half.
- **There's no weekly closing day.** It's open on Mondays too, which is a good day for the free hours.

## Who gets in free at any time

If you're in one of these groups, you don't need the free hours: you can go in whenever you like. According to the museum's website:

- Under-18s.
- Students aged 18 to 25.
- Unemployed visitors and working teachers.
- People with a disability of 33% or more.
- Large families as defined by the museum (one adult with three children, or two if one has a disability).
- Official tour guides, journalists and some professional associations.

You need to **show valid proof at the box office** on the day. Over-65s, youth card holders and other large-family members pay the reduced rate, **€7.50**.

## Free days and El Prado de noche

On a few days the Prado doesn't charge at all. The usual ones are **18 May** (International Museum Day), **12 October** and **19 November**, the museum's anniversary; this year the 19th is a Thursday. The museum confirms them each year on its website, so check before planning your trip around them. Those are the longest queues of the year.

The lesser-known option is **El Prado de noche**: on the **first Saturday of every month**, from **20:30 to 23:30**, the Jerónimos building opens for free, with entry through the **Jerónimos Gate** and last entry at 23:00. There are no tickets: it's first come, first served until capacity is reached. The next one is **Saturday 7 November**.

## The real queue: when to get there

The free-hours queue forms by the **Goya Gate**, on the north side of the building, where the box office is. It starts well before 18:00 and on Sundays, holidays and long weekends the wait can **go past an hour**.

- **Weekdays:** arrive at least **30 minutes early**. Monday to Thursday, outside bank holidays, is usually your best bet.
- **Sundays and holidays:** expect a longer wait and less museum time, as it closes at 19:00.
- **Don't turn up at 18:00 sharp.** By the time you reach the box office you've lost a good chunk of your time.

## What to see in the free two hours

In practice you get an hour and a quarter to an hour and a half. Try to see everything and you'll see nothing. This short route covers the essentials:

1. **Velázquez** (floor 1): *Las Meninas*, *The Spinners* and the court portraits. Start here: it's the room that fills up most.
2. **Goya** (floor 1): *The Family of Charles IV* and the two *Majas*.
3. **Goya on floor 0:** *The Second of May*, *The Third of May* and the **Black Paintings**, including *Saturn*.
4. **Bosch** (floor 0): *The Garden of Earthly Delights*. Give it ten minutes; it's a painting to look at up close.
5. **If you have time left:** Van der Weyden's *Descent from the Cross* or El Greco's *Nobleman with his Hand on his Chest*.

Pick up the free floor plan at the entrance and mark these works before you start.

## Do you need to book free entry online?

**No.** The official site, `entradas.museodelprado.es`, sells paid timed tickets. Free-hours tickets are **handed out at the box office until capacity is reached**, so there's no way to secure them in advance. Be wary of any site offering to "book the Prado free hours" for a booking fee.

## Common mistakes

1. **Assuming free hours include the temporary exhibition.** Permanent collection only; temporary shows are half price.
2. **Going on the afternoon of 24 or 31 December or 6 January.** It closes at 14:00 on those days.
3. **Forgetting your ID, student card or certificate** if you qualify for free entry.
4. **Using the free hours for your only visit to the Prado.** If this is your first and maybe last time, an hour and a half in crowded rooms falls short.

## The paid option: €15 and no queue

General admission is **€15** on the official website and at the box office, and there's no time limit once you're in. At 10:00, with the museum half empty, you'll see in three hours what the free slot never allows. If the official site doesn't have the time you want, the resold [Prado Museum entry ticket](/en/cities/madrid/activities/prado-museum-entry-ticket) starts at €18, and the [guided tour with entry](/en/cities/madrid/activities/prado-museum-guided-tour) at €49. What to do if it sells out and how the box office works is in our [Prado Museum tickets guide](/en/guides/madrid/prado-museum-tickets-sold-out).

## Other free museums near the Prado

Two more museums with free hours are a five-minute walk away:

| Museum | Free hours |
| --- | --- |
| Reina Sofía (*Guernica*) | Monday and Wednesday to Saturday, 19:00 to 21:00; Sundays, 12:30 to 14:30 |
| Thyssen-Bornemisza | Mondays, 12:00 to 16:00 (permanent collection) |

On a Monday you can combine the free Thyssen at midday with the Prado from 18:00. The Reina Sofía's free hours overlap with the Prado's, so save it for another day. If you'd rather pay for one, compare them in [Prado vs Reina Sofía vs Thyssen](/en/guides/madrid/prado-vs-reina-sofia-vs-thyssen). ## In one sentence

The Prado is free from 18:00 to 20:00 Monday to Saturday and 17:00 to 19:00 on Sundays, with no booking and a queue at the Goya Gate: arrive half an hour early, head straight for Velázquez, Goya and Bosch, and if it's your only visit, pay the €15 and go in the morning.
