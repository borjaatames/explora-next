## Royal Palace Madrid tickets: price and opening hours

Tickets are sold with a time slot on Patrimonio Nacional's official website, **tickets.patrimonionacional.es**:

- **General:** €18 (plus a **€0.77** booking fee per ticket online). It includes the **Royal Armoury**.
- **Reduced:** €9, ages 5 to 16, over-65s and students up to 25.
- **Free:** children under 5.
- **Official guided visit:** €26, led by Patrimonio Nacional staff, in Spanish, English or Italian. English tours run Monday to Saturday at 10:45, 11:45, 13:45 and 15:15.
- **Palace + Royal Collections Gallery:** €24. The Gallery ticket is valid seven days before or after your palace visit.
- **Palace + Royal Kitchen:** €24, adding the palace's historic kitchens.

**Opening hours:** October to March, Monday to Saturday 10:00 to 18:00; April to September, until 19:00. **Sundays, 10:00 to 16:00.** Last entry is one hour before closing.

**Closed:** 1 and 6 January, 1 May and 25 December. It can also close at short notice for official events.

## Royal Palace Madrid free entry

If you are an **EU or Latin American citizen or resident**, entry is **free Monday to Thursday** in the last two hours:

- **16:00 to 18:00** from October to March.
- **17:00 to 19:00** from April to September.

It is only available at the **ticket office**, with proof of nationality or residence, and there is usually a queue: arrive early, because the ticket office closes an hour before the palace. On **18 May**, International Museum Day, entry is free for everyone.

## If Royal Palace Madrid tickets are sold out

At Easter, on bank holidays and at weekends some slots show "low availability" or sell out. Unlike other monuments, there is almost always a way in on the same day. Try these in order:

1. **Check other times on the same day.** The self-guided visit takes about 45 minutes, so a later slot won't wreck your plans. And look ahead: in October 2026 the site was already selling dates up to January 2027.
2. **Try the other options** on the same site (official guided visit and combined tickets), which have their own time slots.
3. **Go to the ticket office.** It sells same-day tickets and closes an hour before the palace. Since 20 August 2026 the entrance is via the Almudena Cathedral esplanade and the Plaza de la Armería. In high season the queue grows by mid-morning: go at opening time or late in the day.
4. **Book a tour with entry included** if you don't want to take the risk. Operators book tickets in advance and sell you the visit with a set time; it costs more than the ticket alone (from €36), but includes a guide and you skip the queue. Check that the listing says **"Royal Palace entry included"**: some Habsburg Madrid walks only pass the facade.

## What you can see without a ticket

Even if you don't go in, the palace area fills a morning:

- The **Changing of the Guard**, Wednesdays and Saturdays from 11:00 to 14:00 (10:00 to 12:00 in July and August) at the Puerta del Príncipe, Calle Bailén. It is free.
- The **solemn changing ceremony**, with cavalry and band, on the first Wednesday of each month at 12:00, except January, August and September.
- The **Sabatini Gardens** and the **Campo del Moro**, both free. From the Campo del Moro, entered from Paseo de la Virgen del Puerto, you see the west facade from below, and it is usually almost empty.
- The **Plaza de Oriente** and, at sunset, the **Temple of Debod**, with the palace in the background.

## Tips

- **Buy only on the official website.** Many sites with similar names add surcharges. The official one is tickets.patrimonionacional.es.
- **Don't leave free entry to the last minute.** The ticket office closes an hour before the palace and the queue is long.
- **Bring ID.** It is required for free and reduced tickets.
- **Leave big backpacks and suitcases at the hotel.** Because of building works, the official site asks visitors not to bring large bags.
