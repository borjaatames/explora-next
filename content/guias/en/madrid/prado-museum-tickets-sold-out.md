## What you'll see

The Prado grew out of the royal collections of the Habsburgs and Bourbons, and that shapes its character: it holds the world's finest collection of **Velázquez, Goya and El Greco**, with outstanding works by Bosch, Titian and Rubens. It covers European painting from the 12th to the 19th century.

Must-sees: *Las Meninas* and the Velázquez room, Goya's *Black Paintings* and Bosch's *Garden of Earthly Delights*. Allow **2-3 hours**; once inside there's no time limit.

## Prado Museum ticket price and opening hours

The official ticket site is **entradas.museodelprado.es**, linked from museodelprado.es.

- **General:** €15.
- **Reduced:** €7.50 (over-65s, youth card holders and large families).
- **Always free:** under-18s, students aged 18 to 25 and the unemployed, with proof at the box office.

**Hours:** Monday to Saturday **10:00 to 20:00**, Sundays and holidays **10:00 to 19:00**. On 6 January and 24 and 31 December it closes at 14:00. There's no weekly closing day, but it **closes on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December**.

## If Prado Museum tickets are sold out

The Prado sells timed tickets and almost never runs out for the whole day. What happens, especially on long weekends, weekends and Easter, is that **the slot you wanted is sold out**. Try in this order:

1. **Other times on the same day** on the official site. Each slot has its own quota, and going in at 12:00 instead of 10:00 doesn't cost you museum time. Don't count on last-minute releases: if you see a free slot, buy it.
2. **The Goya Gate box office**, which sells same-day tickets at the same price (see below).
3. **A resale ticket or a guided tour.** Authorised resellers buy quotas in advance and usually have space: a timed ticket from €18, a [guided tour with ticket](/en/cities/madrid/activities/prado-museum-guided-tour) from €49, or a private tour with an official guide from €146. Check the listing says **"Prado Museum entry included"**: some walks only pass the façade.

If you're going for a **temporary exhibition**, buy ahead and check exactly what your ticket includes.

## Free entry and El Prado de noche

The permanent collection is **free in the last two hours of every day**: Monday to Saturday **18:00 to 20:00** and Sundays and holidays **17:00 to 19:00**. Temporary exhibitions are half price in those hours. Entry closes **half an hour earlier**, so you get an hour and a half.

Free tickets can't be booked: they're handed out **at the box office until capacity is reached**. The queue starts long before and on Sundays and holidays can exceed an hour. Bring a short list: Velázquez, Goya and Bosch, not much more. It's also free all day on **18 May** (International Museum Day) and **19 November** (the museum's anniversary). Full details in [Prado Museum free entry](/en/guides/madrid/prado-museum-free-entry).

**El Prado de noche:** on the **first Saturday of every month**, from **20:30 to 23:30**, some galleries open for free, with entry through the Jerónimos Gate until capacity is reached.

## Getting there, box office and queues

The museum is on the Paseo del Prado, a **10-15 minute walk from Atocha**.

- **Box office:** next to the **Goya Gate**, on the north side of the building. In high season the queue builds by mid-morning: go at opening time or in the early afternoon.
- **With your ticket on your phone** you go in through the **Jerónimos Gate**, skipping the box office.

## Tips

- **Buy only from the official site.** Many websites with "Prado" in the name overcharge.
- **Don't turn up for the free hour at 18:00 sharp:** the queue is already long and entry closes at 19:30 (18:30 on Sundays).
- **Bring ID** if you're entering free or with a reduced ticket.
- **If you're seeing all three big museums,** the Paseo del Arte pass includes one visit to the permanent collections of the Prado, the Reina Sofía and the Thyssen within a year: it cost €32.80 at the box offices in 2025, and from €38 online. To choose, see our [Prado vs Reina Sofía vs Thyssen comparison](/en/guides/madrid/prado-vs-reina-sofia-vs-thyssen).
