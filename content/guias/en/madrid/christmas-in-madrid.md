## Madrid Christmas lights: how to see them

This year there are more than **250 illuminated spots** and 14 million LEDs, with light designs drawn by Madrid schoolchildren.

- **On foot:** Sol, Preciados, Gran Vía and Calle Alcalá to Cibeles, then Serrano. About two unhurried hours.
- **Naviluz:** an open-top bus of about 50 minutes, departing hourly from 18:00 to 22:00 and cheaper from 21:00. Tickets are sold at naviluz.es and **can't be changed or cancelled**, not even if it rains. It doesn't run on 24 and 31 December or 5 January.
- **By private tuk-tuk:** an hour and a half for up to 4 people, with no queues or waiting in the cold. It's open-sided: wrap up warm.

## A December evening: Plaza Mayor market, Sol and Cortylandia

1. **17:30, Plaza Mayor:** the market in the last daylight, before it fills up.
2. **18:15, Puerta del Sol:** the Real Casa de Correos nativity scene (there's a queue, but it moves) and the tree.
3. **19:00, Preciados:** Cortylandia, and from there to Gran Vía after dark, when the lights look their best.
4. **20:00, chocolate and churros at San Ginés**, in the Pasadizo de San Ginés by Sol. Open since 1894, it runs 24 hours from Thursday to Sunday and on the eve of public holidays.
5. **21:00, dinner:** tapas in the centre or a flamenco show.

## Traditions, New Year's Eve and Three Kings

- **The Lottery draw (22 December):** at the Teatro Real, with free numbered tickets collected at the box office on showing your ID or passport. Some people queue from the night before: in recent editions, arriving after 4 a.m. meant missing out.
- **The Doña Manolita queue:** Madrid's most famous lottery shop, with hours-long queues in December. For the photo, go first thing on a weekday.
- **The preúvas (30 December):** the dress rehearsal for the chimes, with the same grapes and far fewer people. Free, with capacity for about 15,000; in the last edition the square closed at 21:00 and reopened at 22:00.
- **New Year's chimes at Sol (31 December):** access closes when the square is full. Arrive before 21:00 and bring your grapes peeled.
- **Three Kings parade (5 January):** leaves at 18:00 from Plaza de San Juan de la Cruz, goes down the Castellana and Recoletos and ends at Cibeles around 21:00. Find a spot on the Castellana: at Cibeles the crowd blocks the view.

## Things to do in Madrid at Christmas with kids and on public holidays

**With kids:**

- **Cortylandia** is the number one plan: free and next to Sol. Go on a weekday afternoon.
- **Naviluz** is half price for children aged 7 to 11, and under-7s ride free, though they need a ticket.
- The **ice rink** in Plaza de España works in time slots: buy your ticket in advance on its website.

**Museums:** the Prado collection is free in the last two hours of each day, with limited capacity. December is a good month for the **Royal Palace** and museums, with shorter queues than in spring. With a free day, **Toledo and Segovia** are the classic day trip, and in winter you avoid the heat.

- **On 6 January** the Prado opens from 10:00 to 14:00, and the Reina Sofía and the Royal Palace are closed.
- **Sol metro station, 30 and 31 December:** it closes from 18:00 for the chimes rehearsal and the New Year's Eve chimes. Get off at Ópera, Callao or Sevilla and walk.
- **Shops and restaurants:** on the 24th and 31st most places close mid-afternoon, and few shops open on the 25th and the 1st. Book your dinner.
- **The Royal Palace** can also close for official events: check the Patrimonio Nacional website the day before.

## Common mistakes

- **Going on a December Saturday afternoon:** Sol and Preciados are gridlocked. Better a Tuesday or Wednesday.
- **Leaving dinner on the 24th or 31st until the last minute:** many restaurants close or only offer a set menu. Book in November.
- **Booking Naviluz for a doubtful-weather day:** there are no refunds if it rains. Better a plan you can cancel.
