## Seeing the lights

This year there are more than **250 lit-up spots** and 14 million LEDs, including light designs drawn by Madrid schoolchildren.

- **On foot:** Sol, Preciados, Gran Vía and Calle Alcalá down to Cibeles, then Serrano. About two hours at an easy pace.
- **Naviluz:** an open-top bus, about 50 minutes, leaving every hour from 18:00 to 22:00, and cheaper from 21:00. Book at naviluz.es; tickets **can't be changed or cancelled**, not even if it rains. No service on 24 and 31 December or 5 January.
- **By private tuk-tuk:** an hour and a half for up to 4 people, with no queuing in the cold. It's open-air, so wrap up.

## A December evening, done right

1. **17:30, Plaza Mayor:** the market in the last of the daylight, before it fills up.
2. **18:15, Puerta del Sol:** the nativity scene at the Real Casa de Correos (there's a queue, but it moves) and the tree.
3. **19:00, Preciados:** Cortylandia, then on to Gran Vía once it's dark, when the lights look their best.
4. **20:00, chocolate and churros at San Ginés**, in the Pasadizo de San Ginés by Sol. It has been open since 1894 and runs 24 hours from Thursday to Sunday and on the eve of public holidays.
5. **21:00, dinner:** tapas in the centre or a flamenco show.

If you'd rather not walk that much in the cold, the **night panoramic bus** covers the route for you, with a guide.

## Three traditions you won't see on postcards

- **The Christmas lottery draw (22 December):** it takes place at the Teatro Real and the public gets in free, with a numbered ticket collected at the box office on showing ID. Some people queue from the night before: in recent editions, arriving after 4 a.m. meant missing out.
- **The preúvas (30 December):** the dress rehearsal for the New Year chimes, with the same grapes and far fewer people than on the 31st. It's free, with a capacity of about 15,000; at the last edition the square closed at 21:00 and reopened at 22:00.
- **The Doña Manolita queue:** Madrid's most famous lottery shop, with queues that last hours in December. If you just want the photo, go first thing on a weekday.

## With kids

- **Cortylandia** is plan number one: free and right by Sol. Go on a weekday afternoon.
- **Naviluz** is half price for children aged 7 to 11, and under-7s ride free, though they still need a ticket.
- The **ice rink** at Plaza de España works in time slots: buy your ticket in advance on its website.

## Museums and monuments on public holidays

The **Prado** is closed on 25 December and 1 January, and on 24 and 31 December and 6 January it opens only from 10:00 to 14:00. In the last two hours of each day the collection is free, subject to capacity. December is a good month for the **Royal Palace** and the museums, with shorter queues than in spring.

If you have a free day, a trip to **Toledo and Segovia** with the Alcázar is the classic getaway, and in winter you do it without the heat.

## New Year's Eve and Three Kings

- **Midnight chimes in Sol:** access closes once the square is full. Arrive before 21:00 and bring your twelve grapes peeled.
- **Three Kings Parade:** starts at 18:00 at Plaza de San Juan de la Cruz, comes down the Castellana and Recoletos and ends at Cibeles around 21:00. Get a spot on the Castellana: at Cibeles the crowds block the view.

## Common mistakes

- **Going on a December Saturday afternoon:** Sol and Preciados are gridlocked. Go on a Tuesday or Wednesday.
- **Leaving Christmas Eve or New Year's Eve dinner to the last minute:** many restaurants close or only serve a set menu. Book in November.
- **Booking Naviluz for a rainy day:** there are no refunds. If the forecast is doubtful, pick a plan you can cancel.
