## Madrid 3 day itinerary at a glance

| Day | Focus | Getting around |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Prado, Barrio de las Letras, Habsburg Madrid and Debod | On foot |
| 2 | El Escorial and the Valley of Cuelgamuros | Cercanías, bus or car |
| 3 | Retiro, Chamberí and Reina Sofía | On foot and metro |

## Day 1: the historic core and the Prado

**10:00 — Prado Museum**, on the dot at opening: Velázquez, Goya, El Greco, Bosch, Titian and Rubens with the rooms almost empty. **Ticket €15** (reduced €7.50; free under 18 and students up to 25); buy online to skip the queue. The last two hours (18:00-20:00) are free but packed with groups. Two and a half intense hours, not four half-hearted ones. If you're torn between the Prado, Thyssen and Reina Sofía, see [our comparison](/en/guides/madrid/prado-vs-reina-sofia-vs-thyssen).

**13:00 — Barrio de las Letras**, five minutes away: quotes from Cervantes, Quevedo and Lope in the pavement, Plaza de Santa Ana and Plaza del Ángel, and Valle-Inclán's mirrors in the Callejón del Gato. Eat in the side streets off Huertas, not on Huertas.

**16:30 — Plaza Mayor** (15 minutes and a photo) and the Mercado de San Miguel (pricey and touristy; half an hour for the atmosphere). Then the **Royal Palace**, the largest in Western Europe: about 45 minutes on your own, and **free for EU and Latin American citizens Monday to Thursday** in the last two hours (16:00-18:00 from October to March and 17:00-19:00 from April to September). The Sabatini Gardens next door are free.

**Sunset — Temple of Debod:** a 2nd-century BC Egyptian temple with the Royal Palace and the mountains behind, free. Arrive 45 minutes before sunset.

**Evening:** La Latina (traditional, terraces and classic tapas) or Malasaña (historic bars and a young crowd). Madrid doesn't dine before 21:00.

## Day 2: El Escorial and the Valley of Cuelgamuros

The day that sets this route apart: less than an hour away, two monuments that explain Spain better than any book.

**El Escorial.** Philip II's palace, monastery, basilica, library and pantheon in one building, a World Heritage Site. 2-3 hours; bring a layer, because it's cold inside even in summer.

- **Ticket:** €14 (€7 reduced). Free for EU and Latin American citizens on Wednesdays and Sundays from 15:00, with a queue.
- **Hours:** 10:00 to 18:00 in winter and until 19:00 from April to September. **Closed on Mondays.**
- **Getting there:** Cercanías from Atocha or Chamartín (1 hour; €8.50 return) and a 15-20 minute uphill walk, or bus 661/664 from Moncloa (50 minutes), which stops by the monastery.

**Valley of Cuelgamuros**, 9 km away. Until 2022, the Valley of the Fallen: built between 1940 and 1958 on Franco's orders with forced labour by Republican prisoners, it's now a site of democratic memory holding the remains of more than 33,000 people from both sides; Franco was exhumed in 2019. The basilica carved into the rock is 262 metres long and the cross 150 metres high. Impressive as a work, uncomfortable as history. **Ticket €9** (€4 reduced), closed on Mondays and free for EU citizens on Wednesday and Sunday afternoons.

By car, 15 minutes from the monastery; without one, a local bus from San Lorenzo or a tour that combines both. If you'd rather skip it, head back to Madrid with the afternoon free.

## Day 3: the Madrid locals live in

**Morning — Retiro**, the former royal garden, a World Heritage Site since 2021: the Crystal Palace (free, with Reina Sofía exhibitions), boats on the lake, the Rose Garden, the Fallen Angel and the Forest of Remembrance for the 11-M victims. Two hours.

**Midday — Chamberí**, a 19th-century bourgeois neighbourhood with no tourists: Plaza de Olavide and its terraces, **Andén 0** (the 1919 ghost metro station, free, Friday afternoons and Saturdays), the Chamberí market, Calle Sagasta and Calle Eloy Gonzalo, and Ponzano for a drink.

**Afternoon:** the **Reina Sofía** (€12; *Guernica* and the Spanish surrealists) or a free afternoon to go back to the neighbourhood you liked most. By day three, every traveller wants some unplanned time.

## What not to do in Madrid in 3 days

- **Gran Vía rooftop terraces:** €8 beers in a manufactured setting. The Círculo de Bellas Artes rooftop (€5) has a better view.
- **The Casa de Campo cable car:** long, dull and ends in the middle of nowhere.
- **Puerta del Sol as a destination:** the bear, kilometre zero, ten minutes and move on.
- **San Ginés first thing:** Valor (Postas or Alberto Aguilera) makes better chocolate and churros, with no queue.
- **City-centre tablaos for passing groups:** for real flamenco, see [which tablao to choose](/en/guides/madrid/flamenco-madrid-which-tablao-to-choose).

## Getting around and tips for your Madrid itinerary

- **Transport:** the zone A Tourist Travel Pass costs **€10.30 for one day** and €43.20 for seven (2026), with unlimited metro, bus and Cercanías within the city. It doesn't reach El Escorial (you need zone T, €15.40 a day), so for day 2 a return Cercanías ticket is usually cheaper.
- **Free hours:** the Prado Monday to Saturday 18:00-20:00 and Sundays 17:00-19:00; the Reina Sofía Mondays and Wednesday to Saturday 19:00-21:00 and Sundays 12:30-14:30; the Thyssen on Mondays from 12:00 to 16:00. More in [Madrid on a budget](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-on-a-budget).
- **Closures:** El Escorial and Cuelgamuros on Mondays; the Prado on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.
- **Dinner:** serious restaurants open at 20:00-20:30; at 20:00 the place will be empty, and that doesn't mean it's bad.

With kids, see [Madrid with kids](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-with-kids); with a spare day, [Toledo](/en/guides/madrid/toledo-day-trip-from-madrid), [Segovia](/en/guides/madrid/segovia-day-trip-from-madrid) or [Ávila](/en/guides/madrid/avila-day-trip-from-madrid).
