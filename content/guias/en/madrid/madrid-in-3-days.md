This is the route that works for a first visit: not fifty sights, but three days to understand why Madrid matters, with what's worth your time and what to skip.

| Day | Focus | Getting around |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Prado, Barrio de las Letras, Habsburg Madrid and Debod | On foot |
| Day 2 | El Escorial and the Valley of Cuelgamuros | Cercanías rail, bus or car |
| Day 3 | Retiro, Chamberí and the Reina Sofía | Walking and metro |

## Day 1: the historic core and the Prado

**10:00 — Prado Museum**, at opening: Velázquez, Goya, El Greco and Bosch in near-empty rooms. The free last two hours fill with groups; seeing *Las Meninas* behind six heads isn't worth saving €15.

- **Prices (2026):** €15 general, €7.50 reduced (over 65, youth card, large family), free under 18 and students up to 25.
- **Buy online** to skip the ticket queue, and give it 2.5–3 hours; past that you stop taking anything in.

Torn between the three big museums? See our [Prado vs Reina Sofía vs Thyssen comparison](/en/guides/madrid/prado-vs-reina-sofia-vs-thyssen).

**13:00 — Barrio de las Letras**, five minutes away: quotes from Cervantes and Lope de Vega in the pavement, Plaza de Santa Ana and the Valle-Inclán mirrors on Callejón del Gato. Eat in the side streets off Calle Huertas, not on Huertas itself.

**16:30 — Plaza Mayor, San Miguel and the Royal Palace.** Plaza Mayor needs 15 minutes; the Mercado de San Miguel is pricey but worth 30 minutes for the atmosphere. The **Royal Palace**, the largest in Western Europe, takes about 45 minutes self-guided and is free for EU and Ibero-American citizens Mon–Thu in the last two hours (4–6 PM Oct–Mar, 5–7 PM Apr–Sep). The Sabatini Gardens are free.

**Sunset — Templo de Debod**, a free 2nd-century BC Egyptian temple with the palace and the sierra behind it. Arrive 45 minutes before sunset.

**Evening — La Latina** for terraces and classic tapas, or **Malasaña** for old bars and a younger crowd. Madrid dines from 9 PM; an empty restaurant at 8 PM isn't a bad sign.

## Day 2: El Escorial and the Valley of Cuelgamuros

**El Escorial.** Philip II's palace, monastery, library and royal pantheon in one 16th-century UNESCO-listed building.

- **Prices and hours (2026):** €14 general, €7 reduced (5–16, over 65, students up to 25). 10 AM–6 PM Oct–Mar, 10 AM–7 PM Apr–Sep. **Closed Mondays.**
- **Free** for EU and Ibero-American citizens Wednesday and Sunday from 3 PM, with queues.
- **Getting there:** Cercanías C-3 from Atocha or Chamartín (about an hour), then a 15–20 minute uphill walk. Or bus 661/664 from Moncloa (50 minutes), which stops next to the monastery.
- **Allow 2–3 hours**, and bring a layer: it's cold inside even in summer.

**Valley of Cuelgamuros**, 9 km away. The former Valley of the Fallen was built in 1940–1958 on Franco's orders with forced labour by Republican prisoners; Franco was exhumed in 2019, and it now holds the remains of more than 33,000 people from both sides of the Civil War. The rock-cut basilica is 262 metres long and the cross 150 metres tall: overwhelming as architecture, uncomfortable but necessary as history.

- **Prices:** €9 general, €4 reduced. Closed Mondays. Free for EU citizens on Wednesday and Sunday afternoons.
- **Getting there:** 15 minutes by car. Buses are infrequent, so without a car an excursion combining both sites is easiest.

Skipping Cuelgamuros? Head back to Madrid for a free afternoon.

## Day 3: the Madrid locals live in

**Morning — Retiro Park**, UNESCO-listed since 2021: the Crystal Palace (free, Reina Sofía exhibitions), the boating lake (€4–6 for half an hour), the Rosaleda, the Fallen Angel statue and the Bosque del Recuerdo for the victims of 11 March 2004. Give it two hours.

**Midday — Chamberí**, a 19th-century residential neighbourhood with almost no tourists:

- **Plaza de Olavide**, with terraces full of locals.
- **Andén 0**, a 1919 ghost metro station turned free museum (Friday afternoons and Saturdays).
- **Mercado de Chamberí**, a real neighbourhood market.
- **Sagasta and Eloy Gonzalo** streets for the architecture, and **Calle Ponzano** for drinks and dinner.

**Afternoon —** the **Reina Sofía** (€12; Picasso's *Guernica* and the surrealists) if you still have appetite, or free time in the neighbourhood you liked most.

## What to skip

- **Gran Vía rooftop bars:** €8 beers in a manufactured atmosphere. The Círculo de Bellas Artes rooftop (€5) has a better view.
- **The Casa de Campo cable car:** long, dull, and it drops you where there's little to do.
- **Puerta del Sol as a destination:** the Bear statue and Kilometre Zero take 10 minutes. Walk through.
- **Chocolatería San Ginés:** Valor (Calle Postas or Alberto Aguilera) makes better churros with shorter queues.
- **Tour-bus flamenco:** 45-minute shows for groups. See [which tablao to choose](/en/guides/madrid/flamenco-madrid-which-tablao-to-choose).

## Practical tips

- **Transport:** the Tourist Travel Pass Zone A costs €10.30 for one day and €43.20 for seven (as of 2026), with unlimited metro, bus and Cercanías in the city. It doesn't reach El Escorial (that needs Zone T, €15.40 a day), so for day 2 a Cercanías return is usually cheaper.
- **Free museum hours:** Prado Mon–Sat 6–8 PM, Sun 5–7 PM; Reina Sofía Mon and Wed–Sat 7–9 PM, Sun 12:30–2:30 PM; Thyssen Mon noon–4 PM. More in [Madrid on a budget](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-on-a-budget).
- **Closures:** El Escorial and Cuelgamuros on Mondays; the Prado on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are 3 days enough?** For the essentials at a calm pace, yes.

**El Escorial and Cuelgamuros in one day?** Yes: leave at 8:30 AM, back by 8 PM. Easy by car, tight by public transport.

**What if it rains?** The Prado, Reina Sofía, Thyssen, Royal Palace, CaixaForum and Museo del Romanticismo fill two days indoors.
