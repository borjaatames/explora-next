## Things to do in Segovia

**The aqueduct.** About 28 metres high over the Plaza del Azoguejo, granite without mortar and standing for almost two thousand years. Don't stay at the bottom: climb the steps beside the arches to the **Postigo del Consuelo** to see it at arch level with the city behind. It is free and it is the best photo of the day.

**Calle Real to the Plaza Mayor.** A gentle slope past the **Casa de los Picos** (the diamond-point facade) and the Romanesque church of **San Martín**. You don't need to go into anything; it is the way.

**The Cathedral** (€4, Cathedral and Bishop's Palace; Monday to Saturday 9:30-18:30, Sundays from 13:30). Bright, huge and less ornate than others. If you like heights, the **guided tower visit** (€7, at 10:30, 12:00, 13:30, 15:00 and 16:30) or the **Cathedral and tower pack** (€10, not on Sundays) give the best view of Segovia. With one day, choose: the Cathedral tower or the Alcázar tower.

**The Alcázar** (€7 palace and museum, €5 reduced; €10 with the **Tower of Juan II**). Royal rooms, Mudéjar coffered ceilings and the armoury. The tower is 152 steps and the visit should not exceed 25 minutes, but the view over the Meseta is worth it. Tickets with the tower sell out first at weekends and on bank holidays: buy them on the official website. On Tuesdays from 14:00 to 16:00 the palace is free for EU citizens, at the ticket office and without the tower.

**The view from below.** What almost nobody does: walk down from the Alcázar to the **Alameda del Eresma** and look up. From there, and from the road to the **Vera Cruz** (a twelve-sided Templar church), the Alcázar appears like the prow of a ship. It is the postcard image and you can't see it from inside.

## How to get to Segovia from Madrid

- **Avant from Chamartín:** about **30 minutes**, from about €14 each way. It arrives at Segovia-Guiomar, on the outskirts. First train around 6:30, last return around 21:30 (check Renfe). The first morning trains and last returns fill up at weekends: buy a few days ahead.
- **City bus 11** (Guiomar-aqueduct): 15 minutes and about €2. Count it each way.
- **Avanza bus from Moncloa:** 1 h 15-1 h 20, about €5 each way. The cheapest, and it drops you a few minutes from the aqueduct.
- **Car:** 1 h 15, with tolls if you take the motorway. Leave it outside the old town; traffic is restricted in the centre.
- **Organised tour:** 9-12 hours, from €41. It combines Segovia with Ávila or Toledo; worth it if you don't want to organise anything.

## When to go

- **April-June and September-October:** ideal, with good light and the Alcázar open until 19:30.
- **July and August:** a good choice. At about 1,000 metres, Segovia is several degrees cooler than Madrid or Toledo.
- **November to March:** cold and quiet; the Alcázar closes at 18:00 and it can snow.
- **Weekends and bank holidays:** full of Madrileños. Book lunch and tickets with the tower. Early on a weekday, the aqueduct is yours; at 13:00 on a Saturday, it isn't.

## Where to eat and what to try

**Roast suckling pig** (cochinillo) is why many Madrileños keep coming back. The classic roast houses are by the aqueduct and around the Plaza Mayor (Mesón de Cándido and José María are the best known); they charge per portion, so check the menu before you sit down. If you don't fancy cochinillo, the **judiones de La Granja** (giant beans) are just as Segovian. For dessert, **ponche segoviano**, a sponge cake with custard and toasted marzipan.

## Tips

- **Count the Guiomar bus:** it adds 15 minutes each way.
- **In winter, don't reach the Alcázar at 17:00.** From November to March it closes at 18:00 and the ticket office at 17:30.
- **Don't stay only in the Azoguejo.** The photo of the aqueduct from above and of the Alcázar from below are the best.
- **Bring a coat from October to April**, and don't try to fit Toledo in on the same day on your own: they are in opposite directions.
