Segovia is the easiest day trip from Madrid. It's half an hour by train, the old town runs in a straight line, and it has three sights you won't see anywhere else: a **Roman aqueduct** standing in the middle of town, a **Gothic cathedral** (the last big one built in Spain), and an **Alcázar** that looks like a drawing. It all fits into one day, with a long Castilian lunch in the middle, which locals treat as part of the visit. Here is how we would plan it.

## When to go

| Season | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| April-June and September-October | Best: good light, and the Alcázar is open until 7:30 pm |
| July and August | A good escape: at about 1,000 m, Segovia is several degrees cooler than Madrid or Toledo |
| November to March | Cold and quiet; the Alcázar closes at 6 pm and it can snow. Bring a coat |
| Weekends and Spanish holidays | Busy with day-trippers from Madrid: book lunch and tower tickets |

On a weekday morning you'll have the aqueduct almost to yourself. At 1 pm on a Saturday, you won't.

## How to get from Madrid to Segovia

| Option | Time | Approx. price | What to know |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Avant train from Chamartín | About 30 min | From about €14 each way | Arrives at Segovia-Guiomar, outside town. First train around 6:30 am, last one back around 9:30 pm (check Renfe) |
| City bus 11 (Guiomar to the aqueduct) | 15 min | About €2 | Add it each way |
| Avanza bus from Moncloa | 1 hr 15-1 hr 20 | About €5 each way | Cheapest option; stops a few minutes from the aqueduct |
| Car | 1 hr 15 | Fuel, plus tolls on the motorway | Park outside the old town; the centre has restricted traffic |
| Guided tour | 9-12 hrs | From €41 | Pairs Segovia with Ávila or Toledo; worth it if you don't want to plan |

Note the train station: Avant trains to Segovia leave from **Chamartín**, not Atocha. Early trains out and late trains back sell out at weekends, so buy a few days ahead.

## Things to do in Segovia, ranked honestly

**The aqueduct.** About 28 metres high at Plaza del Azoguejo, granite blocks with no mortar, and close to 2,000 years old. Don't just look up from the square: climb the steps that start beside the arches to the **Postigo del Consuelo** and see it at arch level, with the town behind. Free, and the best photo of the day.

**Calle Real to the Plaza Mayor.** A gentle uphill street past the **Casa de los Picos**, with its facade of stone spikes, and the Romanesque church of **San Martín**. Nothing to pay for; it's simply the way up.

**The cathedral** (€4 for the cathedral and Bishop's Palace; Monday to Saturday 9:30 am to 6:30 pm, Sundays from 1:30 pm). Bright, huge and less cluttered than most Spanish cathedrals. If you like heights, the **guided tower tour** (€7, at 10:30, 12:00, 13:30, 15:00 and 16:30) or the **cathedral and tower pack** (€10, not on Sundays) gives the best view in town. With one day, pick one tower: the cathedral's or the Alcázar's.

**The Alcázar** (€7 for the palace and museum; €10 with the **Tower of Juan II**). Royal halls, Mudéjar ceilings and the armoury. The tower is 152 steps and the visit is capped at 25 minutes, but the view over the plains is worth it. Tower tickets sell out first at weekends: buy them on the official website.

**The view from below.** What most visitors skip: walk down from the Alcázar to the **Eresma river path** and look up. From there, and from the road to the **Vera Cruz** (a 12-sided church linked to the Knights Templar), the Alcázar looks like the prow of a ship. That's the postcard shot, and you can't get it from inside.

## Where to eat and what to try

**Roast suckling pig** (cochinillo) is why many Madrid locals come back. The classic roast houses are by the aqueduct and around the Plaza Mayor (Mesón de Cándido and José María are the best known); they price by the portion, so check the menu before you sit down. If suckling pig isn't for you, **judiones de La Granja** (big white beans stewed with pork) are just as local. For dessert, **ponche segoviano**: sponge cake with custard and a toasted marzipan top.

## Common mistakes

1. **Forgetting the bus from Guiomar:** 15 extra minutes each way.
2. **Reaching the Alcázar at 5 pm in winter.** From November to March it closes at 6 pm, ticket office at 5:30.
3. **Going at the weekend without booking** lunch or the Tower of Juan II.
4. **Staying in the aqueduct square.** The best views are of the aqueduct from above and the Alcázar from below.
5. **No coat from October to April:** Segovia sits at around 1,000 metres.
6. **Trying to add Toledo on your own the same day.** They are in opposite directions from Madrid.
