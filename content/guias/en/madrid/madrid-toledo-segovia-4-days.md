## Day 1: historic Madrid

- **10:00 — Royal Palace**, at opening time (October to March, Monday to Saturday 10:00-18:00; until 19:00 from April to September; Sundays until 16:00). 1 h 15 inside, with the Almudena and the Sabatini Gardens next door.
- **12:00 — Plaza de la Villa and Plaza Mayor**, 10 minutes along Calle Mayor.
- **14:00 — Lunch in La Latina** (Cava Baja).
- **16:30 — Sol and Gran Vía** to Plaza de España.
- **18:00 — Sunset at the Temple of Debod**, free (in summer, after 21:00).
- **Evening** in Malasaña.

The full plan, with alternatives, is in [Madrid in 2 days](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-in-2-days).

## Day 2: Prado, Retiro and Las Letras

- **10:00 — Prado Museum** (2 h 30). Open Monday to Saturday 10:00-20:00 and Sundays until 19:00; free in the last two hours, but with queues.
- **12:45 — Retiro:** the lake, the Crystal Palace (free) and the Rose Garden.
- **14:30 — Lunch** by the Retiro or in Las Letras.
- **Afternoon:** the Reina Sofía (€12, closed Tuesdays) or the Barrio de las Letras and the Círculo de Bellas Artes rooftop (€5).
- **Evening:** flamenco in the centre or a walk through the city's oldest taverns.

## Day 3: Toledo from Madrid by train

**8:30-9:30 — Avant from Atocha** (33 minutes; hourly departures from 6:50). Toledo station is a 20-minute uphill walk from the old town: take **bus 5** or a taxi (€6-8). Times and fares in [Madrid to Toledo by train](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-to-toledo-train). Without the train, Alsa bus from Plaza Elíptica (1 hour, about €11 return).

**10:00 — Primate Cathedral** (€12, 1 h 30; Monday to Saturday 10:00-18:30, last entry 18:00): Arfe's Monstrance, the Transparente and the sacristy with its El Grecos. Then **Santo Tomé** (*The Burial of the Count of Orgaz*, 30 minutes) and **Santa María la Blanca**. The **tourist wristband** (€14) covers seven monuments at €4 each and pays off from the fourth.

**14:00 — Lunch** on Calle de la Sillería or Plaza de Padilla, not in Zocodover: a €12-18 set menu with carcamusas, partridge or Manchego cheese.

**15:30 — Jewish Quarter and San Juan de los Reyes.** The Synagogue of El Tránsito and Sephardic Museum (€3; closed Mondays), the cloister of San Juan de los Reyes and the **San Martín bridge**, which you should cross and look back from.

**18:00 — Mirador del Valle** at sunset, by taxi from the old town (about €10): the view of Toledo above the Tagus. Last Avant back around 21:30. Hour-by-hour plan in [Toledo day trip from Madrid](/en/guides/madrid/toledo-day-trip-from-madrid).

## Day 4: Segovia

**9:00 — Avant from Chamartín** to **Segovia-Guiomar** (about 30 minutes); from there, **city bus 11** takes you to the aqueduct in 15 minutes. Alternative: Avanza bus from Moncloa (1 h 15, about €5 each way), which arrives a few minutes from the aqueduct.

**10:15 — Roman aqueduct**, standing for almost 2,000 years without mortar. Climb the steps alongside to see it from above.

**11:00 — Calle Real and Cathedral.** The street climbs gently to the Plaza Mayor and the **Cathedral** (€4, 45 minutes), the last great Gothic cathedral in Spain.

**12:30 — Alcázar** (€7; €10 with the Tower of Juan II, which adds 25 minutes and the best view). On Tuesdays from 14:00 to 16:00 the palace is free for EU citizens, at the ticket office and without the tower.

**14:30 — Roast suckling pig**, the reason many Madrileños keep coming back to Segovia. Book at weekends.

**Afternoon:** walk down through the Caballeros quarter or the Jewish quarter to the aqueduct and head back to Madrid without rushing. Segovia sits at about 1,000 metres: bring a coat from October to April. More stops in [Segovia day trip from Madrid](/en/guides/madrid/segovia-day-trip-from-madrid).

## Budget, variations and common mistakes

**Budget per person, excluding accommodation: about €345-540.** Trains and city buses (€60-75), tickets (€80-95), meals (€160-240) and one flamenco show or guided tour (€45-130). Accommodation in Madrid is usually the biggest cost; staying central saves on transport.

**Variations:**

- **With 3 days:** Madrid in one and a combined Segovia and Toledo day tour (from €41), knowing it's about 12 hours.
- **With kids:** Segovia is the easiest (the Alcázar looks like a fairy-tale castle and there are hardly any hills); in Toledo, use the Recaredo escalators and the bus. More in [Madrid with kids](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-with-kids).
- **If it rains:** in Toledo the Cathedral, Santo Tomé and the synagogues are indoors; in Madrid, swap the Retiro for the Thyssen (€14).
- **Toledo or Segovia if only one fits?** We weigh it up in [Toledo or Segovia from Madrid](/en/guides/madrid/toledo-or-segovia-from-madrid).
- **With a fifth day:** El Escorial fits the same logic; see [Madrid in 3 days](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-in-3-days).

**Common mistakes:**

1. **Reaching Toledo at 11:00:** leave Madrid at 8:30-9:30.
2. **Forgetting the Guiomar bus** in Segovia: it's 15 extra minutes each way.
3. **Buying the train on the day** in high season.
4. **Putting Toledo on a Monday** and finding the Sephardic Museum closed.
5. **Climbing Toledo's hills on foot** when you could use the escalators from the Safont car park.
