Four days from a single base in Madrid is the easiest way to see three World Heritage cities without packing and unpacking: **two days for the capital and one for each day trip**, on half-hour trains. The only things to watch are the order of the days, because of weekly closures, and booking the morning trains in time.

## The 4 days at a glance

| Day | City | Morning | Afternoon | Evening |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Madrid | Royal Palace and Plaza Mayor | Gran Vía and the Temple of Debod | Malasaña |
| 2 | Madrid | Prado Museum | Retiro and Barrio de las Letras | Flamenco or taverns |
| 3 | Toledo | Cathedral, Santo Tomé, Santa María la Blanca | Jewish Quarter, San Juan de los Reyes, Mirador del Valle | Back to Madrid |
| 4 | Segovia | Aqueduct, Calle Real and Cathedral | Alcázar and roast suckling pig | Back to Madrid |

**Order of the days:** put Toledo between Tuesday and Saturday (the Sephardic Museum closes on Mondays and the Cathedral only opens to visitors at 14:00 on Sundays), and don't leave the Prado for a Sunday afternoon, when it closes at 19:00.

## Day 1: historic Madrid

**10:00 — Royal Palace**, at opening (October to March, Monday to Saturday 10:00-18:00; until 19:00 April to September; Sundays until 16:00). Allow 1 h 15 inside, then the Almudena Cathedral and the Sabatini Gardens next door. With an official guide just for you and hotel pick-up:

**12:00 — Plaza de la Villa and Plaza Mayor**, 10 minutes along Calle Mayor. **14:00 — Lunch in La Latina** (Cava Baja). **16:30 — Sol and the Gran Vía** to Plaza de España. **18:00 — Sunset at the Temple of Debod**, free (after 21:00 in summer). **Evening** in Malasaña. ## Day 3: Toledo

**8:30-9:30 — Avant from Atocha** (33 minutes; hourly departures from 6:50). Toledo station is 20 minutes' uphill walk from the old town: take bus 5 or a taxi (€6-8). No train? The Alsa coach from Plaza Elíptica takes 1 hour (about €11 return).

**10:00 — Primate Cathedral** (€12, 1 h 30; Monday to Saturday 10:00-18:30, last entry 18:00): Arfe's monstrance, the Transparente and the sacristy hung with El Grecos. Then **Santo Tomé** (*The Burial of the Count of Orgaz*, 30 minutes) and **Santa María la Blanca**. The **tourist wristband** (€14) covers seven sights that cost €4 each and pays off from the fourth:

[actividad: toledo-pulsera-turistica-7-monumentos]

**14:00 — Lunch** on Calle de la Sillería or Plaza de Padilla, not on Zocodover: a €12-18 set menu with carcamusas stew, partridge or Manchego cheese.

**15:30 — Jewish Quarter and San Juan de los Reyes.** The Synagogue of El Tránsito and Sephardic Museum (€3; closed Mondays), the cloister of San Juan de los Reyes and the **San Martín bridge**, which you should cross and look back from.

**18:00 — Mirador del Valle** at sunset, by taxi from the old town (about €10): the classic view of Toledo above the Tagus. The last Avant back leaves around 21:30. Hour-by-hour plan and alternatives in [Toledo day trip from Madrid](/en/guides/madrid/toledo-day-trip-from-madrid).

## Day 4: Segovia

**9:00 — Avant from Chamartín** to **Segovia-Guiomar** (about 30 minutes); from there, city bus 11 drops you at the aqueduct in 15 minutes. Alternatively, the Avanza coach from Moncloa (1 h 15, about €5 each way) stops a few minutes from the aqueduct.

**10:15 — Roman aqueduct**, standing for nearly 2,000 years without mortar. Climb the steps alongside it to see it from above.

**11:00 — Calle Real and the Cathedral.** The street climbs gently to Plaza Mayor and the **Cathedral** (€4, 45 minutes), Spain's last great Gothic cathedral.

**12:30 — Alcázar** (€7; €10 with the Tower of John II, which adds 25 minutes and the best view). On Tuesdays from 14:00 to 16:00 the palace is free for EU citizens, at the ticket office and without the tower.

**14:30 — Roast suckling pig (cochinillo)**, the reason many Madrileños keep coming back. Book at weekends.

**Afternoon:** wander back down through the Caballeros quarter or the old Jewish quarter to the aqueduct and return to Madrid at leisure. Segovia sits at around 1,000 metres: bring a coat from October to April.

## Variations

- **With 3 days:** one day in Madrid and the combined Segovia and Toledo day tour (from €41), bearing in mind it's about 12 hours.
- **With children:** Segovia is the most rewarding (the Alcázar looks like a fairy-tale castle and there are hardly any hills); in Toledo, use the Recaredo escalators and the bus. More in [Madrid with kids](/en/guides/madrid/madrid-with-kids).
- **If it rains:** in Toledo the Cathedral, Santo Tomé and the synagogues are indoors; in Madrid, swap the Retiro for the Thyssen (€14).
- **Toledo or Segovia if only one fits?** We compare them in [Toledo or Segovia from Madrid](/en/guides/madrid/toledo-or-segovia-from-madrid).

## Common mistakes

1. **Arriving in Toledo at 11:00:** leave Madrid between 8:30 and 9:30.
2. **Forgetting the Guiomar bus** in Segovia: 15 extra minutes each way.
3. **Buying train tickets on the day** in high season.
4. **Doing Toledo on a Monday** and finding the Sephardic Museum shut.
5. **Climbing Toledo's hills on foot** when the escalators run free from the Safont car park.
