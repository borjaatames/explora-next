Toledo is just over half an hour from Madrid by AVE train, and that is exactly the problem: the line is so short and popular that **the train tickets sell out on weekends and holidays** long before the monuments do. No train left doesn't mean no Toledo. Several options remain, and it's worth trying them in this order.

## 1. The train ticket: why it sells out and what to do

The AVE and Avant trains from Madrid to Toledo leave from Atocha and take about 33 minutes. It's the most comfortable way to travel, and the first to fill up.

- **Buy on renfe.com as early as possible:** the mid-morning trains out and the mid-afternoon trains back on Saturdays and Sundays go days in advance.
- **Try unpopular times:** a very early departure or a later return often has seats when the middle of the day is gone.
- **If there's no train, the alternatives are the bus** (ALSA from Plaza Elíptica) or a day trip that includes transport. The day trip removes the problem entirely: the transfer is already booked.

## 2. The monument tickets

In Toledo there isn't a single ticket: each monument is run separately.

- **The Primada Cathedral** is the star visit. It's sold at the cathedral itself and at `catedralprimada.es`; on holidays the queues are long, so a guided tour with entry included saves time.
- **The tourist bracelet** covers 7 monuments (Santo Tomé with El Greco's *The Burial of the Count of Orgaz*, San Juan de los Reyes, the Mosque of Cristo de la Luz, Santa María la Blanca…) and does not expire.
- **The Alcázar and the Army Museum** have their own separate ticket.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Leaving the AVE for the last day.** It's the first thing to sell out; buy it as soon as you have a date.
2. **Turning up at the cathedral on a holiday without a ticket.** The queue can eat half your morning; buy online or book a guided tour with entry included.
3. **Thinking one ticket covers everything.** Each monument is separate; to see several, the tourist bracelet goes furthest.
4. **Going there and back the same day with no margin.** Toledo is worth a full day; if you go by train, leave plenty of time for the return.
