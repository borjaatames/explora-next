## The one-day route: what to see in Santiago de Compostela

| Time | What |
| --- | --- |
| 9:30 | Praza do Obradoiro and the cathedral |
| 11:00 | The squares: Quintana, Praterías, Inmaculada |
| 12:00 | Rúa do Franco and Rúa do Vilar |
| 13:00 | Abastos Market |
| 14:00 | Octopus |
| 16:30 | San Martiño Pinario and the Alameda at sunset |

- **Obradoiro and the cathedral.** Pilgrims finish in front of the Baroque façade; the square is framed by the Pazo de Raxoi and the **Hostal dos Reis Católicos**, a former pilgrims' hospital and now a parador. Inside, Master Mateo's **Pórtico de la Gloria**, a restored Romanesque gem, and the Apostle's tomb. The **botafumeiro** only swings on major feast days or when a group pays for it: check the calendar.
- **The squares.** **Quintana** (with the Holy Door), **Praterías** (the horse fountain) and **Inmaculada**.
- **The old town.** Rúa do Franco and Rúa do Vilar, arcaded and full of taverns.
- **Abastos Market.** The second most visited spot after the cathedral: buy Galician produce and in hall 5 they cook it for **€4-6**.
- **San Martiño Pinario and the Alameda.** The monastery's Baroque altarpieces and, to finish, the **best view** of the towers from the Paseo de la Ferradura, with the sunset behind you.

**Pilgrim's Mass:** at 12:00 and 19:30; since 16 October 2026, until further notice, it's held in the church of San Agustín due to works. More in [how to visit the cathedral](/en/guides/santiago-de-compostela/santiago-cathedral-how-to-visit).

## When to go and getting there

- **May, June and September** are ideal: drier, long days and Camino atmosphere. July and August have good weather but crowds; October to April is green, authentic and rainy.
- **24-25 July:** the Festas do Apóstolo, with fireworks over the Obradoiro on the eve. In a **Holy Year** (when 25 July falls on a Sunday) the Holy Door opens and pilgrim numbers soar.
- **It rains about 140 days a year:** always carry an umbrella and wear shoes that don't slip on wet granite. Don't stay in: wet stone is half of Santiago's charm.
- **AVE from Madrid** in about 3 hours (station 15 minutes' walk from the old town, uphill on the way back); trains from A Coruña and Vigo.
- **Lavacolla airport:** bus every 30 minutes (€1, 40 minutes) or taxi at a fixed €22. The old town is pedestrianised.

## Where to stay and eat

Stay **inside the monumental area**: everything's on foot and you get the night-time atmosphere of tunas and taverns once the day-trippers leave. The **Ensanche**, by the Alameda, is practical and modern.

Galician cooking is all about produce: **pulpo á feira**, seafood (goose barnacles, razor clams, scallops), **empanada**, **Padrón peppers**, **lacón con grelos** and **tarta de Santiago**, with **Albariño** or **Ribeiro** and an orujo or queimada as a digestif. Avoid taverns with touts on Rúa do Franco: the market and the back streets of the old town are better value.

## Tips and common mistakes

1. **Don't rush it** or treat it as a stopover between the Rías Baixas and A Coruña: an extra night gives you the atmosphere and a trip to the Rías Baixas or Fisterra.
2. **Book the Pórtico de la Gloria and the rooftops.**
3. **Check the botafumeiro calendar** before counting on it.
4. **Go up to the Alameda** at sunset.
5. **Bring an umbrella** and don't stay in the hotel because it's raining.
