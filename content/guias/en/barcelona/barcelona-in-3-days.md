## Day 1: Sagrada Família, Eixample and Casa Batlló

**9:30 — Sagrada Família.** Right at opening and booked weeks ahead on the official website: **€26 with audio guide**, €36 with towers, €30 guided. The **Hour of Silence** (9:00-10:00) is the least crowded slot. If you're going up to the Carmel or Montjuïc, skip the towers: the stained glass is what justifies the ticket. Shoulders covered. Details in [how to visit Sagrada Familia](/en/guides/barcelona/sagrada-familia-how-to-visit).

**12:00 — Lunch in the Eixample:** a €15-18 menú del día on any street parallel to Provença, away from menus in six languages.

**14:00 — Block of Discord** (Passeig de Gràcia at Aragó): Casa Batlló, Casa Amatller and, two streets up, La Pedrera. If you go inside one, **Casa Batlló** (from €29): the experience is well done. La Pedrera (from €29) is more austere and, for architecture lovers, more interesting.

**20:30 — Dinner** around Aribau and Enric Granados, where locals eat; not on the touristy stretch of Passeig de Gràcia.

## Day 2: Park Güell, Gràcia and sunset at the Carmel

**10:00 — Park Güell.** The monumental zone costs **€18** on the official website (€13.50 for ages 7 to 12 and over-65s; free up to age 6), and you can enter up to 30 minutes after your slot starts; the rest of the park is free. Metro L3 Lesseps and a 15-20-minute uphill walk, or bus H6 or D40. Allow 1 h 30 min inside and another half hour for the **Turó de les Tres Creus**, a free viewpoint at the top. If you're short of time, it's the first thing I'd cut.

**13:00 — Lunch in Gràcia**, 20 minutes downhill. It's where people who live in Barcelona eat: Plaça del Sol, Plaça de la Vila and Plaça del Diamant, and Carrer Verdi and Torrijos, in four-table places, not big terraces.

**19:00 — Bunkers del Carmel** (Turó de la Rovira): Civil War anti-aircraft batteries, free and with the best panorama of the city. Metro L5 El Carmel and a 20-25-minute uphill walk, or bus V17. Allow time to catch the sunset and check the site's closing time beforehand. It's a residential area: no drinking parties or noise. Head down to Gràcia for dinner.

## Day 3: Gothic Quarter, Born, the sea and Montjuïc

**9:30 — Gothic Quarter**, before the crowds. The **Cathedral** (paid tourist visit with cloister, choir and rooftops, price on its website; free for worship first thing), **Plaça del Rei**, genuinely medieval, and **Plaça Sant Felip Neri**, with Civil War shrapnel marks on the façade. The neo-Gothic bridge on Carrer del Bisbe dates from 1928: don't queue for the photo.

**11:30 — Born.** The Mercat del Born (ruins from 1714, free), **Santa Maria del Mar** (the purest Catalan Gothic; free during worship hours, €5 with towers) and, two streets away, the **Picasso Museum** (€14, free on Thursday afternoons, closed Mondays).

**14:00 — Lunch:** Catalan food in the Born or Sant Antoni. Not paella in Barceloneta: pre-cooked rice and frozen seafood at high prices.

**17:00 — Montjuïc**, after a short stroll round Barceloneta. Funicular from Paral·lel (included in the T-casual) or cable car from the port (€20 return).

- **Castle:** €12, with views of the port.
- **MNAC:** €12, free on Saturdays from 15:00; a must if you like Romanesque art.
- **Fundació Miró:** €14.
- **At sunset**, the MNAC esplanade gives the best horizontal view of the city. The **Magic Fountain** runs at night Thursday to Saturday (Friday to Sunday in summer), though it closes for months at a time due to drought or maintenance: check first.

**22:00 — Dinner** in Sant Antoni or the Eixample.

## When to go, getting there and where to stay

| Season | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| May, June, September and the first half of October | Ideal: 20-28 °C, warm sea in September |
| November | Underrated: good light, empty museums |
| March and April | Good, not overcrowded |
| July and August | Avoid: sticky heat and queues |
| Mobile World Congress (late February or March) | Avoid: hotels triple their prices |

**Dates to plan around:** **Sant Jordi** (23 April, books and roses), **Festes de Gràcia** (mid-August, decorated streets) and **La Mercè** (24 September, castellers, free concerts and fireworks).

- **From the airport:** Rodalies R2 Nord to Sants or Passeig de Gràcia (€5, 25 minutes), Aerobús (€7.25) or taxi (€30-35).
- **By AVE:** arriving at Sants; from Madrid, €50-90 booked ahead.
- **By car:** don't; parking costs €25-35 a night and there's a low-emission zone.
- **Getting around:** 10-journey T-casual, **€13 in 2026** (single, €2.90); under-4s free. The metro closes at 24:00 on weekdays, 2:00 on Fridays and runs all night on Saturdays.
- **Neighbourhoods to stay in:** Eixample (well connected, close to everything), Born (historic and pedestrianised), Sant Antoni (market and reasonable prices) or Gràcia (neighbourhood life). **Avoid** La Rambla and Plaça de Catalunya (expensive, noisy, pickpockets), Barceloneta (tension with residents) and the northern Raval at night.

## Tips: what we don't recommend and common mistakes

- **Book the Sagrada Família and Park Güell:** in high season they sell out 60 days ahead.
- **Don't underestimate distances:** from the Sagrada Família to Park Güell is a 45-minute uphill walk. Take the metro.
- **Wear good shoes:** you'll do 12-15 km a day on cobbles and hills.
- **Eat on local time:** lunch is 14:00 to 16:00 and dinner 21:00 to 23:00.
- **Pickpockets** on La Rambla and the metro: backpack on your front and phone out of your back pocket.
- **Cross La Rambla once** (the Miró mosaic, the Boqueria entrance) and that's it. Petritxol or Avinyó are livelier. The **Boqueria**, before 10:00 or after 19:00.
- **Skip** fixed-price tapas tours (commission-paying venues and tiny portions for €50-60), Casa Vicens (€22) on a first visit, "flamenco experiences" (flamenco isn't Catalan) and the Bus Turístic (€30-35 to go slower than the metro).
- **Leave Montserrat** for 4-5-day trips.
