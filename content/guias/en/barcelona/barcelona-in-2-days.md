Two days in Barcelona works if you split it into two halves that don't overlap: **one day for Gaudí**, who lives in the Eixample and the upper city, and **one for the old town and the sea**. That way you avoid crossing the city three times a day, which is where most visitors lose their time.

## The 2 days at a glance

| Slot | Day 1: Gaudí | Day 2: old town and the sea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning | Sagrada Família (9:00) and Passeig de Gràcia | Gothic Quarter and the Born |
| Lunch | Eixample | Born or Barceloneta |
| Afternoon | Gràcia and Park Güell (17:00) | Barceloneta and Montjuïc |
| Evening | Dinner in Gràcia | Tapas in Poble-sec or flamenco in the Born |

## What to book, and how far ahead

| What | Official price | Book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sagrada Família | €26 basic with audio guide; €36 with towers | 1-3 weeks |
| Park Güell (monumental zone) | €18 | 1 week; 30-minute entry slots |
| Casa Batlló or La Pedrera | from €29 | a few days |
| Flamenco | from €30 | 1-2 days |

## Day 1: Gaudí from top to bottom

**9:00 — Sagrada Família** (1 h 30). The first slot, with morning light through the stained glass. Hours: 9:00-18:00 November to February, until 19:00 in March and October and until 20:00 April to September; Saturdays always close at 18:00 and Sundays open at 10:30. No vests, swimwear or shorts above mid-thigh. The towers (€10 extra) add 30 minutes; on a two-day trip, skippable. More in [how to visit the Sagrada Família](/en/guides/barcelona/sagrada-familia-how-to-visit).

[actividad: entrada-sagrada-familia-audioguia]

For three or four people, the private visit with an official guide and tickets included works out well per head, and you can stay inside afterwards:

[actividad: sagrada-familia-visita-privada-guia-entradas]

**11:00 — Passeig de Gràcia.** Metro L2 from Sagrada Família to Passeig de Gràcia (10 minutes) or a 25-minute walk. **Pick one house to go inside**: Casa Batlló (from €29, 1 h 15) or La Pedrera, 500 metres further up (from €29, with the chimney rooftop). See the other from the street.

[actividad: casa-batllo-entrada-con-audioguia]

**13:30 — Lunch in the Eixample.** Not on Passeig de Gràcia itself; two streets either side you'll find more honest set lunches.

**15:30 — Gràcia.** From La Pedrera, 15 minutes' walk up Gran de Gràcia. A former village with its own squares: Plaça del Sol, Plaça de la Vila and the façade of **Casa Vicens**, Gaudí's first house.

**17:00 — Park Güell** (1 h 15). From Gràcia, a 20-25 minute uphill walk or a bus. Monumental zone €18, online only, with a 30-minute entry window: the dragon, the Hypostyle Hall and the serpentine bench. From 25 October to 6 February last entry is 17:30, so don't run late. April to October, if there's still light, carry on to the **Bunkers del Carmel** (20 minutes), which close at 19:30 in summer and 17:30 in winter. Ticket comparison in [which Park Güell ticket to choose](/en/guides/barcelona/park-guell-which-ticket-to-choose).

[actividad: park-guell-entrada-zona-monumental]

**20:30 — Dinner in Gràcia**, then metro L3 from Lesseps (15 minutes' walk from the park) back to the centre.

## Day 2: Gothic Quarter, Born, the sea and Montjuïc

**10:00 — Gothic Quarter.** The **Cathedral** (paid tourist visit with cloister and rooftop; 13 geese live in the cloister), **Plaça del Rei** and **Plaça Sant Felip Neri**, its façade still pockmarked by Civil War shrapnel. If it's your first day and you want context from the start, this private 2-hour walk covers the Gothic Quarter and the Born:

[actividad: barcelona-tour-privado-2-horas-gotico-born]

**12:00 — The Born**, 10 minutes across Via Laietana: **Santa Maria del Mar**, the purest Catalan Gothic, and Carrer de Montcada. The Picasso Museum is here; if you want it, book a timed slot and add 1 h 30.

**14:00 — Lunch in the Born**: Bar del Pla (book) or Bodega La Puntual on Montcada. Not in La Boqueria or on the Ramblas.

**16:00 — Barceloneta**, 15 minutes on foot: the seafront promenade to the beach and Port Vell. A swim in summer; a sunny terrace in winter.

**18:00 — Sunset on Montjuïc.** Metro to Paral·lel and the Montjuïc funicular, included in the metro fare. From the top, the **cable car to the castle** (€19 return) gives the harbour view; for free, the steps of the MNAC look over the whole city.

**21:00 — Evening.** Pintxos on Carrer de Blai in Poble-sec, 15 minutes down from Montjuïc, or flamenco back in the Born in a 17th-century palace:

| Sagrada Família and Park Güell | €44 |
| Casa Batlló or La Pedrera | from €29 |
| Transport (10-journey T-casual) | €13 |
| Cable car or flamenco | €19-30 |
| Meals | €80-120 |
| **Total excluding accommodation** | **about €185-235** |

Barcelona is the priciest city on routes like this: in the first half of 2026 the average hotel night was around €195.

## Variations

- **If it rains:** swap Park Güell for La Pedrera (if you skipped it) and day 2 for the Picasso Museum and the MNAC.
- **With children:** Park Güell, the Montjuïc cable car and the beach all work; so does Gràcia in the afternoon. Ideas in [Barcelona with kids](/en/guides/barcelona/barcelona-with-kids).
- **In summer:** Barceloneta first or last thing, never at midday, and the latest Park Güell slot.

## Where to stay

The **Eixample** (near Passeig de Gràcia) is the most practical base for this plan; the **Born** and the **Gothic Quarter** have more atmosphere but more noise; **Gràcia** is more local and quieter. From Sants station, metro L3 or L5 (€2.90 single).

## Common mistakes

1. **Not booking the Sagrada Família and Park Güell:** you'll be left outside.
2. **Cramming Batlló, Pedrera, Sagrada Família and Park Güell** into one day.
3. **Going back and forth between Park Güell and the centre:** pair Gràcia with the park.
4. **Eating in La Boqueria or on the Ramblas.**
5. **Leaving your phone loose on the metro and the Ramblas:** that's where pickpockets work.

## In a nutshell

Gaudí one day, the Gothic Quarter and the sea the next, with the two big tickets booked before you arrive: that's how two days in Barcelona go a long way.
