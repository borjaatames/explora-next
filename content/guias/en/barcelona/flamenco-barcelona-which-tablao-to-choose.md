Barcelona isn't flamenco's historic home, but it has four established venues with daily shows, each with a distinct angle: from the city's best-selling large theater to a 17th-century baroque palace in El Born. Most artists come from Andalusia and the level is professional at all four.

## Barcelona's 4 flamenco shows

| Show | Price from | Format | Atmosphere | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Teatro City Hall](/en/cities/barcelona/activities/flamenco-show-at-the-theater-city-hall-top-pick) | €25 | Large-capacity theater | Barcelona's best-seller, 4 daily showtimes | Anyone wanting the lowest price and maximum schedule flexibility |
| [Palau Dalmases](/en/cities/barcelona/activities/palau-dalmases-flamenco-show-17th-century-palace) | €30 | 17th-century baroque palace, El Born | The most architecturally striking venue, next to the Picasso Museum | Anyone wanting more historical character |
| [Tablao de Carmen](/en/cities/barcelona/activities/tablao-de-carmen-flamenco-dinner-show-poble-espanyol) | €88 | Tablao with dinner served during the show, Poble Espanyol | The only one serving dinner while the performance happens, as was traditionally done | Anyone wanting the full dinner + show experience |
| [Tablao Cordobés](/en/cities/barcelona/activities/tablao-flamenco-cordobes-dinner-show-las-ramblas) | €48 | Historic tablao (1970) on La Rambla, unamplified show | Intimate format, voice and guitar with no amplification | Anyone wanting real acoustic sound right on La Rambla |

## How to choose in 30 seconds

- **Tight budget or want the most flexible schedule:** Teatro City Hall (€25), with 4 showtimes a day.
- **Looking for the most spectacular venue:** Palau Dalmases, a 17th-century baroque palace in El Born.
- **Want a real dinner during the performance:** Tablao de Carmen, in the Poble Espanyol, with free entry to the site from 4pm included.
- **Care about unamplified live sound:** Tablao Cordobés, with 55 years of history on La Rambla.
- **Going with children under 4:** skip Cordobés, which doesn't admit them. Pick the earliest City Hall or Palau Dalmases show.
- **Using a wheelchair:** City Hall isn't accessible (theater stairs). The other three are.

## What each ticket actually includes

The "from" price is the cheapest option. Here is what you get at each level (official prices as of 2026):

- **Teatro City Hall:** Standard €25 (no drink), Premium €40 (one drink), VIP €55 (two drinks or a bottle of cava or wine). The show lasts one hour. No food is sold with the ticket.
- **Palau Dalmases:** Zone B €30 (back rows, no drink), Zone A €40 (middle rows, one drink), VIP €50 (front rows, one drink). Children pay €15, or €20 in VIP. All tickets include the palace art gallery. Tapas (€18) or a Spanish dinner (€35) can be added. Show of 50–55 minutes. Zone A is the best value.
- **Tablao de Carmen:** from €88 with the three-course Carmen menu and one drink; a tapas menu and a premium Noche Estrella menu are also offered. There's no show-only ticket. Allow 2–3 hours in total.
- **Tablao Cordobés:** show and one drink €48; with tapas €69; with a buffet of 40+ tapas and unlimited drinks €84; with a served restaurant menu €89. Dinner is served before the show, which runs about 70–75 minutes.

All four offer free cancellation up to 24 hours ahead.

## Showtimes and how to get there

| Venue | Showtimes | Where | Nearest metro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teatro City Hall | 17:00, 18:45, 20:30, 22:00 | Next to Plaça de Catalunya | Catalunya (L1/L3) |
| Palau Dalmases | 17:30, 18:45, 20:00, 21:15 | Carrer Montcada 20, El Born, by the Picasso Museum | Jaume I (L4) |
| Tablao de Carmen | 18:00 and 20:30 seatings (shows at 18:45 and 21:15; closed Tuesdays) | Inside Poble Espanyol, Montjuïc | Espanya, then bus 150 or 13 |
| Tablao Cordobés | Usually 16:30 to 22:30, four sessions | La Rambla 33–35 | Liceu (L3) |

Check the exact times for your date when booking. From Plaça d'Espanya to Poble Espanyol it's a steep 15-minute walk uphill, so take bus 150, which stops at the door.

## What to know before booking

- **What you'll see:** a group of 5 to 8 artists (singing, guitar, dance, percussion) with no storyline. Different flamenco styles follow each other and the show closes with bulerías.
- **Arrive 20–30 minutes early.** Seats aren't numbered at City Hall, Palau Dalmases or Cordobés; you sit by arrival or booking order within your zone. At Cordobés the ticket is valid only up to 15 minutes after the booked time, and City Hall may refuse entry after 10 minutes.
- **Dinner or no dinner:** at Tablao de Carmen the meal is part of the show, which is why it costs more. Elsewhere, food is optional and fine rather than memorable. If eating well matters, dine in El Born or Poble Sec and buy a ticket with a drink.
- **Combine with your plans:** Tablao de Carmen suits a Montjuïc afternoon; your ticket gets you into Poble Espanyol from 4pm. Palau Dalmases fits a day in El Born: take the 21:15 show and eat before, or the early show and eat after.
- **Children:** from age 7–8 most kids sit through the hour. Book the first show. Palau Dalmases has flat seating, so ask for a booster at the door.
- **Photos and video** are not allowed during the performance; some venues allow a few seconds at the end.
- **No dress code** at any of the four.
- **Hearing:** Cordobés uses no microphones. Sit close if your hearing isn't great; dinner bookings get the best seats.
