## How to choose the best flamenco show in Barcelona

- **Tight budget or maximum flexibility:** Teatro City Hall (€25), with 4 shows a day.
- **The most spectacular setting:** Palau Dalmases, a 17th-century baroque palace in El Born.
- **A proper dinner during the show:** Tablao de Carmen, in the Poble Espanyol, with free entry to the complex from 4 PM included.
- **Live, unamplified sound:** Tablao Cordobés, with 55 years of history on La Rambla.
- **With children under 4:** rule out the Cordobés, which doesn't admit them. Choose the first show at City Hall or Palau Dalmases.
- **Using a wheelchair:** City Hall is not accessible (theatre stairs). The other three are; at the Cordobés, manual wheelchairs only.

## Flamenco tickets in Barcelona: prices and what's included

Official prices for each tier in 2026:

- **Teatro City Hall:** Standard **€25** (no drink), Premium €40 (one drink), VIP €55 (two drinks or a bottle of cava or wine). The show lasts one hour. No food is sold with the ticket.
- **Palau Dalmases:** Zone B **€30** (back rows, no drink), Zone A €40 (central rows, one drink), VIP €50 (front rows, one drink). Children pay €15, or €20 in VIP. All include the palace's art gallery, and you can add tapas (€18) or a Spanish dinner (€35). Show of 50-55 minutes. Zone A is the best value.
- **Tablao de Carmen:** from **€88** with the three-course Carmen menu and a drink; there's also a tapas menu and a premium menu. There's no show-only ticket. Allow 2-3 hours in total.
- **Tablao Cordobés:** show and one drink **€48**; with tapas €69; with a buffet of 40+ tapas and unlimited drinks €84; with a served menu in the restaurant €89. Dinner comes before the show, which lasts about 70-75 minutes.

All four offer **free cancellation up to 24 hours before**.

## What to know before you go

- **What you'll see:** 5 to 8 artists (singing, guitar, dance and percussion) moving through flamenco styles, with no storyline.
- **Arrive 20-30 minutes early.** At City Hall, Palau Dalmases and the Cordobés seats aren't numbered: you sit in order of arrival or booking within your zone. At the Cordobés your ticket is only valid until 15 minutes after the booked time, and City Hall may refuse entry after 10 minutes.
- **With or without dinner:** at Tablao de Carmen dinner is part of the show, which is why it costs more. Elsewhere food is optional and decent. If you want to eat well, have dinner in El Born or Poble Sec and buy a ticket with a drink.
- **Combine it with your plans:** Tablao de Carmen fits an afternoon on Montjuïc (your ticket gets you into the Poble Espanyol from 4 PM); Palau Dalmases, a day in El Born.
- **Children:** from 7-8 they can manage the hour; book the first show. At Palau Dalmases the seats are all at the same level: ask for a booster at the door.
- **Photos and video:** not allowed during the performance; a few seconds are usually allowed at the end. **Dress code:** none at any of the four.
- **Hearing:** the Cordobés uses no microphones. If your hearing isn't great, sit close; dinner bookings get the best seats.
