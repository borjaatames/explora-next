Park Güell sells timed tickets with a fixed capacity of **700 people every half hour**, and in summer, at Easter and on Spanish bank holidays the best slots go days or weeks ahead. "Sold out" on the official website doesn't mean you miss Gaudí's park: there are still a few routes in, some official and some paid, and it pays to try them in this order.

## 1. Check the official website again (and how)

The official website is `parkguell.barcelona`. General admission costs **€18**; children aged 7 to 12 and over-65s pay **€13.50**, and children up to 6 go free but still need a ticket, as they count towards capacity.

- **Check several days in a row.** Individual tickets can be moved to another date up to the day before, so slots shift.
- **Be flexible with the time.** The first morning and last afternoon slots are the most wanted; midday holds out longer, though it is hotter and there is little shade.
- **In a group, split the booking.** There may be room for two in one slot and two more in the next.
- **Mind the residents' hours.** From 7:00 to 9:30 and from 20:00 to 22:00 the park is for residents and Passi Verd holders only. The old trick of walking in free early no longer works.

Remember that official tickets can't be cancelled: once you have one, there is no refund.

## 2. A resale ticket

Authorised resellers buy allocations in advance, so they may have places when the official site doesn't. They cost more than the official ticket: entry to the monumental zone starts at **€21.50**, and the combined ticket with the **Gaudí House Museum** at **€28**. They can't be cancelled either, so only buy once your day is fixed.

## 3. A tour with entry included

If you'd also like someone to explain the park, guided tours come with a timed ticket already booked. The **Sagrada Familia and Park Güell** tour (from €104, 4 hours) includes both tickets, a guide inside both monuments and transport between them, and it has **free cancellation up to 24 hours before**.

Before paying, check the listing says **"Park Güell entry included"** and that the visit covers the **monumental zone**: some walks only go round the free part.

The park's own official guided visits (from €17) don't help here: they are paid separately and you still need an entry ticket.

## 4. What you can see without a ticket

The monumental zone is a small part of the park. The rest is free and worth the climb:

- The **woodland paths** around the paid zone, with shade and far fewer people.
- The **Turó de les Tres Creus viewpoint**, the highest point, with the best view over Barcelona and the sea.
- The **gatehouses** at the main entrance (the Casa del Guarda and the pavilion opposite), which you can see from Carrer d'Olot without going in.

If what you want is Gaudí indoors, **Casa Batlló** and **La Pedrera** have more availability, and the **Sagrada Familia** has its own fallback options when it sells out: see [what to do when Sagrada Familia tickets are sold out](/en/guides/barcelona/sagrada-familia-tickets-sold-out).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Buying from sites that look like the official one.** They add surcharges, and the park takes no responsibility for tickets from unofficial channels. The official site is `parkguell.barcelona`.
2. **Booking a tour without checking it enters the monumental zone.** Walks round the free area are cheaper for a reason.
3. **Arriving late for your slot.** You can only enter in the **30 minutes** after your time; after that you lose your ticket.
4. **Leaving to "come back later".** Once inside, you can't leave and re-enter.

## In one sentence

If the official site is sold out, check other slots and days, try a resale ticket or a tour with entry included and, failing that, walk up to the free part of the park anyway; everything else is in [which Park Güell ticket to choose](/en/guides/barcelona/park-guell-which-ticket-to-choose).
