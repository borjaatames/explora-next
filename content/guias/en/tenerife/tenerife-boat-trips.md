**The sea is the big draw of southern Tenerife.** Between the south-west coast and La Gomera there are pilot whales and dolphins all year, cliffs hundreds of metres high and coves you can only reach by water. There are lots of trips and they differ a lot: the boat, the length, what's included and the departure port all change. Here's how to choose.

The key: if you only want to **see whales and dolphins**, a short 1.5-2-hour trip is enough. If you want **a day at sea** with a swim, food and drinks, go for a 3-4-hour catamaran or sailing trip.

## Types of boat

- **Yacht or whale-watching boat (1.5-2 h):** the cheapest and quickest. It leaves Los Cristianos or Puerto Colón, heads straight to the pilot whale area and comes back. Ideal if you're short on time or have small children.
- **Catamaran (2-4 h):** wide and stable, with nets to lie on, music and often drinks included. Longer trips add a swim stop and a buffet-style meal.
- **Sailing boat (3 h):** calmer and quieter, for small groups. You sail along the coast with a swim and snorkel in a cove.
- **Speedboat or RIB (2 h):** more adventure and spray. From Los Gigantes, many stop at Masca bay for a swim.
- **Pirate boat or glass-bottom boat:** aimed at families, with entertainment or underwater windows.

## Which port to leave from

- **Puerto Colón (Costa Adeje):** the most departures; walkable from Playa de las Américas and Costa Adeje.
- **Los Cristianos:** lots of whale-watching boats and good bus links across the south.
- **Los Gigantes:** the most spectacular, as you leave below the cliffs and are minutes from the whale area and Masca. The harbour car park is small: arrive early.

## What's usually included

- **Short trips:** life jacket, crew and sometimes water. A swim stop isn't always included.
- **3-4-hour trips:** drinks (soft drinks, beer or sangria), food or snacks, a swim stop and snorkel gear. Check the listing before booking.
- **Hotel pick-up:** only on some trips, and almost always in the south (Costa Adeje, Las Américas, Los Cristianos).

## Tips

- **Book a morning trip** if you get seasick: the wind tends to pick up in the afternoon.
- **Choose an authorised whale-watching operator:** they fly the yellow *Barco Azul* flag and keep their distance from the animals.
- **Bring** swimwear, a towel, sun cream, a hat and a sweatshirt for the way back.
- **With children,** 2-hour trips on boats with a toilet and shade work best.
- **In rough seas** the operator may cancel; you're usually offered another date or a refund.

## When to go

You can go **all year**: the south of Tenerife is sheltered and the sea is around 19 °C in winter and 23-24 °C at the end of summer. In winter some days bring more swell.

If whales are your main interest, see our guide to [whale watching in Tenerife](/en/guides/tenerife/whale-watching-tenerife). For the rest of the trip, [what to see in Tenerife](/en/guides/tenerife/what-to-see-in-tenerife).

## In one sentence

For pilot whales a short trip from Los Cristianos or Puerto Colón is enough; for a day at sea with a swim and food, take a 3-4-hour catamaran or sailing trip, ideally in the morning.
