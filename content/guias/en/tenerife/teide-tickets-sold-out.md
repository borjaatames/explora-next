## Teide cable car sold out: change the time or the day

Tickets are sold at volcanoteide.com and cost **about €42** return per adult. The cable car climbs in under 8 minutes from the base station (2,356 m) to **La Rambleta (3,555 m)**.

- **Check other days.** If your date is full, go up another day of your stay: Teide doesn't need a specific day.
- **Try the afternoon.** Morning slots sell out first; from late October to March the last ascent is at 16:00.
- **Don't count on the ticket office.** It only sells if there is space left, and in high season there almost never is.
- **If you're driving, arrive early:** the cable car car park has about 220 spaces.

## A tour with the cable car included

If there are no single tickets left, tours with the cable car included are the most direct way up. The **tour with cable car and transfer** (7 hours, from €92) includes the ticket, a guide and pickup in the north and south, so you also avoid the parking problem. It has **free cancellation up to 24 hours before**.

Watch out for two things: it **doesn't include the summit permit**, and some park tours, such as the Masca and Garachico one, don't go up in the cable car. If you want to go up, look for "cable car ticket included" on the listing.

## Teide permit sold out: when places are released

The last stretch, from La Rambleta to the summit (3,715 m) on the **Telesforo Bravo** trail, requires a permit. Since August 2025 it is booked on **Tenerife ON** (tenerifeon.es), the Cabildo's platform, not on the National Parks website. It costs **€15** for visitors from outside the Canaries.

- **Places are released every Monday at 7:00** (Canary time) for the **next 56 days**. If you want a specific date, log on that Monday at that time.
- **Check again in the days before:** anyone who cancels at least 3 days ahead gets their money back, so single places come free.
- **One booking every 15 days per person**, with no changes of day or time.
- **If you can't go, cancel:** not using a booking can stop you booking again for three months.

Without a permit you still get the views: from La Rambleta two short trails lead to the **La Fortaleza** and **Pico Viejo** viewpoints, which don't need one.

## The Altavista refuge or a guided ascent

If you can't get a permit, there are two legal ways to reach the summit:

- **Stay at the Altavista refuge (3,260 m).** It reopened on 20 July 2026 after years closed. A night costs **€71** and the booking, at refugioaltavista.com, includes the permits for the Montaña Blanca and Telesforo Bravo trails, with summit access **from 6:00 to 9:00** the next morning, before the cable car arrives. It has 49 beds and you reach it on foot via the Montaña Blanca trail: only for confident mountain walkers.
- **A guided ascent.** Volcano Teide says its guided summit activities include the crater permit. The [guided summit hike](/en/cities/tenerife/activities/mount-teide-summit-guided-hike) we list (from €189, groups of 8) doesn't include the cable car ticket: check with the provider who arranges the permit before booking.

## Teide without going up

If there's no way up, the park is still worth the trip:

- The **Roques de García circuit** (3.6 km, about 2 hours), with Teide behind.
- The viewpoints along the TF-21 road, such as **Minas de San José**.
- The **stars**: sunset tours with a telescope are run from viewpoints in the park and need neither the cable car nor a permit.

## Tips

- **The cable car ticket doesn't include the summit:** without a permit you only reach La Rambleta.
- **Match your permit to the cable car:** the permit is only valid for its slot, even if the cable car isn't running that day.
- **Don't go up in sandals:** the cable car requires closed shoes and it's cold at the top all year.
- **Check whether a tour goes up in the cable car:** some park tours only stop at viewpoints.
- Everything else is in [climbing Mount Teide](/en/guides/tenerife/climbing-mount-teide).
