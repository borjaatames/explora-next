## Tenerife in 5 days: how to split the island

With **five or six days** you'll see the essentials:

1. **Mount Teide** and Las Cañadas.
2. **The north:** La Orotava, Puerto de la Cruz, Icod and Garachico.
3. **La Laguna and Anaga.**
4. **The west:** Masca and Los Gigantes.
5. **The south and the sea:** beach time and a whale-watching trip (one or two days).

With three days, prioritise Teide, the north and a whale-watching trip.

## Mount Teide

**Teide National Park**, a World Heritage Site, surrounds the 3,715 m volcano. Drive across **Las Cañadas**, stop at the **Roques de García** and ride the **cable car** up to 3,555 m (about €42 return). To stand on the summit you need a separate permit, which sells out. At night, Teide is one of the best places in the world for stargazing. We cover it all in our [guide to climbing Mount Teide](/en/guides/tenerife/climbing-mount-teide).

It's cold up there: go with a warm layer and a booked cable car slot.

## What to see in Tenerife, area by area

### The north: La Orotava, Puerto de la Cruz, Icod and Garachico

- **La Orotava:** the island's stately town, with the Casa de los Balcones and views over the valley.
- **Puerto de la Cruz:** seafront promenade, the Lago Martiánez pools and [Loro Parque](/en/guides/tenerife/loro-parque-tickets-prices).
- **Icod de los Vinos:** the **Drago Milenario**, the thousand-year-old dragon tree.
- **Garachico:** partly buried by the 1706 eruption, it keeps its historic centre and the natural lava pools of **El Caletón**.

### La Laguna and Anaga

**San Cristóbal de La Laguna**, a World Heritage Site, is a colonial old town of straight streets with a lively student scene. Behind it begins **Anaga Rural Park**, a Biosphere Reserve: laurel forests, viewpoints such as **Pico del Inglés** and hamlets like **Taganana**, or the wild beach of **Benijo**.

### Masca and Los Gigantes

The road to **Masca** is one of the most spectacular in the Canaries. From the hamlet, the **Masca Gorge** runs down to the sea: since 2021 only with a **free advance booking** and limited numbers; the easiest way is with a guide and a boat back to Los Gigantes (€15-20). The village is open to all, but its car park has 30 spaces and fills up around 10:00. Further south, the **Los Gigantes Cliffs** drop sheer into the Atlantic; they look best from the water.

### The south and the sea

**Costa Adeje, Las Américas and Los Cristianos** are home to beaches, hotels and most boat trips. Pilot whales and dolphins live between Tenerife and La Gomera all year, so sightings are almost certain; see our [whale-watching guide](/en/guides/tenerife/whale-watching-tenerife). For families, [Siam Park](/en/guides/tenerife/siam-park-tickets-prices) is one of the best water parks in Europe.

## Getting around: car, buses and distances

- **Hire a car:** essential for Teide, Masca and Anaga. Book weeks ahead in summer and at Christmas; local companies (Cicar, AutoReisen) have no excess and no surprises. A small car is enough and easier to park in Masca and La Laguna.
- **Distances from Costa Adeje:** to Teide, 1h 45min; to Masca, 50 minutes (20 of them on narrow bends); to La Laguna, 1 hour on the southern motorway; to Garachico, 1h 15min.
- **From the north:** to Teide, 1 hour from Puerto de la Cruz. To Anaga, 40 minutes from La Laguna to Cruz del Carmen.
- **TITSA buses:** from Santa Cruz and La Laguna they cover the north well (lines 101 and 102 to Puerto de la Cruz); to the south, lines 110 and 111 from Santa Cruz to Costa Adeje and Los Cristianos (1 hour, €10). Line **348** goes up to Teide from Puerto de la Cruz once a day (leaving at 9:30 and returning at 16:00). With the **Ten+** card (€2), trips are 30-50% cheaper.
- **Free:** Masca, Garachico, La Laguna and Anaga.

## Where to stay and what to eat

- **Costa Adeje and Los Cristianos:** sun, beach and boats. The south is dry and sunny all year.
- **Puerto de la Cruz and La Orotava:** the green north and its towns, with low cloud on many mornings, but closer to everything.
- **La Laguna or Santa Cruz:** city life and Anaga.

If you're coming for sun and beach, sleep in the south; if you're coming to see the island, the north. With a week, split your nights.

### What to eat

Look for a **guachinche** in the north (simple eateries serving their own wine) and try:

- **Papas arrugadas with mojo.**
- **Conejo en salmorejo** (marinated rabbit).
- **Grilled goat's cheese** with palm honey.
- **Barraquito**, coffee with condensed milk and liqueur.

For wine, those from **Tacoronte-Acentejo** and the Orotava valley.

## Tips

- **Don't go up Teide without a warm layer or a booking:** it's cold at the top and the cable car sells out.
- **Don't hike Masca without a permit:** the gorge requires a booking and hiking boots.
- **Choose boats that don't chase cetaceans:** authorised, respectful operators.
- **Don't stay only in the south:** the north and Anaga are the most authentic Tenerife.
