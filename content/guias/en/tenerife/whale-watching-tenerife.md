## Pilot whales and dolphins in Tenerife: which species you'll see

- **Short-finned pilot whale:** the star. It lives in family groups and is seen on almost every trip.
- **Bottlenose dolphin:** also resident, curious and common.
- **With luck:** Atlantic spotted dolphin, common dolphin, Bryde's whale and, more rarely, sperm whale.

Because pilot whales live here all year, sightings are very frequent, though nobody can guarantee them. Ask when booking whether you get a **free repeat trip** if you see nothing; many serious operators offer one.

## Whale watching boats: Los Cristianos, Puerto Colón and Los Gigantes

The three ports on the south-west coast are a few minutes from the cetacean area:

- **Los Cristianos:** the port with the most departures, reached by bus from all over the south. The **eco-yacht** (an hour and a half) leaves from here; it is designed not to chase the animals and stops for a swim at the Cueva del Amor.
- **Puerto Colón (Costa Adeje):** within walking distance of Playa de las Américas and Costa Adeje. It is the base for the **catamarans**, with plenty of deck space and a swim stop when the sea allows.
- **Los Gigantes:** as well as the cetaceans, you see the 800-metre cliffs from the water, on smaller boats. The car park is small, so allow extra time.
- **Las Galletas:** home to a **private boat** (€450 per boat for 2 hours, €550 for 3 hours, drinks included), which with 4-6 people comes close to a shared trip per head.

## How to choose a responsible boat

Whale watching in the Canaries is regulated by **Decree 178/2000**. What it means for you:

- **Look for the yellow *Barco Azul* flag.** Boats authorised by the Canary Islands government must fly it. No flag, no licence.
- **Distance:** boats may not go closer than **60 metres**, and within 500 metres the speed limit is 4 knots.
- **Time:** no more than **30 minutes** within 100 metres of the animals, and no more than two boats within 100 metres at once.
- **No chasing**, and no swimming within 500 metres: treat any "swim with dolphins" offer as a red flag, because it is illegal.

If your boat breaks these rules, it is breaking the law. Prefer **small or eco-friendly boats**, which are quieter and more stable.

## Trip length and prices

- **Standard trips:** **2-3 hours**, between **€25 and €45** per adult.
- **Long trips with lunch and a swim:** 4-5 hours, between **€50 and €80**.
- **Children:** pay half, and under-3s usually go free.
- **Pregnancy and babies:** conditions vary by boat and operator; speedboats often have restrictions. Ask when booking.

If you are short on time or travelling with children, the short eco-boat trip is enough to see pilot whales. If you want a day at sea, the long catamaran with lunch is the most complete option.

## When to go, seasickness and what to bring

**All year**, because the main species are resident. **Mornings** usually have the calmest sea; winter can bring more swell.

Against **seasickness**: choose a wide-hulled boat or a catamaran and the first departure of the day. Take a tablet an hour before, eat a light breakfast and sit in the middle of the boat looking at the horizon. The area is sheltered, but there is a ground swell.

**What to bring:** sunscreen, a cap, swimwear and a towel (many trips include a swim stop), a light jacket and a seasickness tablet if you need one.

**Getting there:** Los Cristianos and Puerto Colón have pay car parks next to the port. Without a car, walk to Puerto Colón from Las Américas and Costa Adeje, or take the bus to Los Cristianos.

## Other ways to see the sea
