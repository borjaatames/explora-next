**Tenerife is one of the best places in Europe to see whales and dolphins**, and all year round. Between its south-west coast and La Gomera lives a stable population of **short-finned pilot whales** and **bottlenose dolphins**. In 2021 this stretch of sea became the **first Whale Heritage Site in Europe**, a label awarded by the World Cetacean Alliance to places with responsible whale watching. The key is choosing the right boat.

## Which one to choose

- **Short on time or travelling with children:** the **eco-yacht** from Los Cristianos. An hour and a half is enough to see pilot whales, the boat is designed not to chase them, and it stops at the Cueva del Amor for a swim. Toilet on board; drinks are for sale.
- **Staying in the west (Los Gigantes, Puerto de Santiago):** the **Los Gigantes** trip adds the 800-metre cliffs seen from the water, on smaller boats. Transport to the port isn't included.
- **Staying in Costa Adeje or Las Américas:** the **3-hour catamaran** has deck space and a swim stop, conditions permitting.
- **Want a full day at sea:** the **buffet catamaran** (5-6 hours) is the best-rated trip on the island and includes lunch; alcoholic drinks cost extra.
- **Group of 4-6 or want quiet:** the **private boat** from Las Galletas costs €450 for 2 hours or €550 for 3 hours (with a swim or snorkel stop) for the whole boat, drinks included. With 4-6 people it gets close to a shared trip per head.

## Which species you'll see

- **Short-finned pilot whale:** the star. It lives in family groups and is seen on almost every trip.
- **Bottlenose dolphin:** also resident, curious and common.
- **With luck:** Atlantic spotted dolphin, common dolphin, Bryde's whale and, more rarely, sperm whale.

## How to choose a responsible boat

Whale watching in the Canaries is regulated by **Decree 178/2000** of the Canary Islands government. What it means for you:

- **Look for the Barco Azul flag.** Authorised boats must fly it: a yellow flag with "Barco Azul / Blue Boat" in blue and two pilot whales, issued with the licence and renewed every year. No flag, no licence.
- **Distance:** boats may not manoeuvre closer than **60 metres** to the animals, and within 500 metres they must slow to 4 knots or the speed of the slowest animal.
- **Time:** no more than **30 minutes** within 100 metres of the same group, and no more than two boats within 100 metres at once.
- **No chasing, circling, feeding or touching**, and no swimming or diving within 500 metres. Treat any "swim with dolphins" offer as a red flag.

If your skipper breaks these rules, the operator is breaking the law. Prefer **small or eco-friendly boats**, which are quieter and more stable.

## When to go

All year, because the main species are resident. **Mornings** usually have the calmest sea; winter can bring more swell. In summer, a trip with a swim stop is worth the extra hour.

## Seasickness and practical tips

- **Pick a stable hull:** a catamaran or the eco-yacht, and the first departure of the day.
- **Take a tablet an hour before**, eat a light breakfast and sit in the middle of the boat looking at the horizon.
- **Arrive early:** on the eco-yacht, be at the boarding gate in Los Cristianos 20 minutes before departure; late arrivals lose the trip without refund.
- **Getting there:** from Playa de las Américas and Costa Adeje you can walk to Puerto Colón. The Los Gigantes port car park is small, so allow extra time.
- **Ask about repeats:** some operators offer a free second trip if you see nothing.
- **Pregnancy:** speedboats often have restrictions; ask the operator before booking.
- **Cancellation:** the shared trips listed here allow free cancellation up to 24 hours before. The private boat refunds 90% if you cancel 72 hours or more ahead, and 100% if the operator cancels for weather.

## What to bring

Sunscreen, a cap, swimwear and a towel (many trips include a swim stop; the eco-yacht doesn't provide towels), a light jacket and a seasickness tablet if you need one.

## In one sentence

Choose a boat flying the Barco Azul flag that doesn't chase the animals, head out in the morning from the south-west and you'll have every chance of seeing wild pilot whales.
