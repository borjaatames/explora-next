## The Lobos Island permit, step by step

Lobos has required prior authorisation since 2019. You request it at **lobospass.com**, the official website of the Cabildo de Fuerteventura, which stepped up controls and patrols in summer 2026.

1. **Go to lobospass.com** when booking opens for your date. The Cabildo and the Ferry Lobos company say **3 days** in advance; some websites say 5. Check on the site itself a few days before.
2. **Choose your slot:** **morning (10:00–14:00)** or **afternoon (14:00–18:00)**. Each has a quota of **200 people**, under the islet's management plan.
3. **Fill in the details** for up to **three people** per application: first name, surname and ID card or passport. If there are more of you, make several applications.
4. **Save the QR code** on your phone or print it. You'll be asked for it when you land and it can be checked on the island.

The permit is **free, personal and non-transferable**. Don't pay websites offering to "handle it". The Cabildo has announced an access fee in the future, with no amount set yet.

**What about a tour?** Some ferry tickets include park entry and some catamarans arrange it. Always ask when booking and check it appears in your confirmation.

## The Lobos Island ferry from Corralejo

All boats leave from **Corralejo harbour** and the crossing takes **15–20 minutes**. There are several operators (Ferry Lobos, Nortour, Corralejo Charter), plus water taxis and catamarans.

- **Ferry Lobos fares:** **€17** return for adults, **€11** for children aged 4 to 11, free up to 3 (Canary residents €14 and €9), taxes and insurance included.
- **Winter timetable (11 Sep–13 Jun):** out at 9:45, 11:00, 12:30 and 13:30; back at 14:00, 16:15 and 17:15.
- **Summer timetable (13 Jun–10 Sep):** more departures, from 9:45 to 15:30, with returns until 18:15.
- **You choose your return when you board** on the way out, and you're given a return boarding card.

The key is to **match the boat to your slot**: with a morning permit, take the 9:45 and return at 14:00; with an afternoon permit, go out around 13:30 and return on the last boat. Overstaying can get you fined. Timetables change with the sea and the season: confirm them when you buy.

## What to see on Lobos: the three-hour route

- **La Concha beach** (or La Calera): a lagoon of white sand and still water five minutes from the jetty. The best place to swim.
- **El Puertito:** fishermen's huts and rock pools by the jetty.
- **Las Lagunitas and the Marrajo salt pans:** wetlands and old salt flats on the way to the lighthouse.
- **Martiño lighthouse (1865):** at the northern tip, with views of Lanzarote.
- **La Caldera (127 m):** the highest point, a volcano with its crater open to the sea. A short, steep climb with views of the Corralejo Dunes.

**Recommended route:** jetty, El Puertito, Las Lagunitas, Martiño lighthouse, La Caldera and back to La Concha for a swim. It's about **three hours** at an easy pace, with no shade. Don't just stay at La Concha: the lighthouse and La Caldera are the best of the island. It's also the start of the first stage of the **GR 131** on Fuerteventura.

## Eating on Lobos

There is only **one restaurant**, by El Puertito, known for its fish and paella. It fills up: if you want to eat there, put your name down as soon as you arrive. Always bring **water and food** just in case.

## Park rules and tips

- **Ferry and permit are two separate things:** don't buy one and forget the other.
- Walk **only on marked trails**; some sections may be temporarily closed.
- **Forbidden:** camping, flying drones, taking stones, sand or shells, shellfishing and spearfishing. **No pets** on land.
- **Take your rubbish** back to Corralejo.
- **Water and a hat:** there's no shade and the sun is strong even when it's windy.
- **Leave a spare day:** with a heavy swell, boats may cancel or change times.
