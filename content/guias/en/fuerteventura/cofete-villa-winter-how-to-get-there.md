## How to get to Cofete by car: the track and your hire car

The track leaves **Morro Jable** heading west, climbs the massif and crosses to the north side at the **Degollada de Agua Oveja** pass, where the whole beach suddenly appears: this is the **Cofete viewpoint** and the best photo of the trip. It's about **20 km** of dirt; allow **45 minutes to an hour** each way.

- **Do you need a 4x4?** No: when dry, ordinary cars do it slowly. After rain or when the track is in poor condition, a higher car helps.
- **Insurance:** many hire contracts **exclude damage on unpaved roads**, even with fully comprehensive cover. Read yours before setting off; if it isn't covered, any puncture or knock to the underside is on you.
- **Driving:** go slowly, no overtaking on bends, and never leave the track, which is forbidden.
- **Before you go:** full tank, water and food. There are no petrol stations and no mobile signal; download the map offline.
- **Parking:** a dirt clearing next to the beach at the end of the track. It fills up in high season: arrive before 10:00.

## Without a car: the Cofete bus (line 111)

Tiadhe's line **111** links **Morro Jable, Cofete and Punta de Jandía** every day along the track. According to the published timetable:

- **Out from Morro Jable:** 10:00 and 14:15.
- **Back from the Punta Jandía lighthouse:** 12:15 and 16:15, passing Cofete about 30 minutes later (times at intermediate stops are approximate).
- **Fare:** about **€8.70** each way.
- **No booking:** if it's full it may not stop. Get to Morro Jable bus station early and check the current timetable on tiadhe.com.

To spend several hours at Cofete, take the 10:00 and come back in the afternoon. If you take the 14:15, the only way back is the last bus: don't miss it.

## On a tour from Jandía

The easiest option: groups of up to eight, pick-up in Jandía, a stop at the viewpoint, a **35-minute guided visit to Villa Winter** and 35 minutes on the beach, for €57. Note: the guide speaks **German, English or Polish**, not Spanish.

## Villa Winter

It was built by German engineer **Gustav Winter** in the mid-20th century; the exact date is disputed (official sources say 1946 and there are building documents from 1949). Its isolation and its tower have fed decades of legends about German submarines that have never been proven.

Today it is **private property**. The caretaker shows it in exchange for a **donation**: visitors in 2025 and 2026 mention about €5 including the basement. Respect the signs and the times posted on the door.

## What to see at Cofete beach (and why not to swim)

- **The viewpoint** at Degollada de Agua Oveja, with the whole beach at your feet.
- **The beach:** kilometres of sand to walk. To the right, less than a kilometre away, is the Cofete islet.
- **Cofete cemetery,** right by the sand: look at it respectfully from outside.
- **The hamlet,** with a bar-restaurant that isn't always open.
- **Puertito de la Cruz and the Punta Jandía lighthouse,** at the end of the other branch of the track and of line 111.

**Swimming: better not.** Cofete has **big waves, very dangerous currents and no lifeguards**, and people have drowned. Walk, paddle, and swim on the leeward beaches of Jandía instead. Keep out of fenced areas: there has been a loggerhead turtle recovery project since 2006.

## Tips

- **Go early,** before 10:00, for parking and soft light. Midday in August means heat, a full car park and flat light.
- **When:** all year; spring and autumn are the most pleasant. After heavy rain the track may close.
- **Wind:** it blows hard and whips up sand. Sunglasses and a layer.
- **No facilities or shade:** bring water, food, sunscreen and a hat.
- **Forbidden:** camping, flying drones and taking sand, stones or shells.
