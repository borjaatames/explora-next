## Fuerteventura in 5 days: how to split the island

With **five or six days** you can see the whole island without rushing:

- **Two days in the north:** Corralejo, Lobos, El Cotillo and Calderón Hondo.
- **One day in the centre:** Betancuria, Vega de Río Palmas and Ajuy.
- **Two or three in Jandía:** Sotavento, Morro Jable and Cofete.

With **three or four days**, pick one half: Corralejo to Morro Jable is over an hour and a half by car, and a day return is tiring. With **a week**, split your accommodation between north and south.

## The north: Corralejo Dunes, Lobos Island and El Cotillo

The **Corralejo Dunes** are a nature park of more than 2,600 hectares of white sand that reaches the sea at the **Grandes Playas**. The sand is marine in origin (crushed shells), not from the Sahara. The FV-1 crosses them: park in the designated lay-bys and walk into the dunes at sunset.

From Corralejo harbour, **15–20 minutes** by boat, you reach **Lobos Island**: La Concha beach, the Martiño lighthouse and La Caldera mountain. To land you need a **free authorisation** from the Cabildo with a quota per slot, and in high season it goes fast: we explain it step by step in the [guide to getting to Lobos Island](/en/guides/fuerteventura/lobos-island-how-to-get-there).

To the west, **Calderón Hondo**, near Lajares, is climbed on a waymarked loop of about 4.7 km (1.5 hours) to a crater 70 metres deep. **El Cotillo** rounds off the north with the El Tostón castle, the lighthouse and the coves of white sand and calm water north of the village, which form lagoons at low tide.

## The centre: Betancuria and Ajuy

**Betancuria**, founded in the early 15th century, was the island's first capital: the church of Santa María, whitewashed houses and a valley between mountains. Drive up to the **Morro Velosa viewpoint** to see the massif and come down through **Vega de Río Palmas** and the **Las Peñitas** ravine.

On the west coast, the **Ajuy Caves**, a Natural Monument, hold the oldest rocks in the Canaries, ocean-floor sediments more than 100 million years old. A short path leads down to them from Ajuy's black-sand beach.

## The south: Sotavento, Morro Jable and Cofete beach

The **Jandía** peninsula has the longest beaches. **Sotavento**, between Costa Calma and Risco del Paso, forms a turquoise lagoon with the tide and is the big stage for **windsurfing and kitesurfing**. **Morro Jable** has Matorral beach, the lighthouse and the harbour where dolphin-watching boats leave from.

On the other side of the massif lies **Cofete beach**: more than ten kilometres of untouched sand beneath Pico de la Zarza (807 m), with **Villa Winter** on the hillside. You reach it on a dirt track of about 20 km from Morro Jable, and **swimming is dangerous** because of the currents and the lack of lifeguards. Everything about the track, bus 111 and the Villa is in [how to get to Cofete](/en/guides/fuerteventura/cofete-villa-winter-how-to-get-there).

## The best beaches for what you want

- **Calm water and families:** Caleta de Fuste, the coves of El Cotillo, La Concha on Lobos and Matorral beach.
- **Endless sand:** Grandes Playas in Corralejo, Sotavento and Cofete (the latter for walking, not swimming).
- **Kite and windsurf:** Sotavento and the Corralejo area, with plenty of schools.
- **Surf:** the north coast between Corralejo, Lajares and El Cotillo, with beginner schools in the villages.
- **Wild:** Cofete and the west coast (Ajuy, Garcey): look, but don't swim in a heavy swell.

## Getting around, where to stay and what to eat

**Getting around:**

- **Car:** practically essential. Corralejo to Morro Jable is over an hour and a half on the FV-1 and FV-2. If you plan to go to Cofete, check your contract: many hire companies don't cover damage on dirt tracks.
- **Buses:** Tiadhe links Puerto del Rosario with Corralejo, Caleta de Fuste and Morro Jable; there are very few services inland and to Cofete. Timetables at tiadhe.com.
- **Fuel:** fill up before heading into Jandía and onto the Cofete track, where there's nothing.
- **Lanzarote:** from Corralejo, the ferry to Playa Blanca takes about 25–35 minutes. An easy day trip.

**Where to stay:**

- **Corralejo:** the most complete base for the north, with atmosphere, restaurants, the boat to Lobos and the ferry to Lanzarote.
- **El Cotillo:** small and quiet, for surfers and sunset seekers.
- **Caleta de Fuste:** a calm bay near the airport, handy for families and short stays.
- **Costa Calma:** next to Sotavento, with wind almost guaranteed.
- **Morro Jable and Jandía:** long beaches and the base for Cofete and the southern boats.

**What to eat:** **majorero cheese** from goat's milk (with Protected Designation of Origin), **kid goat**, **papas arrugadas with mojo**, fresh fish of the day in Corralejo, El Cotillo or Morro Jable, **gofio** and **ron miel** (honey rum). A good introduction is the [walking tour with tastings in Corralejo](/en/cities/fuerteventura/activities/corralejo-walking-tour-tastings).

**Wind and sun:** the wind blows almost daily and harder in summer; the beaches on the east and in southern Jandía are more sheltered than the west coast. The cool breeze is deceptive: high-factor sunscreen, a hat and water, in winter too.
