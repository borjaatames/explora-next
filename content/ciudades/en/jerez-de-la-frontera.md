Jerez de la Frontera is the largest city in the province of Cádiz —about 213,000 people— and the birthplace of three Andalusian icons: **sherry**, the **Andalusian horse** and much of **flamenco**.

Its trademark is the **bodegas**: towering halls of stacked casks where the wine ages through the criaderas y solera system. Most open to visitors with a guided tour and tasting, from the big houses to family bodegas right in the centre. In the evening, **tabancos** pour wine straight from the cask, some nights with live singing.

The **Royal Andalusian School of Equestrian Art** stages its show "How the Andalusian Horses Dance" and offers visits to the training sessions. The old town holds the Almohad **Alcázar**, the **Cathedral** and the flamenco quarters of **Santiago** and **San Miguel**.

Jerez is half an hour from **Cádiz** and **Sanlúcar de Barrameda**, and an hour from Seville by train. **One or two days** are enough. In May it holds the **Horse Fair** and in September the **Harvest Festival**.
