Gran Canaria is the third-largest of the Canary Islands and, with Tenerife, the most populated, with about **860,000 inhabitants**, almost half of them in the capital, **Las Palmas de Gran Canaria**. It's called a **miniature continent** because this round island, barely 50 km across, packs in desert, pine forests, ravines and summits of almost 2,000 metres. Around half of it is a UNESCO **Biosphere Reserve**.

The south has the famous **Maspalomas Dunes**, the beaches of Playa del Inglés and Meloneras, and picturesque **Puerto de Mogán**. The interior is another world: **Roque Nublo**, Pico de las Nieves (1,949 m) and ravines such as **Fataga** and **Guayadeque**, with cave houses still lived in today. In the north are the traditional towns of **Teror**, **Arucas** and **Agaete**, among banana plantations and coffee farms.

**Las Palmas** deserves at least a day: the colonial old town of **Vegueta**, Santa Ana Cathedral, the Casa de Colón and the city beach of **Las Canteras**, one of Europe's best.

With **four or five days** you can combine beach, interior and capital. The practical choice is to stay in the south for sun or in Las Palmas for city life, and hire a car or book tours for the interior. The climate is mild **all year round** (18-28 °C on the coast), but it gets much cooler on the summits, especially at night.
