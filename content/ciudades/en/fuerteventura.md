Fuerteventura is the second-largest of the Canary Islands and the closest to Africa, less than 100 km from the Moroccan coast. It has about **129,000 inhabitants**, more than a third of them in the capital, **Puerto del Rosario**, and since 2009 the whole island and its surrounding sea have been a UNESCO **Biosphere Reserve**. This is the beach island: more than 70 km of sand, with turquoise water on the east coast and Atlantic surf on the west.

The **north** revolves around **Corralejo**: the **Corralejo Dunes**, the boat to **Lobos Island**, the **Calderón Hondo** volcano and the surf village of **El Cotillo**, with its coves and lighthouse. In the **centre** the landscape turns to bare mountains and palm groves: **Betancuria**, the first capital, the **Morro Velosa** and **Las Peñitas** viewpoints, and the **Ajuy caves**, with the oldest rocks in the archipelago.

The **south** is the **Jandía** peninsula: the beaches of **Sotavento** and **Costa Calma**, a world-class windsurfing and kitesurfing spot, **Morro Jable**, where the dolphin boats leave from, and, on the other side of the massif, wild **Cofete** beach with **Villa Winter** on the hillside. Here too is **Pico de la Zarza** (807 m), the island's highest point.

With **five or six days** you can combine the north, the centre and Jandía. The practical choice is to **hire a car**: buses link the main towns, but services to the south and the interior are infrequent. It's **sunny almost all year** (average highs of 21 °C in January and 28 °C in August at the airport) and it rarely rains; what never lets up is the **wind**, which takes the edge off the heat but can be relentless on exposed beaches.
