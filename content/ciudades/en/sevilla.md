Seville is the Andalusian city by definition. It packs the best of southern Spain into a few square kilometers: a Gothic cathedral with the Giralda built into it, a Mudéjar Alcázar that's among Europe's most beautiful, a river that splits the city into two distinct identities, and the best sunset light in the western world. Visiting Seville **is something to be understood, not enumerated**.

In return, two honest warnings: **summer heat is brutal** and the most touristy zone is saturated in spring. Well planned, Seville is one of Spain's most rewarding trips. Badly planned (a July noon walking through Santa Cruz), it's a nightmare.

## When to visit Seville

**The best months**: **March, April, May, October and November**. Temperatures of 18–28°C / 64–82°F, long days (especially April–May), orange trees in bloom (mostly March–April). The smell of azahar — orange blossom — is one of the strongest sensory memories visitors take home.

**When to avoid (with caveats)**:

- **June, July, August**: 38–45°C / 100–113°F at midday. The city still works, but you have to surrender to the Sevillano schedule: up early, retreat for siesta at 2 PM, back out at 7 PM. If you can only travel in these months, **adapt or suffer**.
- **Holy Week and Feria de Abril**: unique experiences, but hotels book out a year in advance and prices skyrocket. Only come during these if the festival itself is the point of your trip.

**Underrated month**: **November**. Still pleasant (15–22°C / 59–72°F), terraces working, beautiful autumn light, low hotel prices, almost no tourists.

## How many days you'll really need

- **2 days**: the decent minimum. Cathedral + Alcázar + Plaza de España + Triana. Tight, but you take Seville home.
- **3 days**: the sweet spot for most travelers. Adds less-touristy neighborhoods, a visit to Itálica, or one calmer day.
- **4–5 days**: ideal if you're combining with a side trip to **Córdoba, Cádiz or Jerez**.
- **More than 5**: only if you're using Seville as a base for all of Andalusia (Granada, Ronda, white villages).

## Getting around

### On foot
**Seville is the most walkable city in Andalusia**. The historic center (Cathedral, Alcázar, Santa Cruz, Plaza de España, Arenal) fits in a square of **1.5 km / 0.9 mi per side**. With a well-located hotel, you don't need transport on day one.

### Tram and bus
- **Tram (MetroCentro)** runs from Plaza Nueva to Prado de San Sebastián. Useful for skipping a 15-minute walk in the heat.
- **City bus** reaches almost everywhere. Single ticket: €1.40. Multi-trip card: €7 for 10 trips.

### Metro
Only one line, doesn't reach any tourist site (it's for the outskirts). **Skip it** unless your hotel is in a very specific area.

### Bicycle
Seville has **a very developed bike-lane network** and totally flat terrain. **Sevici** is the public rental system. Good way to cover the city without battling the heat.

### Horse-drawn carriage
The classic tourist postcard. **Pricey** (€45–50 per carriage, not per person), and the animals suffer in extreme summer heat. If you do it, avoid the hottest months.

### What I don't recommend
**Driving in central Seville** is complicated: pedestrian zones, narrow medieval streets, hard parking. Only if you're leaving the city.

## Where to stay: yes neighborhoods, no neighborhoods

### Recommended neighborhoods

**Historic center (between the Cathedral and Plaza Nueva)** — the best central option
Pros: walking distance to everything. Cons: night noise, more expensive hotels. If you're staying 2–3 days and you're flexible about noise, perfect.

**Santa Cruz** — the postcard Jewish Quarter
Narrow streets, white houses, total charm. But **very touristy** and sometimes loud. Excellent for a first visit if you want to soak up "typical Seville".

**Arenal** — alternative to the center with better value
Right next to the cathedral but with less tourism, near the bullring. A good balance.

**Triana** — to live the authentic Seville
Across the Guadalquivir river. Flamenco bars, the Triana market, ceramic workshops, pure Sevillano atmosphere. Ten to fifteen minutes' walk from the center. **My recommendation if you've been to Seville before** or if you want some distance from the tourist zone.

**Alameda de Hércules** — the modern, nightlife-driven zone
Plaza with terraces, young vibe, alternative restaurants. Good for ages 25–40 looking for nightlife without center prices.

### Neighborhoods to avoid (for staying)

**Polígono Sur, Tres Mil Viviendas**: residential areas with social problems. **Far from the center and not recommended**.

**Lower Macarena**: the upper part is interesting (basilica, market), but the lower zone near the old station has problematic areas at night. If in doubt, choose the center.

**Airport / Tablada**: only if your flight is at 6 AM.

## Eating in Seville

### Real meal times
- **Lunch**: 2:00–4:00 PM (Seville eats late, even by Spanish standards).
- **Dinner**: 9:00–11:30 PM.

In summer, authentic places **simply don't open** between 4 PM and 8 PM. If you want to eat at 5 PM, your only options are tourist spots.

### Areas with good dining

- **Triana (Calle Betis and Triana market)**: authentic tapas with river views. Don't miss it.
- **Alameda de Hércules**: modern, creative cooking, bars with personality.
- **Calle Mateos Gago** (near the Cathedral): **TRAP** — tourist quality. Avoid except for known exceptions.
- **Calle Pureza, Pagés del Corro (Triana)**: honest taverns.
- **Upper Macarena**: market and authentic bars, far from the tourist zone.

### Tourist traps to avoid

- **"Mixed tapas + sangria + paella" tourist menus**: none of those three dishes will be good. Tapas are individual, not pre-fabricated platters.
- **Sangria on any terrace next to the Cathedral**: same as Barcelona — bad quality, inflated price.
- **"Dinner with flamenco show" in tourist areas**: weak show, worse food. If you want flamenco, go somewhere built around flamenco (not built around dinner).
- **Pestiños and pastries near the Alcázar**: tourist prices. Buy sweets at neighborhood pastry shops instead.

## Common tourist mistakes in Seville

**1. Visiting the Alcázar at midday without an online ticket**: brutal queue, extreme heat. **Buy your ticket online** several days in advance, **9:30 AM slot** (opening) or last afternoon slot. Without advance booking in high season you'll be turned away.

**2. Rushing the Cathedral and the Giralda**: the Giralda deserves a slow climb (it's a ramp, not stairs), and the views from the top are spectacular. Budget 1.5–2 hours for the full Cathedral, not 30 minutes.

**3. Plaza de España at midday**: in summer, the sun is direct and there's no shade. **Go first thing in the morning or at sunset** — the golden-hour light on the tiles is extraordinary.

**4. Trying to eat lunch at 1 PM**: many authentic restaurants don't open until 1:30–2 PM. If you arrive earlier, you're stuck with tourist spots or waiting.

**5. Looking for flamenco anywhere**: there's good flamenco and there's tourist flamenco. **La Casa del Flamenco**, **Casa de la Memoria** and **Tablao Lo Nuestro** are usually honest options. Restaurant flamenco shows with dinner included are almost always weak.

**6. Crossing the river and forgetting Triana**: the other side is **half of Seville**. If you stay only in the historic center, you take home the postcard version. Triana is the real city.

**7. Underestimating summer heat**: 42°C in August is not 35°C in Madrid. Bring a hat, water, and **surrender to the schedule**: activities 9 AM to 1 PM, siesta or air-conditioned museums from 2 PM to 7 PM, back out at 8 PM.

## Events worth timing your trip around (if you can)

- **Holy Week (Semana Santa)**: one of the world's most famous. Processions from Palm Sunday to Easter Sunday. Unique atmosphere but the city is **completely saturated**.
- **Feria de Abril**: two weeks after Holy Week. Casetas (tents), sevillanas dancing, horses. You really need a local host inviting you into a private caseta to live it properly.
- **Bienal de Flamenco (September, even years)**: the world's reference flamenco festival.
- **Velá de Triana (July)**: Triana's festival, local atmosphere along Calle Betis.

## In one sentence

Seville isn't visited: it's walked. Take it at your own pace and respect its schedule, and it'll keep you forever. Try to do it in a day as a checklist and you'll only take home heat and queues — and miss the best Andalusian city.
