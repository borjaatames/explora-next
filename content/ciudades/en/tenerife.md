Tenerife is the largest and most populated of the Canary Islands, with about **950,000 inhabitants**, and home to the archipelago's joint capital, **Santa Cruz de Tenerife**. Everything revolves around **Teide** (3,715 m), Spain's highest peak, which dominates **Teide National Park**, a World Heritage Site.

The island has two faces. The dry, sunny **south** has the beaches and resorts of Costa Adeje, Playa de las Américas and Los Cristianos, where boats leave to watch the **pilot whales and dolphins** that live here year-round. The greener **north** holds the most historic towns: **La Orotava**, **Puerto de la Cruz**, **Garachico** and **Icod de los Vinos**, with its Drago Milenario.

In the north-east is **San Cristóbal de La Laguna**, the former capital and a World Heritage Site, and beyond it the **Anaga** massif, with laurel forests and wild beaches. To the west, the **Teno** massif hides **Masca** and ends at the **Los Gigantes Cliffs**.

With **five or six days** you can combine beach, Teide, the north and Anaga. The practical choice is to stay in the south for sun or the north for nature and towns, and hire a car or book tours. The coast is mild **all year round**, but Teide is cold and can see snow in winter.
