Lanzarote is the easternmost of the Canary Islands and the one most shaped by volcanoes. It has about **167,000 inhabitants**, measures some 55 km from north to south and its highest point, **Peñas del Chache**, only reaches 671 metres. Between **1730 and 1736** an eruption buried almost a quarter of the island in lava: that is where **Timanfaya National Park** comes from, along with much of what makes the island unique today. Since **1993** the whole island has been a **Biosphere Reserve**, and since 2015 it has formed a **UNESCO Global Geopark** together with the Chinijo archipelago.

The other half of the story is **César Manrique**. The Lanzarote-born artist persuaded the island to build low and white, and designed its most visited places: **Jameos del Agua**, the **Mirador del Río**, the **Cactus Garden** and his own house set in lava bubbles, now the **César Manrique Foundation**. Most of these centres are run by the island council, and for the Fire Mountains and Cueva de los Verdes tickets are sold online only, for a set date and time.

The north has **Haría**, the valley of a thousand palms, **Famara** beach and the port of **Órzola**, where the ferry leaves for **La Graciosa**. The centre has **Teguise**, the old capital, and the vineyards of **La Geria**. The south has **Playa Blanca**, the **Papagayo** coves and **El Golfo**.

**Four or five days** cover the essentials; with a week you can add La Graciosa and some beach time. Hiring a car is the practical choice: distances are short, but public transport doesn't reach Timanfaya. The climate is dry all year, with average highs of 21 °C in January and 29 °C in August at the airport, according to Spain's weather agency AEMET, and the trade wind blows hard, especially in summer.
