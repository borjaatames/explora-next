## What you'll see

- **Lobos Island**, a volcanic islet of about 4.7 km² protected as a natural park.
- **La Concha beach**, a lagoon of white sand and calm water a few minutes from the jetty.
- **El Puertito**, with its fishermen's huts and rock pools by the sea.
- **Martiño lighthouse** at the northern tip and **La Caldera** mountain (127 m), with views of Corralejo and Lanzarote.

## How it works

This is a ferry ticket, not a guided tour: you go to **Corralejo harbour**, board the Corralejo Charter boat and in about **20 minutes** you're at the Lobos jetty. Before boarding you choose your return from **12:15, 13:00, 14:30, 16:00 and 17:00**. On the island you get around on foot along the marked trails; once back, you have the afternoon for Corralejo's old town.

The ticket includes **natural park entry**. Access to Lobos is limited by time slots (morning 10:00-14:00 and afternoon 14:00-18:00), so check that the authorisation appears in your name in the confirmation and, if in doubt, request it free of charge at lobospass.com. We explain it all in our [guide to getting to Lobos Island](/en/guides/fuerteventura/lobos-island-how-to-get-there).

## Practical tips

- **No shade:** bring a hat, sunscreen and at least a litre of water per person.
- **Eating:** there's only one restaurant, near El Puertito. Pack sandwiches just in case.
- **Walking:** La Concha is five minutes from the jetty; the full loop with the lighthouse and La Caldera takes about three hours at an easy pace.
- **Sea:** if there's a swell, times may change.

## Who I'd recommend it to

- Anyone who wants to **walk and swim at their own pace** without paying for a catamaran.
- Travellers on a **tight budget**.
- Hikers: Lobos is the start of the first Fuerteventura stage of the GR 131.

## Who I would NOT recommend it to

- People with **reduced mobility**: the paths are dirt and stone.
- Anyone who wants **everything sorted** (food, drinks, snorkelling): the catamaran is a better fit.
