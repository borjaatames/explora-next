## What you'll see

This boat trip takes you along the **wild north-west coast of Mallorca** from the picturesque harbour of Sóller to the remote cove of **Sa Calobra** — one of the most spectacular and least accessible spots on the island.

### The coastal journey
The Serra de Tramuntana mountains drop almost vertically into the sea along this stretch of coastline. From the boat you'll see towering limestone cliffs, sea caves, and wild landscapes that are inaccessible by road.

### Sa Calobra cove
Sa Calobra is Mallorca's most dramatic cove: a narrow opening in the cliffs where the **Torrent de Pareis** river meets the sea. The cove is backed by walls of pale limestone hundreds of metres high.

### The Torrent de Pareis gorge walk
From the cove, a path leads through a **narrow limestone gorge** — sometimes only a few metres wide — that cuts through the mountain. The walk takes about 20–30 minutes and involves scrambling over rocks. Parts may have standing water from the seasonal river.

## Who it's for

- Anyone visiting Mallorca who wants to see the island's dramatic wild coast.
- Hikers and nature lovers who enjoy a short walk in spectacular scenery.

## Who it's not for

- Visitors with mobility difficulties: the gorge walk is physically demanding.
- People with severe seasickness: the northern coast can have rough seas.
