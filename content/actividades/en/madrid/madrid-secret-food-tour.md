## What you'll see

Secret Food Tours operates with a deliberate format: standard local food, plus one "secret dish" the operator never reveals before the tour. The format is an international one — they run tours in over 100 cities — but the Madrid edition leans into the city's specialities: tortilla, patatas bravas, padrón peppers, and a hidden finale that the guide saves for last.

- **A complete tapas circuit, not a sample.** The "What's Included" list of dishes — marinated olives, crispy squid sandwich, tortilla, padrón peppers, patatas bravas, plus seven more in the operator's menu — covers the canonical Madrid tapas you should try at least once.
- **A 'secret dish' finale.** This is the gimmick that makes the tour memorable. Past reviews don't spoil it (one of the few format details where travellers respect the operator). What we'll say: it's a Madrid speciality you wouldn't typically order yourself.
- **Choose your length.** 2-hour express or 3-hour deep dive. Same operator, same quality, different depth. Most travellers take the 3-hour for first-time Madrid visits.

## How it works

You book the meeting point during checkout — it's not fixed at one location. Check your confirmation email for the exact spot and arrive 10 minutes early. The tour ends at Calle Mayor 10 in Centro, about 200 metres from where you started.

The 3-hour version covers more stops at a more contemplative pace; the 2-hour is a faster trim. The guide's job in both is to walk you through Madrid's food and culinary traditions, not just put plates in front of you. **Wine, vermouth, and non-alcoholic options are all available** — the operator explicitly notes this on Viator, which is useful if you're traveling with someone who doesn't drink.

The tour walks the historic centre between Sol and Plaza Mayor — well-trafficked but not generic. Carry comfortable shoes; it's not a long walk total, but the cobblestones add up over three hours.

The "secret dish" arrives toward the end. Don't ask the guide to spoil it — past reviews are consistent that the reveal is part of the value.

## Who I'd recommend it to

- If you want the highest-rated food tour in Madrid at a reasonable price (€64.80 for the 3-hour version).
- If you're a repeat food-tour traveller. Secret Food Tours' international consistency means you know what you're getting.

## Who I would NOT recommend it to

- If you need a confirmed, fixed meeting point before booking. The variable meeting point is a small UX friction.
- If you specifically want a small-group tour with a 12-person cap. Secret Food's group size isn't disclosed in the same way.
- If you booked a Madrid trip last week and need a tour for tomorrow. Capacity in peak season is gone weeks out.
- If you want a tour deeply rooted in one neighbourhood (e.g. La Latina). This one covers the historic centre more broadly.
