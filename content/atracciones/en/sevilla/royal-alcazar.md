The Royal Alcázar of Seville is a group of palaces and gardens that grew over more than a thousand years beside the Cathedral. It began as a fortress for the Umayyad governors in the 10th century; the Almohads enlarged it in the 12th, and the Courtyard of the Plaster survives from that period. After the Castilian conquest of 1248, Alfonso X built a Gothic palace, and in 1364 Peter I commissioned the Mudéjar palace that is the heart of the visit.

Craftsmen from Seville, Toledo and Granada worked on the Palace of Peter I, where Arabic and Gothic inscriptions sit side by side with carved plaster, tilework and coffered ceilings. Its highlights are the Courtyard of the Maidens, with its sunken garden, and the Hall of the Ambassadors, under a gilded dome, where Charles V married Isabella of Portugal in 1526. The Baths of María de Padilla and the gardens, with the Grotesque Gallery, round off the visit.

It is the oldest royal palace still in use in Europe: the Royal Family keeps its rooms in the Upper Royal Quarters. Since 1987 it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the Cathedral and the Archive of the Indies.
