The Cathedral of Santa María de la Sede stands on the site of Seville's great Almohad mosque, which was used as a church after the Castilian conquest of 1248. In the early 15th century the cathedral chapter decided to pull it down and build a church so large that, as tradition has it, those who saw it finished would think them mad. Work ended in 1506, and the result is the largest Gothic cathedral in the world.

Two parts of the mosque survive: the Court of the Orange Trees, once the ablutions courtyard, and the Giralda, the minaret completed in 1198. In 1568 Hernán Ruiz added the Renaissance belfry, topped by the Giraldillo weathervane. Inside the tower you climb ramps rather than stairs, built so the top could be reached on horseback.

Inside, the highlights are the main altarpiece, one of the largest in Christendom, the carved choir stalls, the Royal Chapel and the tomb of Christopher Columbus, his coffin carried by four heralds representing the kingdoms of Castile, León, Aragon and Navarre. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987, together with the Royal Alcázar and the Archive of the Indies.
