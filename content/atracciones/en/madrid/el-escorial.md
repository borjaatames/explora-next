Philip II had the Royal Monastery of San Lorenzo de El Escorial built at the foot of the Guadarrama mountains to commemorate the victory at St Quentin (1557), won on St Lawrence's day. Work began in 1563 under Juan Bautista de Toledo and, after his death, was completed in 1584 by Juan de Herrera, whose austere, geometric style has carried his name ever since: Herrerian.

The granite building, laid out on a rectangular plan, brings together a Hieronymite monastery, a royal palace, a basilica, a pantheon and a library under one roof. Most Spanish kings since Charles I lie in the Royal Pantheon beneath the high altar. The Royal Library, with its frescoed vault, holds thousands of manuscripts and early printed books.

The Basilica, built on a central plan and crowned by a large dome, presides over the whole complex. Philip II ruled for much of his reign from here and died in the palace in 1598. The Monastery and the Royal Site have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1984.
