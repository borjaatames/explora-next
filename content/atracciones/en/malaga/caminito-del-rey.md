The Caminito del Rey is a walkway pinned to the walls of the Gaitanes Gorge, the canyon cut by the Guadalhorce river between Ardales, Álora and Antequera, in inland Málaga province. It was built between 1901 and 1905 so that workers from the Sociedad Hidroeléctrica del Chorro could get from one hydroelectric plant to the other and keep an eye on the canal running along the canyon.

It owes its name to King Alfonso XIII, who walked it in 1921 when he came to open the Conde del Guadalhorce dam. Over the decades the old concrete path crumbled and became a dangerous route used by climbers, until it was closed after several fatal accidents.

Málaga's provincial council restored it with a new wooden walkway laid above the old one, which can still be seen below, and reopened it in 2015. Today it is a one-way trail of about 7.7 km, with some 3 km of walkways more than 100 metres above the river, walked in a single direction and with a helmet.
