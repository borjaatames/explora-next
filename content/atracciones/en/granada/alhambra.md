The Alhambra is a palace city built on the Sabika hill, facing the Albaicín with Sierra Nevada behind it. Its name comes from the Arabic *al-Hamra*, "the red one", after the colour of its walls. Muhammad I, founder of the Nasrid dynasty, began it in the 13th century, and his successors turned it during the 14th into the residence of the last sultans of al-Andalus.

The complex brings together four areas: the Alcazaba, the military quarter and the oldest part; the Nasrid Palaces, with the Mexuar, the Comares Palace and the Court of the Lions; the Generalife, the summer estate with its gardens and water channels; and the Renaissance Palace of Charles V, built after the Catholic Monarchs took Granada in 1492.

Carved plaster, tilework, muqarnas and the play of water make the Nasrid Palaces the high point of Andalusi art. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1984 and is one of the most visited monuments in Spain, which is why tickets sell out weeks in advance.
